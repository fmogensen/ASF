## C — must change

1. **`asf/workers/caches.py:107` — an entry's `bytes` is the *max* of its sibling files, not their sum, contradicting the module's own docstring and the plan.**
   `entries()` does `group['bytes'] = max(group['bytes'], st.st_size)` while grouping an archive
   with its `-meta.json` sidecar. The module's own docstring, four lines up
   (`asf/workers/caches.py:15`), says "its size the sum of their sizes," and the plan's Step 7
   says the same: "an `Entry`'s `bytes` the sum of its files' sizes." The spec's C5 is explicit
   about *why* it must be a sum — grouping exists so pruning removes the archive and its sidecar
   together, "because the sidecar is bytes the archive's removal does not free" — i.e. the
   sidecar's bytes have to be counted, not discarded in favor of whichever file is bigger.
   Concretely: `entries()`'s `total` (the sum of every entry's `bytes`) undercounts the
   directory's real disk usage by the size of every sidecar, so `plan()`'s cap comparison
   (`total - doomed_bytes > cap_bytes`) and `prune_dir`'s reported `bytes`/`pruned_bytes` all
   understate what is actually on disk — the pass can print `nothing to prune (…, 10.0 GB cap)`
   or `pruned N (X GB)` while the directory, measured with `du`, is still over the configured cap
   by exactly the sum of every sidecar it has. No test in this branch catches it because
   `CacheFixture.entry()`'s sidecar is a few dozen bytes of JSON beside an MB-scale archive, and
   every assertion that touches a byte count is either an inequality (`assertGreaterEqual`) or a
   string built from a one-decimal GB rounding, neither of which the sidecar's few bytes can move.
   Fix: replace line 107 with `group['bytes'] += st.st_size`, and remove or rewrite the comment at
   lines 105-106 (it currently argues *for* the behavior the docstring one paragraph above says is
   wrong).
