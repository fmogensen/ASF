# Plan F-0131 — four Tasks, three waves: the limit, the cause, the rows, the red

Epic: E-0002 — ASF 0.2 — self-improvement. Spec: `docs/specs/f-0131.md` (approved). Branch:
`plan/F-0131`, cut from `origin/main` at `95670d84d`.

**Four Tasks, three waves, and one strict spine.** The spec is one fact in two halves, and the
halves are not independent: nothing can say *it hit its limit* until something knows the limit, and
nothing can count a timeout as red until a `cause` exists on the row. So the split follows the data,
not the file list:

1. **Task 1 — §2, a job's limit, out of the workflow file.** `ci_pool.DEFAULT_JOB_TIMEOUT_MIN`,
   `parse_timeouts`, `Backend.timeouts()`, `GitHubBackend.timeouts()` and the `_workflow_texts()`
   walk factored out of `runs_on`. `after: none` — it reads nothing this card writes.
2. **Task 2 — §1 and §3, the cause on the row.** The new `asf/ci_jobs.py` (the three classes, `RED`,
   `TIMEOUT_SLACK_MIN`, `cancel_cause`), `JOB_SCHEMA`'s `limit` and `cause`, `metrics.job_limits`,
   `_limit_of` and `ci_from_api`'s two new fields. `after: Task 1` — `_limit_of` reads
   `ci_pool.DEFAULT_JOB_TIMEOUT_MIN` and `job_limits` calls `timeouts()`.
3. **Task 3 — §4, the two new row kinds.** `ci_jobs.p90`, `ci_jobs.near_limit` and the three
   `NEAR_LIMIT_*` constants, `_median_p90` reading `p90`, `scorecard_rows`' `ci7=` and its two new
   row blocks, `render_daily`'s bound week, the `job()` fixture helper and `EXPECTED_DAILY`.
   `after: Task 2` — it reads `cause` off the row and imports the module Task 2 creates.
4. **Task 4 — §5, the reader that acts.** `diagnose.rank`'s `red` predicate, the two new counters on
   *both* `setdefault` literals, and the `ci-job:` cause detail. `after: Task 2` — it needs
   `ci_jobs.TIMEOUT` and `ci_jobs.RUNNER_LOSS`, and nothing Task 3 writes.

Wave order: **1**, then **2**, then **3 and 4 together**. Tasks 3 and 4 have disjoint footprints
(PD1) and neither reads the other's code, so the third wave has two seats.

Read **PD4 before touching `tests/test_metrics.py`** — the `Backfill` test the spec names by name is
not the one that reaches the real host, and patching the named one leaves a live `gh api` inside the
suite. Read **PD5 before writing `parse_timeouts`** — copying `parse_runs_on`'s loop as it stands
reads a *step's* `timeout-minutes` as the job's, which is the one mistake this parser can make. Read
**PD6 before editing `rank`** — the spec's snippet shows one of that function's two `setdefault`
literals, and the spec's own acceptance test fails if only that one gains the counters. **PD7** is
the shape of the one line in `causes` the spec writes as if it ended a statement.

| PD5 | **`parse_timeouts` needs one thing `parse_runs_on` does not have — the job's mapping-key indent — and without it a step's `timeout-minutes` is read as the job's** | The spec's docstring states the rule (*"the job's own `timeout-minutes` sits at the indent of its mapping keys, beside `runs-on:`, while a **step's** `timeout-minutes` is deeper, under `steps:`"*) and C7 says the reader is *"the same line reader as `parse_runs_on`"*. Read that loop: it tracks `jobs_indent` and `job_indent` (the indent of the job **key**), and then accepts `runs-on:` at **any** indent inside the current job, because no step in any workflow has a `runs-on:` and the depth never had to be checked (`asf/ci_pool.py:585-590`). A step *does* have a `timeout-minutes:`, so copying the loop as it stands gives the job whichever `timeout-minutes` it meets first — a step's, on the commonest workflow shape there is. **Decided:** `parse_timeouts` carries one more variable. On entering a job (the `m and indent == job_indent` branch) it resets `key_indent = None` and the job's pending minutes; the **first** non-blank line at an indent greater than `job_indent` sets `key_indent` to that indent; and `timeout-minutes:` and `name:` are read **only** when `indent == key_indent`. A sequence item at the job-key indent (`steps:` followed by `- name: …` at the same column) stripped is `- name: …`, which starts with `- ` and never with `name:`, so it cannot be mistaken for a job key. This is the whole of the parser's risk, and the spec's own acceptance table already asks for the case (*"a **step**-level `timeout-minutes` that must not be read as the job's"*) — this decision is the mechanism that makes that row pass, written down so it is not rediscovered at the third red run. |
