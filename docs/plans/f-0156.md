# Plan F-0156 — the key, the census, then the rule that reads both

Epic: E-0001 — ASF 0.1 — a working factory. Spec: `docs/specs/f-0156.md` (approved). Branch:
`plan/F-0156`.

Three Tasks, cut exactly the way the spec's `## Stories` are cut and the way `## The design` is
sectioned: the **convention** (`settled_days` and its off switch), the **census** (the
whole-namespace tip-and-containment read, the classifier, the doctor's widened row) and the **rule**
(the third arm of `candidates`, the reason on the delete line, the `reaped` log).

Task 1 owns `asf/conventions.py`. Task 2 owns `asf/workers/retention.py`'s read half — `tips`,
`settled_reason`, `census`, `census_line`, `doctor_line`, the state file's `census` key — and the one
docstring in `asf/doctor.py`. Task 3 owns the same module's write half: `candidates`' third arm,
`sweep`'s wiring, `due`'s fifth field and `reaped`.

Wave order: **1 → 2 → 3**, one coder a wave — in `after:` terms, 1 with `none`, 2 after 1, 3 after
1 and 2. There is no parallelism to win here and the plan does not pretend otherwise: all three
Tasks write `tests/test_retention.py` (Story 1's own acceptance fence is
`tests.test_retention.RetentionConventions`, so the convention's tests live there too), so any two
of them would be serialised by their `writes:` whatever their `after:` said. The order is also the
true dependency order — Task 3 reads `conv.retention('settled_days')` from Task 1 and `contained`
and `settled_reason` from Task 2.

**`settled_reason` lands in Task 2, not Task 3** (PD4). That is what makes the middle wave worth
having on its own: after Task 2 the doctor's `branches` row reports how many of origin's heads the
settled rule *would* take, per class and per prefix, while nothing has yet been deleted. D6 says
that reading is what answers this card's numeric bar, and it is what the operator line at the foot
of this session's report asks for.

**The one place this plan says the spec is wrong:** the spec's `candidates` snippet puts the settled
arm in an `elif` chain after the legacy arm, and then claims in prose that `worker/T-0110` with
T-0110 `Closed` "is dead whether or not `worker/` is a prefix the product still mints". Under the
snippet it is not — a head under a prefix that is both minted and configured `legacy` matches the
legacy arm, is dropped by the "a prefix the product still mints is never legacy" guard, and never
reaches the settled arm. PD1 has the chain that makes the prose true. Read it before Task 3.
