# Plan F-0239 — the release the park can read, then the row that reads it, then the Bug judged on it

Epic: E-0002 — ASF 0.2 — self-improvement. Spec: `docs/specs/f-0239.md` (approved). Branch:
`plan/F-0239`.

Three Tasks, in the order the spec forces — *the release, then what the park says about it, then the
row that had no lever at all*:

1. **the release, written and read** — `step_wave.released_since`, `adjudications` reading it beside
   `ruled_since`, `unpark.stalemate_release`, and `cmd_unpark`'s third source. From here an operator
   has a lever on the derived park, and the next tick acts on it.
2. **the park says how it ends** — `_capped`'s PARKED reason naming both commands, and
   `parked_cell(product, root=None)` listing every derived stalemate park in `asf status`. From here
   the two places a person looks name the levers Task 1 built.
3. **the Bug past the limit, judged on the same fact** — `bug_rows(…, adjudicated=None)`, its
   `n > limit` branch handing the row to `_capped`. From here the one class of item that reached no
   ruling, no release and no park reaches all three.

They run **1 → 2 → 3**, one at a time, and the serialisation is forced twice over. By footprint:
Tasks 1 and 2 both write `tests/test_cli.py` and `tests/test_hidden_work.py`, and Tasks 2 and 3 both
write `asf/feeder/rows.py`, so no two may share a wave. By dependency, the same order: Task 2's one
new assertion in `RulingLiftsParkTest` sits on top of the class Task 1 leaves untouched, and Task 3
needs **both** — its acceptance asserts that a *release* relaunches the Bug row (Task 1's
`released_since`) and that the parked row's reason names `` `asf unpark B-0057` `` (Task 2's reason).
Writing Task 3 first would make its own acceptance unwriteable.

**Read PD2, PD3, PD4 and PD12 before writing a line of any Task.** PD2 and PD3 are the two places
this plan departs from the spec's design, each for a reason measured in this checkout; PD4 and PD12
are the two places following the spec literally is right and a plausible "improvement" would be
wrong.

The fences of §*The fences* bind every Task; every Gate below carries them beside its own.

### Task 3: a Bug past the attempt limit is judged, not shelved
stories: S-53455
writes: asf/feeder/rows.py, tests/test_feeder.py
after: Task 1, Task 2

The spec's §*The design* §3 in full: `bug_rows` takes `adjudicated`, `_candidates` passes it, and the
`n > limit` branch stops being a dead end.

This is the half the other two make possible. Today an over-limit Bug emits
`WAITS ON operator — adjudicated after N sessions (limit 3): a person decides` and **nothing** moves
it: no ruling, no card edit, no `asf unpark`, no `asf correct`, ever. A person is told to decide and
then handed nothing that acts on a decision.

Read **PD5** (the reason's `STALEMATE → ADJUDICATE` prefix is correct — do not special-case it),
**PD6** (`tiers._remedy` prints a different clause and `asf/feeder/tiers.py` is not in this
footprint) and **PD7** (the docstring clause this Task must fix) before starting.

**Files**

- `asf/feeder/rows.py`:
  - `bug_rows` (**:787**) takes one keyword argument, `adjudicated=None`, appended after `why`.
  - its `n > limit` and `n == limit` branches (**:833-842**) merge into one, exactly as the spec
    writes it: `if n >= limit:` builds the STALEMATE row the cap builds today — `tier=tier`
    (identical to today's `0 if sev == 'S1' else 1`), `kind=STALEMATE`, `action=LAUNCH`,
    `brief_kind='adjudicate'`, `branch=branch_for(product, 'fix', b['id'])`,
    `reason=f"{sev} open after {n} sessions: adjudicate, not another fix"` — then
    `out.append(row if n == limit else _capped(row, attempts, limit, product, adjudicated))`.

    The `waits('operator', …)` call the old branch made is gone, and `waits` keeps its other four
