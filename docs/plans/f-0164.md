# Plan F-0164 — measure the publishes, stop pushing a branch that cannot publish, push only what this factory could have minted

Epic: E-0004 — Control plane — the factory's dashboard, served from the record. Spec:
`docs/specs/f-0164.md` (approved). Branch: `plan/F-0164`.

Three Tasks, one per Story, cut along the three things the spec leaves to do — and in the card's
own order, *prove first*:

1. **the split** — `run_steps` returns what each publish cost, `publish_timing_line` says it in one
   line, `health` measures its own wall clock and carries the line in `found`. Nothing the factory
   *does* changes: this Task adds a number that does not exist, and that is the only thing a later
   decision to reorder the tick's steps could rest on (spec D1).
2. **the timeout** — `gitpush` owns the words of a killed push and the reader of them, `publish`
   refuses with the timeout instead of `git_error`'s pick, and `republish_steps` counts per branch,
   caps at two and parks for a person. From here the 120 s of a doomed push stops being spent every
   tick, and the branch gets a next action instead of a loop.
3. **the guard** — `conventions.launch_prefixes()`, `publish(..., prefixes=())`, and `publish_gap`
   passing it. A branch this factory could not have minted is refused before any push, for free.

They run **1 → 2 → 3**, one at a time. The serialisation is not a choice: all three write
`asf/workers/health.py` and `tests/test_health_publish_steps.py`, and Tasks 2 and 3 both write
`asf/workers/lifecycle.py` and `tests/test_lifecycle.py`, so their `writes:` intersect and the
queue orders them anyway. The order *is* a choice, and the reason is PD5 and PD8: Tasks 2 and 3
both rewrite the refusal tail of `republish_steps`, and landing 2 first means 3 edits the final
shape once rather than rebasing its own edit onto a function that moved underneath it.

**Read PD2 and PD3 before writing a line of Task 3.** The spec's `launch_prefixes()` enumeration
is incomplete in a way that would refuse to publish every groom session's branch on every product
— measured below — and the plan fixes the enumeration rather than shipping the guard as written.
PD5, PD6 and PD7 are the three places where following the spec's design snippets literally does not
compile or does not say what the acceptance asserts.

### Task 1: the health pass says what its publishes cost
stories: S-39700
writes: asf/workers/health.py, tests/test_health_publish_steps.py
after: none

The spec's first `## In` bullet and `## The design` §§1–2: `run_steps(..., clock=None)` returns
`[(job, seconds)]`, `publish_timing_line(timings, wall, workers)` says it in one line, `health`
measures its own wall clock around the `run_steps` call and carries the line as a single
`('health', 'publishes', line)` entry in `found`.

Nothing the factory *does* changes when this lands: no judgement, no hold, no ledger field, no
push. That is why it goes first and can be proven on its own — and why the five existing
`RunStepsTest` cases staying green is what proves the timing changed no behaviour (PD14).

Read **PD5** (the queues carry no job today), **PD6** (the row's first element is the job, and the
line is printed raw) and **D3**'s spacing fence before starting.

**Files**

- `asf/workers/health.py`
  - `run_steps` (`:460-495`) — the `clock` parameter, `queues` carrying `(job, generator)` pairs
    (`:470`), the `timed` helper, `asks` as `(job, qi, ask)` (`:479`), both publish branches
    (`:486-490`), and `return timings` in place of the bare `return` (`:485`)
  - `publish_timing_line(timings, wall, workers=PUBLISH_WORKERS)` — new, beside `run_steps`
  - `health` (`:802-806`) — the wall clock around the `run_steps` call, and the one appended entry
    after the per-run ones
  - the print loop (`:845-846`) — `elif what in ('published', 'publishes'):`
  - `time` and `PUBLISH_WORKERS` are already in the module (`:52`, `:425`): no new import
- `tests/test_health_publish_steps.py` — `PublishTimingTests`; `RunStepsTest`'s five cases untouched

**Steps**

1. `run_steps` gains `clock=None` and binds `clock = clock or time.monotonic` — a fake clock is how
   the acceptance scripts the seconds without sleeping.
2. `queues = [[(entries[i][0], entries[i][2]) for i in g] for g in _groups(entries)]` (PD5). The
   send site becomes `asks.append((queue[0][0], qi, queue[0][1].send(sends[qi])))`; the
   `StopIteration` branch (`queue.pop(0)`, `sends[qi] = None`) is unchanged, and `_groups` is not
   touched at all.
3. One local helper, no `try`/`finally` (PD5):

   ```python
       def timed(job, ask):
           started = clock()
   ```
