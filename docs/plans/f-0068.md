# Plan F-0068 — seven Tasks: the seats, the keys, the count, the rule, the line, the lane, the instrument

Epic: E-0001 — ASF 0.1 — a working factory. Spec: `docs/specs/f-0068.md` (approved). Branch:
`plan/F-0068`.

Seven Tasks, cut on the spec's own §2 section boundaries and on the five test classes its §3 fences
name. Task 1 is the arithmetic everything else rests on — `Pool.banded_seats()` and the test that
pins it to `capacity.usable_slots` so the two readings of one fact cannot drift (§2.1's first half,
§3.1, §3.2). Task 2 declares the two keys and puts them on every tick line, the digest and
`asf tick watch` (§2.3, §3.4). Task 3 is the seam and the count: `wave(..., stats=...)`,
`ready_rows`, `ready_waiting`, and the two numbers stamped on the tick (§2.1's second half, §2.2,
§3.3). Task 4 is the rule, pure (§2.4, §3.5). Task 5 is the one row an operator reads (§2.5, §3.6).
Task 6 is `lane` on the `sessions` stream (§2.6's first half, §3.9). Task 7 is the throwaway
instrument and the protocol (§2.6's second half, §2.7, §3.7, §3.8, §3.10).

**The wave order is seven waves of one Task: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7.** This plan is a chain, and
PD1 says why rather than pretending otherwise: every one of §3's nine fences names a class of the
single module `tests/test_quota_pressure.py`, and four of the seven Tasks write
`asf/metrics/metrics.py`. Two Tasks whose `writes:` intersect never run together, so every pair here
is already ordered by its footprint; declaring it on an `after:` line costs nothing and makes the
order the plan's rather than the scheduler's.

§3's preamble binds every Task — *"Every fence runs from the repository root. None of them launches
a session, reaches a network or spends money."* Every **Gate** below runs the targeted fences plus
`python3 tools/run_tests.py`, `bash tools/check_generic.sh` and `bash tools/check_conventions.sh`:
this Feature touches the tick, the metrics module and the pool, and the whole-tree fences are the
only proof that a counter added to `ctx.counts` did not break a reader somewhere else.

Each Task's own **Acceptance** is the §3 fence its Story names, copied byte for byte.

### Task 1: `Pool.banded_seats()` — the seats the guard has taken, off readings the pool already holds
stories: S-33550, S-33551
writes: asf/workers/pool.py, tests/test_quota_pressure.py
after: none

§2.1's first half. One method, four lines of arithmetic, and the test that keeps it honest: `caps −
banded` must equal `capacity.usable_slots` over the same config and the same quota source, for every
band combination, or the factory holds two readings of one fact that can disagree.

Nothing here reads quota. `Pool.usage` memoises one reading per account per instance (`:343-346`)
and `Pool.band` adds the session-limit stop on top of it (`:354-358`), so the method's whole cost is
a loop over `self.accounts`. That is the property §3.1's second comment line asserts, and it is the
reason `wave` can ask the question at the end of a pass it has already paid for.

**Files**

- `asf/workers/pool.py` — `Pool.banded_seats()`, beside `band`
- `tests/test_quota_pressure.py` — new module; `SeatsTests`

**Steps**

1. `asf/workers/pool.py`: `banded_seats(self)` on `Pool`, immediately after `band` (`:358`), with
   §2.1's docstring copied as it stands — including the sentence that names
   `asf.capacity.usable_slots` as the complement and §3.2 as the test that pins them. Body exactly
   as §2.1 writes it: `caps = sum(a.cap for a in self.accounts)`, then per account
   `a.cap` at `quota_mod.STOP`, `a.cap - 1` at `quota_mod.COOLDOWN`, `0` otherwise, returning
   `(banded, caps)`.
2. The docstring's last sentence names the one asymmetry a reader will look for: an unreadable
   account bands as `STOP` (`quota.band`'s *"unknown ≠ free"*), so its whole cap counts as taken —
   which is what the pool does with it anyway.
3. `tests/test_quota_pressure.py`, new, with §3's preamble as its module docstring — every fence
   runs from the repository root, none launches a session, reaches a network or spends money — and
   the module named for F-0068 in its first line.
4. `SeatsTests`: a pool of three accounts with caps 3, 2 and 1 and a fake quota source. All free →
   `(0, 6)`. The cap-3 account stopped → `(3, 6)`. The cap-3 account in cooldown instead → `(2, 6)`.
   Build the pool through `pool_mod.Pool(accounts, quota_source=fake, guards=…)` with the guards the
   product's own `guards_from_config({})` gives, and drive the bands by what the fake source
   returns, never by patching `band` — the test is of the arithmetic *over the band function*, not
   of the arithmetic alone.
5. `SeatsTests` second: the reading source is asked exactly **once per account across two calls** to
   `banded_seats()`. Count the fake source's `read` calls; three accounts, two calls, three reads.
   An account stopped by a session limit (`limits=`) must not be read at all beyond that one, and is
   counted at its full `cap`.
6. `SeatsTests.test_caps_less_banded_is_usable_slots`: over the same `cfg` dict and the same fake
