# Plan F-0159 — a rehearsal's transcript carries no wall clock, and the test stops pinning one

Epic: E-0001 — ASF 0.1 — a working factory. Spec: `docs/specs/f-0159.md` (approved). Branch: `plan/F-0159`.

Two Tasks, cut exactly the way the spec's `## Stories` are cut and for the reason D6 gives: the
**product** stops printing a wall clock into the rehearsal's transcript (`asf/harvest/harvest.py`,
`asf/harvest/lane.py`, and the assertions that pin both shapes in `tests/test_harvest.py`), and then
the **test** stops freezing the clock and fences the transcript against the next one
(`tests/test_dry_run.py`).

They run **1 → 2**. The footprints are disjoint — nothing forces the order — but Task 2 deletes
T-0087's `mock.patch.object(harvest_mod.time, 'monotonic', return_value=0.0)`, and until Task 1 has
landed that deletion is exactly the 1-in-N red the card is about, put back. Task 2 with `after: none`
is a coder told to make the flake reproduce.

The card's own number is **already at or near 0** (P1, re-measured below), because T-0087 stopped the
bleeding two days before the card was filed. Neither Task can move that number further. What they
change is *why* it is 0: a monkeypatch nobody will remember, or a product that does not print a clock
into a document whose whole value is that two runs of it diff clean.

**The one thing every Task must know before it writes a line:** the new `GateTimingLineTests` the
spec's §4 asks for has **no harness of its own**, and the obvious way to give it one — subclassing
`ProductHarvestTests` — silently re-runs that class's **113** test methods a second time. PD1 has the
measurement and the file's own answer. A coder who does not read it either doubles the gate's slowest
module or writes a test that passes vacuously (PD2).
