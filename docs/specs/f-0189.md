# F-0189 — the seat is the machine's, but the claim was one process's memory

An account's cap is the machine's, not one product's — `Pool.from_config` already sums load over
every product's registry and over the sessions `ps` sees that no registry knows (F-0076 S-8154).
That sum is read **once**, when a wave builds its pool. From then on a wave's own launches are held
in `Pool.live`, a Python list, by `Pool.take` — and `take` writes nothing anybody else can read.

A launch does not reach the shared ledger until the very end of `spawn`: the worktree is made, the
branch is published, `worktree_setup` runs (the wave prints `setup 41.3s` for it), and only then is
the launch line appended (`asf/workers/spawn.py:955`). Two waves of two products that start inside
that window each see the account at its opening load, and each fills it to the cap. That is the
card's proof: two waves launched on one account within 7 s and put 6 sessions against a cap of 4.

The defect reproduces with no clock and no race, in this checkout:

```
$ python3 -c '...'          # two Pools, one ASF_HOME, one account cap 4 — run live, 2026-09-29
load a/b at start: 0 0
  take -> None
  take -> None
  take -> None
  take -> None
a sees 4 under_caps False
b sees 0 under_caps True
b pick -> (Account('acct-a', role='local', cap=4), '')
```

Product `a` has filled the account; product `b`'s pool says the account is empty and hands it out
four more times. Nothing is racing — the second pool simply cannot see the first one's seats.

So this card is not "make the pool read fresher". It is: **a seat must be claimed where every
product can see it, and the claim must be tested against the cap in the same atomic step**. The
mechanism is the one this repo already uses for every other cross-process hazard — an `flock` on a
file under `ASF_HOME` (`tick.acquire_lock`, `harvest.try_lock`, `ci_queue` pass lock, `snapshot`) —
applied to a new machine-wide claim file, with `Pool.take` becoming a compare-and-set that can
refuse.

### The choices

| # | Chosen | Against | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **One machine-wide claim file, `<ASF_HOME>/state/seats.json`, guarded by `flock` on `<ASF_HOME>/state/seats.lock`.** | Appending a pre-launch line to each product's `sessions.jsonl`; a lock directory per account; sqlite. | The ledger already spans products by being read across them (P4) — what it lacks is a row for a seat that is claimed but not yet launched. A pre-launch line in `sessions.jsonl` would be a run the fold has to un-invent when the spawn fails, and would reach harvest, health and every view. A separate file has exactly one reader and one writer rule, and P5 says nothing else at that path notices it. A separate lock file, not the ledger itself, so a rewrite (`os.replace`) never swaps the inode out from under a waiter. |
| C2 | **`Pool.take` is the compare-and-set and returns `True`/`False`.** Under the lock it re-reads the registries and the claims, re-applies `under_caps`, and writes the claim only if the account still fits. | A global "launch lock" serialising whole waves; a check in `pick_account` alone. | The card names `take()`, and it is the only point that is both after the pick and before the spawn — "a wave re-reads the account load under the lock just before spawning" is literally this function. A wave-wide lock would serialise two products' entire waves (minutes of setup) to fix a seat count. A check in `pick_account` alone is the bug: pick and claim must be one atomic step or the gap comes back. P1 makes the bool free. |
| C3 | **Under the lock the re-read is `lifecycle.live_all` + the claim file; the `ps`-observed extras are kept from the pool's build.** | Re-running `observe.read` per take. | P4: `observe.read` is a `ps` subprocess. Running it per launch would put a process spawn inside a held lock on every take, for a reading that changes on the scale of a session's lifetime, not a wave's. The foreign sessions it finds are already only reflected at wave start today; this card does not make that worse, and does not pretend to fix it. |
| C4 | **The compare-and-set tests `under_caps` only — the account cap and its per-model `caps`.** The S1 reserve, the 5h headroom, the capacity share and `cloud.max_inflight` stay pick-time, per-process decisions. | Re-deciding the whole `pick_account` under the lock. | The card's test is "two concurrent waves never exceed an account cap". `under_caps` is that sentence in code (P8). The others are policy over one product's own rows (the reserve, the share) or a budget with its own reading (headroom) — folding them in would change what a wave launches in ways the card did not ask for and the acceptance cannot measure. A cloud-lane seat is still **claimed** (so the count is shared) but never refused: its bound is `max_inflight`, which `take` has no settings for. Named here as out. |
| C5 | **A claim dies three ways: its claimer's pid is gone, it is older than `CLAIM_TTL_S` (30 min), or a live registered run already holds its `(product, job)`.** Every writer sweeps before it writes. | An explicit release at the end of every launch only. | An explicit release cannot survive a killed tick, and a killed tick that leaves 4 phantom claims is a worse outage than the overcommit. Rule (c) is what releases the claim on the happy path — the launch line lands (P3) and the claim stops counting the same instant, so a seat is never counted twice. Rules (a) and (b) are the crash floor. `untake` still removes its claim outright, so a failed spawn frees the seat now, not in 30 minutes. |
| C6 | **A busy lock fails the take closed** (`False`, the row re-decides); **an unusable ledger fails it open**, with `Pool.seats_degraded` set and one line printed by the wave. | Failing both closed; failing both open. | A lock held by another process is transient — milliseconds — and the caller has a retry (C7); refusing is free and safe. A ledger that cannot be created at all (no `ASF_HOME`, read-only state dir) is a host fault that would otherwise stop every launch on the machine; the pool already has this exact posture for an unreadable session table (P13), and the operator sees the line. |
| C7 | **A refused take re-decides the row, at most `SEAT_RETRIES = 2` times, through the existing `tentative` path** (P10); after that the row waits with `seat claimed elsewhere — <acct> <load>/<cap>`. | Waiting on the first refusal; retrying without a bound. | Re-deciding is what a serial wave would have done: the re-read that refused the seat is now in `self.live`, so the next `pick_account` either finds another account or produces the true reason (`pool full — accounts at cap: …`). Progress is guaranteed because each refusal strictly narrows the candidates; the counter is a fence against a pathological flapping ledger, not the mechanism. |
| C8 | **`Pool.retake(account, seat, model)` for the model swap in `settle()`** — one lock, claim replaced, never refused. | Calling `untake` + `take` and handling a refusal. | P11: the swap happens **after** the launch succeeded. Refusing there would free a seat for a session that is already running — the overcommit, inverted. `retake` states that this is a correction of a held claim, not a new claim. |
| C9 | **`asf workers spawn` claims its seat too** (P12), and prints `waits <job> — seat claimed elsewhere — …` with exit 1 when the claim is refused. | Leaving the hand launch as it is. | It picks an account off a pool and then spends the whole of `spawn` invisible to every wave — the same hole, on the path an operator reaches for precisely when the machine is busy. One call site. |
| C10 | **No new config key, no new command, no new view, no doctor row.** | A `worker_pool.seat_claims: off` switch; an `asf seats` table. | The claim is not a policy the operator chooses between — an overcommitted account is a defect in every configuration. The two constants (`CLAIM_TTL_S`, `LOCK_WAIT_S`) are module constants, as `REFRESH_S` and `DEFAULT_LAUNCH_CONCURRENCY` are in `wave`. A view over a file whose rows live for the length of one setup would mostly print nothing. |

### In, and out

**In.** A claim ledger under one lock, shared by every product on the host; `Pool.take` as a
compare-and-set against the account cap and its per-model ceiling; `Pool.from_config` counting other
products' outstanding claims; `untake`/`retake` maintaining the claim; the wave re-deciding a refused
row and printing both the refusal and the degraded state; the hand launch claiming too.

**Out, deliberately.** The 5h headroom's cross-product commitment (`_committed` stays per process);
`cloud.max_inflight` across products; the capacity share; the S1 reserve; a foreign `ps` session that
appears mid-wave (C3); any change to `sessions.jsonl`, to the fold, to harvest, to the views or to
the tick's steps. Nothing about what a wave *chooses* to launch changes — only whether the seat it
picked is still there when it takes it.

## The design

### 1. `asf/workers/seats.py` — the claim ledger (new file)

The whole cross-process mechanism lives in one module, so the rule about when a claim is alive is
written once.

```python
LEDGER = 'seats.json'          # <ASF_HOME>/state/seats.json — the machine's, not one product's
LOCK = 'seats.lock'
CLAIM_TTL_S = 30 * 60          # a claim no launch caught up with is the crash floor, not a seat
LOCK_WAIT_S = 5.0              # a holder writes one small file; a wait past this is not a holder
POLL_S = 0.02
FIELDS = ('account', 'product', 'job', 'model', 'kind', 'lane', 'pid', 'at')

class Busy(Exception): ...     # the lock was not free inside LOCK_WAIT_S
class Unusable(Exception): ... # the state dir cannot hold the ledger at all (its reason is its str)

def root():                    # os.path.join(env.ASF_HOME, 'state') — resolved per call (P7)
def ledger_path(); def lock_path()

@contextlib.contextmanager
def held(wait_s=LOCK_WAIT_S):
    """The exclusive claim lock; yields a :class:`Ledger` already swept. Raises Busy or Unusable."""

def read(registered=None, now=None):
    """The live claims, lock-free — what :meth:`Pool.from_config` counts. A garbage or missing
    file is no claims, and the reason comes back beside them as ``(claims, why)``."""
```

A `Ledger` holds `claims` (the live ones, after the sweep) and two writers, `add(claim)` and
`remove(product, job)`; each writes the whole file as `json.dumps` to `seats.json.tmp` and
`os.replace`s it, under the lock the caller holds. A sweep that dropped rows writes on the way in.

The sweep (C5) drops a claim when any of:

1. `not lifecycle.pid_alive(c['pid'])` — the tick that claimed it is gone;
2. `now - parse(c['at']) > CLAIM_TTL_S`;
3. `(c['product'], c['job'])` is the key of a live registered run in `lifecycle.live_all(root())` —
   the launch line landed, and the registry holds that seat now.

A `seats.json` that will not parse is treated as no claims and rewritten; the reason travels as
`why` so the wave can print it (C6) rather than losing it.

### 2. `asf/workers/pool.py` — the seat is claimed, not remembered

`Pool` gains one attribute and one private rebuild:

- `self.seats_degraded = ''` — why claims are not being shared, when they are not.
- `Pool._refresh(ledger)` — `self.live` is rebuilt as `registered + extras + claim rows`, where
  `registered` is `lifecycle.live_all(...)` filtered exactly as `from_config` filters it, `extras`
  are the `ps`-observed rows kept from the build (C3), and each claim with no registered run of its
  key becomes a live row. **A claim of this process's own pid is rebuilt into the exact 7-key seat
  dict `take` appended** — `{job, account, model, product, kind, wave: True, lane}` — so `untake`
  still finds it (P11); another process's claim becomes
  `{account, model, product, job, kind, lane, claim: True}`, which `load()` counts and `headroom`
  reads as a running session.

`take` becomes the compare-and-set:

```python
def take(self, account, model, job='', product=None, kind=None, lane=None):
    """Claim one seat on ``account`` for this launch, across every product on this host.

    Returns True when the seat is ours. False when the account filled up between the pick and
    this call — another product's wave claimed it, or a launch of ours landed — and the row must
    be decided again (:func:`asf.workers.wave.wave`).
    """
```

- opens `seats.held()`; on `Busy` returns `False` (C6);
- on `Unusable` sets `self.seats_degraded` and does exactly what it does today, returning `True`;
- under the lock: `self._refresh(ledger)`, then `if not self.under_caps(account, model): return False`
  — except for `lane == 'cloud'`, which is claimed unconditionally (C4);
- then `ledger.add(...)` with `pid=os.getpid()` and `at=now_iso()`, append the seat to `self.live`,
  add the cost to `self._committed`, return `True`.

`untake` keeps its signature and its `True`/`False`, and now also removes the claim under the lock;
a ledger that is `Busy` or `Unusable` there loses only the eager release — rule (a)/(b) of the sweep
still collects it. `retake(account, seat, model)` (C8) replaces the claim's `model` and the live
row's, under one lock, with no cap test, and returns the new seat dict.

### 3. `asf/workers/wave.py` — the row whose seat went

At the take site (`wave.py:296-306`) the seat is no longer assumed:

```python
if not pool.take(acct, **seat):           # another product claimed it between the pick and now
    refused[row.job] = refused.get(row.job, 0) + 1
    if refused[row.job] <= SEAT_RETRIES:
        said.remove(entry)                # decided again, as a freed seat re-decides today (P10)
        pending.insert(0, row)
        continue
    entry[1] = (f'seat claimed elsewhere — {acct.name} '
                f'{pool.load(acct)}/{acct.cap:g}')
    continue
```

`running`, `branches`, `local_taken` and `cloud_tries` are only advanced once the take succeeded —
a refused row has launched nothing and must not hold its branch or spend a cloud try. The waits line
reads:

```
waits    spec-f-0031  F-0031     — seat claimed elsewhere — acct-a 4/4
```

and, when the ledger could not be used at all, one line before the first row, beside the existing
`pool: sessions unreadable` (P13):

```
seats: claims are not shared (<why>) — another product's wave may overlap this one
```

`SEAT_RETRIES = 2` joins `REFRESH_S` and `DEFAULT_LAUNCH_CONCURRENCY` at the foot of the module.

### 4. `asf/workers/__init__.py` — the hand launch claims too

`cmd_spawn` keeps the pool it built for the pick (today it is discarded), calls `pool.take(...)`
before `spawn.spawn(...)`, and on a refusal prints
`waits {row.job} — seat claimed elsewhere — {acct.name} {load}/{cap}` and returns 1. With
`--account` given, the pool is built for the take alone. A spawn that raises calls `untake` so the
claim does not sit for its TTL.

### What it looks like when it is there

Two ticks, two products, one account at `cap: 4`, starting inside one second of each other: the
first four claims win in whatever order the lock grants them, split across the two products; every
further row of either wave prints `pool full — accounts at cap: acct-a 4/4` or, for the row that
lost the race between its pick and its claim, `seat claimed elsewhere — acct-a 4/4`. The account
carries 4 sessions, never 6.

## Acceptance tests

One new test file, `tests/test_seat_claims.py`, built on the temp-`ASF_HOME` fixtures that already
exist (`tests/test_workers.py:82-115`, `tests/test_capacity.py:32-52`), with `sessions: 'fake'`
so nothing shells out (P14).

**The card's test — two concurrent waves never exceed an account cap.** `TwoWavesOneAccount` makes
one temp `ASF_HOME` with `state/a` and `state/b`, one account at `cap: 4`, and starts two real
child processes with `ASF_HOME` in their environment, each running `wave_mod.wave` for its own
product over 4 rows with a `spawn_fn` that sleeps `SETUP_S = 0.6` and then appends its launch line
through `pool_mod.append_session` — the same last step the real `spawn` takes (P3), which is what
makes the window real. Each child writes `ready-<p>` and waits for `go`; the parent touches `go`
once both are ready, so the waves overlap by construction. Both children exit 0; the launches they
report sum to **exactly 4** — not fewer, so no seat is lost to the protocol — and the two registries
together hold 4 live runs. Against this checkout the sum is 8.

## Stories

- S: The claim ledger is one file under one lock, and a claim dies three ways — `asf/workers/seats.py` with `held()`/`read()`, the `{account, product, job, model, kind, lane, pid, at}` row, an atomic whole-file rewrite, and the sweep that drops a claim whose claimer is dead, whose age is past `CLAIM_TTL_S`, or whose `(product, job)` a live registered run already holds; a garbage file reads as no claims with its reason, an unwritable state root raises `Unusable`, and a lock not free inside `LOCK_WAIT_S` raises `Busy` — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_seat_claims.SeatLedger`
- S: `Pool.take` is a compare-and-set across products — the account load is re-read under the lock from the registries and the claim file, `under_caps` decides, a refused take writes nothing and returns `False`, `from_config` counts every other product's outstanding claim so the pick sees them, `untake` returns seat and claim together, `retake` moves a held claim's model without ever being refused, and an unusable ledger degrades to today's in-memory take with `seats_degraded` naming why — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_seat_claims.PoolClaims`
- S: A wave re-decides the row whose seat was taken under it, and every launch path claims before it spawns — a refused take re-enters the wave's existing re-decide path up to `SEAT_RETRIES` times without holding the row's branch or spending a cloud try, then waits with `seat claimed elsewhere — <acct> <load>/<cap>`; the degraded line prints once before the first row; and `asf workers spawn` takes its seat before `spawn` and frees it when the spawn raises — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_seat_claims.WaveRefusedSeat tests.test_workers tests.test_cloud`
- S: Two concurrent waves of two products never exceed an account cap — two real processes over one `ASF_HOME`, released together by a start file, each racing four rows at one account of `cap: 4` through a launch stub that holds the setup window open and then appends its launch line, both exit 0 and their launches sum to exactly 4, with 4 live runs across the two registries — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_seat_claims.TwoWavesOneAccount`
