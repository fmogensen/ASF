# F-0177 — 94% of the step is a process starting, and the step starts 566 of them one at a time

The card's measurement is unusually clean, and it says something narrow. A read-only equivalent of
the tick's `wave` step — `python -m cProfile -m asf.cli harvest --dry-run` — took **36.8s of wall
clock and logged 11.7s of user+sys CPU**. The missing 25 seconds are not lost; they are named:
**34.5s (94%) sat inside `subprocess.run` / `select.poll`**, which is this process blocked with
nothing to do, waiting for a child to answer.

Two children, and they are answering 566 separate times:

- **`gh`, 17 invocations, ~20.8s** (57% of the wall) — ~1.0–1.5s each, which is a network round
  trip to GitHub and back. Eight of them are one `gh pr checks` per gated PR.
- **`git`, 549 invocations, 11.2s** — ~20ms each, which is a process starting, mapping a repo and
  exiting. Three to four of them per lane branch per fact, across 30+ branches.

Neither number is a computation this factory could do faster. **Both are latency, and latency is
the one cost that does not have to be paid serially.** A step that spends 94% of itself blocked on
independent reads is a step whose reads are being issued in the wrong shape, not a step that is
doing too much work.

There is also a second, smaller thing hiding in the 549: the same read, issued again. `touched_files`
ran **105 times** for ~30 branches; `already_on_trunk` **105 times**; `branch_facts` **108**. The
lane's fact functions are pure reads over `origin/<trunk>...origin/<branch>`, and within one pass
those refs do not move — so the second and third call of each one is a process spawned to be told
something this process was told 40 milliseconds ago.

So: two mechanisms, in the order they pay, and nothing else.

1. **A pass-scoped read cache** at the two places the lane shells out — `harvest.sh` for `git` and
   `harvest._gh` for `gh` — so a read issued twice in one pass costs one process. It is dropped
   the instant anything in the pass writes, at exactly the chokepoints `asf.mutation_guard` already
   guards, so no call site has to remember to invalidate it.
2. **A bounded prefetch** that issues the per-branch `git` reads, and the gated PRs' `gh pr
   checks`, eight at a time *before* the decision loop runs — then throws the answers away. The
   loop is untouched, still sequential, and reads them back out of the cache. The prefetch is
   never load-bearing: skip it, break it, turn it off, and the pass computes exactly the same
   thing at exactly today's speed.

The third cost the card names — `ci_queue.relieve_trunk`'s 9.59s and its re-scan of the same
items at growing queue positions — is **not** a third mechanism, and §Out says why: its `gh` reads
are already the one-per-pass minimum, and what is left of it is CPU inside an 11.7s CPU budget.

### In

- **`harvest.read_cache()`** — a context manager opening one pass-scoped store `{(cwd, argv): (rc,
  stdout, stderr)}`, off by default, nesting the way `mutation_guard.active()` nests (only the
  outermost exit closes it). With `harvest.drop_reads()` to empty it and `harvest.read_stats()` to
  report what it did.
- **`harvest.sh(cmd, cwd=None, env=None, cache=False)`** — `cache=True` is the call site saying
  *this read is a function of refs that do not move inside a pass*. Only such a call is served
  from, or stored in, the cache, and only when `env is None`.
- **`harvest.READ_SUBCOMMANDS`** — the `git` subcommands that read. A `git` argv through `sh`
  **outside** that set drops the whole cache before it runs. This is the invalidation, and it is
  the complement of a small list rather than a list of writes, so an unrecognised subcommand drops
  rather than serving something stale.
- **The same drop at the three `mutation_guard` chokepoints** — `gitpush.push` drops unconditionally
  (every call moves a ref or refuses); `harvest._gh` and `ci_queue.GitHubSource.gh_try` drop when
  `mutation_guard.is_mutating_gh(args)` says the argv writes.
- **`harvest._gh` reads cached, keyed by argv** — a non-mutating `gh` argv is served from and stored
  in the cache. `gh_json` gets it for free, since it is a wrapper over `_gh`.
- **`harvest.prefetch(calls, width=PREFETCH_WIDTH)`** — `pool.map` over zero-argument callables in a
  `ThreadPoolExecutor`, `PREFETCH_WIDTH = 8`, results discarded, every exception swallowed. Each
  worker re-enters `mutation_guard.active()` when the submitting thread had it on.
- **`Lane.prefetch_facts(names, runs)`** — called inside `gather`, after `names` is known and before
  the per-branch loop: the per-branch readers of P2, for every branch, through `prefetch`.
- **`Lane.prefetch_checks(found)`** — called in `lane_pass` between `gather` and the `advance` loop:
  `pr_checks(self.slug, number)` for every branch in `found` with an OPEN PR that is not landed and
  not already on the trunk, through `prefetch`.
- **One `lane: reads …` line per pass** — what the pass spent on processes, split by `git` / `gh` /
  served from the cache, with the seconds **only when the pass is not a dry run** (P8).

### Out

- **No `git` command is merged, rewritten, batched or replaced.** The 549 spawns stay 549 distinct
  reads minus their duplicates; what changes is how many of them are in flight at once. Folding
  `trunk_history`'s two spawns into one `--cherry-mark` log with `%P`, or `touched_files` and
  `already_on_trunk` into one diff, would change what `git` is asked and therefore what the lane
  may conclude — for ~10% of an 11.2s cost, against every landing decision in the factory.
- **The decision loop is not parallelised.** `for b in sorted(found): lane.advance(...)` stays
  sequential and stays in that order. `branch_facts` mutates `Lane` state (`orphans_taken` against
  `ORPHANS_PER_PASS`, `self.out` lines, `self.results`), and `advance` pushes refs, opens PRs and
  merges. Threading the decisions would put the pass's own output order — which P8 pins — and its
  per-pass caps at the mercy of scheduling, to save a CPU cost that the measurement says is 6% of
  the step.
- **`ci_queue`'s reads are not cached.** P7: `gh run list` is already one call per workflow per
  pass through `listing`, and `listing` is **hand-patched** when the pass cancels a run so later
  steps skip it. A second copy of those runs keyed by argv would not be patched, and would hand a
  later step a run this pass had just cancelled. `Queue._needs` and the file-cached estimate cover
  the rest. Only `gh_try`'s *writes* participate here, by dropping the cache.
- **`relieve_trunk`'s re-scan is not changed.** The same item logged "would wait" at 14th, 18th,
  24th and 25th in line is the admission scan naming each entry's position — it is the transcript,
  and collapsing it would change what a rehearsal prints (P8). What it costs is CPU, inside a
  measured **11.7s total CPU** for the whole 36.8s run; the 9.59s cumulative the card attributes to
  it is overwhelmingly the `gh` time of the same six calls counted once more up the stack.
- **No convention key, no config key, no flag.** `PREFETCH_WIDTH` is a module constant. A width is
  a property of this host's cores and of GitHub's tolerance, not of a product's conventions, and
  `conventions.HARVEST_KEYS` is a fence four files wide for a number nobody would set.
- **No new module.** P11: a new `asf/**/*.py` enters three whole-tree fences, and both functions
  being wrapped already live in `asf/harvest/harvest.py`, which `lane.py` imports as `H`.
- **No new record, no new event, no new metric stream.** P9: `[step:wave] <s>s` already carries the
  number this card is about.
- **No wall-clock assertion anywhere in acceptance.** A test that asserts a pass got faster is a
  test that goes red on a loaded host. Acceptance asserts the *structure*: no read argv twice, the
  same facts with the prefetch on and off, and a recorded concurrency high-water mark.
- **`ls-remote` is not in the cacheable set.** `remote_heads` is one call per pass already
  (`lane.py:1230`), it is the snapshot the whole pass is taken against, and it is the one `git`
  read here whose answer can change with no local write at all.
- **`STORY_ID_RE`'s four-digit shape is not fixed here** (P14).

### The choices

| # | Chosen | Against | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | **Overlap the latency; do not reduce the work** | Ask `git` and `gh` fewer, bigger questions | The measurement leaves no room for doubt about which one pays: 94% of the wall is a process being waited for, and 11.7s of CPU says the asking and parsing is nearly free. Fewer, bigger questions is the only option here that can change a landing decision, and it would be trading correctness risk against the 6%. |
| D2 | **Cache at the two subprocess wrappers, keyed by `(cwd, argv)`** | Cache at the fact functions, keyed by `(branch, fact)` | The argv is the thing that actually costs a process, and it is the thing three different callers arrive at from three different directions — which is why `touched_files` ran 105 times for 30 branches. Keying by fact would need every one of the card's per-branch functions to grow a cache parameter and every caller to thread it, and would still miss the duplicate that arrives through a fourth path. The argv key also makes the `gh pr checks` warm trivial: P4 says its argv is a function of `(slug, number)` alone, so the prefetch never has to know what `required` or `rerun` mean. |
| D3 | **`cache=True` opt-in per call site, with a fail-safe default of off** | Cache every read-shaped `git` argv implicitly | P3 found the exact trap: `merge_queue.py:381` runs `git diff --name-only --diff-filter=U` with no revs, against the index of a merge in progress, two lines before `git merge --abort`. A subcommand allow-list would serve that a stale answer. Opt-in means the worst a forgotten site can do is stay as slow as today, and a reviewer can see at each of the dozen cached sites that the read names two revs. |
| D4 | **Invalidate by the complement — a `git` argv outside `READ_SUBCOMMANDS` drops the whole cache** | Invalidate at each site that moves a ref; or list the writes | Listing the writes fails in the unsafe direction: a subcommand nobody thought of, or one added later, would serve a stale read. The complement fails in the safe direction — an unrecognised argv drops a cache, which costs a few processes and can never be wrong. And doing it *inside* `sh` rather than at each site is the same structural argument `mutation_guard`'s own docstring makes: every individual `if self.dry_run` was correct and the bug still happened, because correctness at N call sites is a property nobody can hold. `git merge-tree --write-tree` and `git commit-tree` (P3) are outside the set for this reason, though they move no ref. |
| D5 | **Drop the cache at exactly the `mutation_guard` chokepoints** | Reason about which writes could affect which cached read | That set is already argued, already tested, and already the answer to "where does this process change the world" — `gitpush.push`, `harvest._gh`, `ci_queue.gh_try`. Reusing it means the cache's invalidation is exactly as complete as the dry-run guard is, and one more mutating call site in the future gets both properties from one edit instead of neither. A whole-cache drop, never a selective one: a `run cancel` changes what `gh pr checks` says, a ref push changes what every `origin/<branch>` read says, and a partial drop is a judgement call made once and wrong later. |
| D6 | **A prefetch that calls the lane's own fact functions and discards the answers** | A prefetch that knows the argv it wants, or one that returns the facts for the loop to use | Discarding makes the prefetch structurally incapable of changing an answer: it produces nothing anyone reads. The loop still calls the same functions and still derives everything itself, so the only observable effect of the prefetch is *when* the processes ran. It also means the prefetch needs no knowledge of what `already_on_trunk` does with its file list, and does not go stale when one of those functions changes its argv. |
| D7 | **Warm the open PR of every non-landed branch in `found`, a small superset of the gated set** | Warm only the PRs the pass will actually gate | The predicate for "will gate" is the `next_state` machine, and coupling the prefetch to it is exactly the brittleness D6 buys its way out of. A prefetch that guesses wide costs one extra `gh` read and can never produce a wrong answer; the facts it filters on (`landed`, `on_trunk`, `pr.state`) are already sitting in `found[b]` from `gather`. Against the card's numbers — 8 gated PRs, the round trips going from 8 sequential to 2 at width 8 — a handful of extra reads is a good trade. |
| D8 | **One `PREFETCH_WIDTH = 8` for both `git` and `gh`** | Separate widths, or a configured one | 8 is `ci_from_api`'s existing width over `gh api` calls in this codebase (`metrics.py:1682`, P6), so it is a precedent for concurrent `gh` here, not a guess. Two constants would need two justifications and a reason they differ; the `git` half is bounded by cores at roughly the same number, and the `gh` half is two orders of magnitude below anything GitHub objects to. |
| D9 | **Carry `mutation_guard` into the prefetch's workers** | Rely on the prefetch only ever submitting reads | It *is* only reads, so nothing needs this today — which is precisely the argument `mutation_guard`'s docstring refuses to accept, because P5 shows the guard is thread-local and `metrics.py:1682` already runs `gh` in a pool that cannot see it. Three lines here close the class for the first pool that submits something someone later makes mutating, and leave a named precedent for the next one. |
| D10 | **The counts line drops its seconds under a dry run** | Print the seconds always; or print no line at all | P8's rule is explicit and has a working precedent in `product_gate(timing=False)` for a line in exactly this position. The counts themselves are a function of the record and the origins, so they stay. And a line is worth having: when this step is slow again, the next reader needs to know whether it was `git`, `gh`, or a cache that stopped hitting — the card had to run `cProfile` against a live product to learn that once. |

## The design

### 1. The cache, in `asf/harvest/harvest.py`, beside `sh` and `_gh`

```python
#: ``git`` subcommands that only read. An argv through :func:`sh` whose subcommand is NOT here
#: drops the whole read cache before it runs (F-0177 D4): the complement is listed, not the
#: writes, so a subcommand nobody has thought of yet invalidates a cache instead of being served a
#: stale answer. ``merge-tree --write-tree`` and ``commit-tree`` are deliberately absent — they
#: move no ref, but they write objects, and the safe side of that line is the cheap side.
#: ``ls-remote`` is absent too: it is the one read here whose answer moves with no local write,
#: and it is the snapshot the whole pass is taken against (:meth:`asf.harvest.lane.Lane.remote_heads`).
READ_SUBCOMMANDS = frozenset({'rev-parse', 'rev-list', 'log', 'diff', 'merge-base',
                              'for-each-ref', 'cat-file', 'show', 'cherry'})

#: How many reads the prefetch has in flight (F-0177 D8). 8 is what
#: :func:`asf.metrics.metrics.ci_from_api` already uses for concurrent ``gh api`` calls.
PREFETCH_WIDTH = 8
```

`read_cache()` is a context manager over thread-shared state guarded by a `threading.Lock`,
nesting like `mutation_guard.active()`:

```python
@contextlib.contextmanager
def read_cache():
    """One store of read answers for the life of the ``with`` block — a *pass*, during which
    ``origin/<trunk>`` and ``origin/<branch>`` do not move. A ``git`` read marked ``cache=True``
    (:func:`sh`) and a non-mutating ``gh`` argv (:func:`_gh`) are served from it and stored in it;
    anything that writes empties it (:data:`READ_SUBCOMMANDS`, :func:`asf.gitpush.push`,
    :func:`is_mutating_gh`). Nests: only the outermost exit closes it, so a helper opening its own
    block never closes its caller's."""
```

`sh` grows one argument and two branches; the shape it returns is unchanged, and the cache stores
the three fields rather than the `CompletedProcess` itself, so no caller can poison it by mutating
what it was handed:

```python
def sh(cmd, cwd=None, env=None, cache=False):
    """``cmd`` in ``cwd``, a :class:`subprocess.CompletedProcess`. ``cache``: this read is a
    function of refs that do not move inside a pass, so inside :func:`read_cache` it costs one
    process however many callers ask for it (F-0177: ``touched_files`` ran 105 times for 30
    branches). Never with an ``env`` of its own, and never for a ``git diff`` against the index or
    the worktree — mark only a read that names its revs."""
```

- cache open, `cache=True`, `env is None` → the key is `(cwd, tuple(cmd))`; a hit rebuilds a
  `CompletedProcess` from the stored triple and counts a hit; a miss runs it and stores the triple.
- cache open, `cmd[0] == 'git'` and `cmd[1] not in READ_SUBCOMMANDS` → `drop_reads()`, then run.
- anything else → exactly today's behaviour.

`_gh` needs no new argument, because P4's argv is already the whole key and
`mutation_guard.is_mutating_gh` already sorts reads from writes:

- cache open, not mutating → served from / stored in the cache, keyed `(None, ('gh', *args))`.
- cache open, mutating → `drop_reads()`, then the existing guard check, then run.

`ci_queue.GitHubSource.gh_try` and `gitpush.push` each gain one line: drop the cache when this
call writes (`gh_try`) or unconditionally (`push`, where every call writes or refuses). Neither
starts caching anything — P7 and the `listing` patching are why.

### 2. The prefetch, in `asf/harvest/harvest.py`

```python
def prefetch(calls, width=PREFETCH_WIDTH):
    """Run zero-argument ``calls`` up to ``width`` at a time and throw every answer away — they
    are in :func:`read_cache` now, which is the point. The caller's sequential pass then derives
    everything itself, from the same functions, in the same order, at cache speed.

    **Never load-bearing.** Nothing reads what this returns; an exception in one call is swallowed,
    leaving that one read for the pass to make itself. Skip this function entirely and the pass
    computes exactly the same thing, exactly as slowly as before it existed.

    Each worker re-enters :func:`asf.mutation_guard.active` when the submitting thread had it on:
    the guard is ``threading.local`` (F-0177 D9), so a pool is a hole in it by default, and a pool
    that only submits reads today is a pool somebody changes tomorrow."""
```

Returns the number of calls made, for the counts line. `contextlib.suppress(Exception)` per call —
a broken prefetch must never be able to fail a pass.

### 3. The two warms, in `asf/harvest/lane.py`

`Lane.prefetch_facts(names, runs)`, called from `gather` immediately after `names` is complete and
before `for b in sorted(names)` (P10 — every branch and its item are known by then):

```python
def prefetch_facts(self, names, runs):
    """The per-branch git reads of every branch in ``names``, eight at a time, before the
    sequential loop reads them back out of the cache: ``touched_files``, ``changed_lines``,
    ``_subjects``, ``merge_commits``, ``trunk_history``, the ahead count, then — in a second wave,
    because its argv is a function of the first wave's answer — ``already_on_trunk``. Answers
    discarded (:func:`asf.harvest.harvest.prefetch`)."""
```

Two waves, because `already_on_trunk`'s path-filtered diff (P2, `lane.py:847`) takes
`touched_files`'s output as arguments: wave 1 is the reads that need only
`(repo, trunk, branch)`; wave 2 is `already_on_trunk(repo, trunk, b, conv, item)` with no `files=`,
which now finds its `touched_files` in the cache and issues only the one diff it adds.

`Lane.prefetch_checks(found)`, called from `lane_pass` between `gather` and the `advance` loop:

```python
def prefetch_checks(self, found):
    """``gh pr checks`` for every branch in ``found`` whose PR is OPEN and which is neither landed
    nor already on the trunk — eight round trips at a time instead of one after another (F-0177:
    8 calls, 8.17s). A small superset of the PRs the pass will actually gate, by choice (D7): a
    warm that guesses wide costs one read and can never give a wrong answer, and the argv of
    ``pr_checks`` is a function of ``(slug, number)`` alone (P4), so ``required`` and ``rerun`` are
    none of this method's business."""
```

`lane_pass` opens the window, once, around everything after its fetch:

```python
H.sh(['git', 'fetch', '-q', '--prune', 'origin'], cwd=lane.repo)
with H.read_cache():
    try:
        found = lane.gather(prs=True)          # prefetch_facts inside
        lane.prefetch_checks(found)
        for b in sorted(found):
            lane.advance(found[b])
    finally:
        lane.finish_ref_pushes()
    lane.out(H.reads_line(dry_run=lane.dry_run))
    ...                                        # the ci_queue pass, as now
```

`gate_pass` (`lane.py:2298`) gets the same `with` around its own body. It gathers only when its
caller handed it no `found` (`:2305-2307`) — but when it does, that is the same `gather` over the
same per-branch readers, with `prs=False`, and so the same fan-out. Its own half adds little
(`precheck` re-reads `touched_files` only for an entry whose facts carry none, `lane.py:2468`; the
gate's `git worktree add` at `:2783` is outside `READ_SUBCOMMANDS` and drops the cache, which is
correct and costs nothing at that point). The window is opened there for the `gather` alone, and
because a pass that is about to spawn a whole suite is the one place where a few hundred saved
processes matter least and cost nothing.

### 3a. What a drop mid-loop costs, and why that is the right answer

A branch the pass actually *works* on drops the cache from under the branches after it:
`Lane.fresh_trunk` fetches (`lane.py:1576-1580`), `drop_trunk_copies` runs `merge-tree
--write-tree` and `commit-tree` (`:682`, `:721`, `:908`), a ref push goes through `gitpush.push`,
and each of those empties the store. Every branch the loop has not reached yet then re-spawns its
reads at today's cost.

This is not a flaw to be engineered around, and the spec states it plainly rather than leaving a
reviewer to find it:

- **It is correct.** A fetch moved `origin/<trunk>`; `already_on_trunk` and `trunk_history` for
  every remaining branch genuinely have different answers now. Serving the prefetched ones would
  be the bug this card must not introduce.
- **It is the uncommon case.** The card's own profile is a `--dry-run`, where every push, archive,
  delete and merge is a `DRY:` line and no ref moves at all; and on a live pass the drops happen on
  the branches that had work, which is a handful of the thirty-plus in the lane.
- **It costs speed only.** The prefetch is never load-bearing (D6): after a drop, the pass is a
  pass with no prefetch, which is the pass that runs today.

No attempt is made to invalidate selectively — to keep the reads of branches a fetch could not
have affected. That is a judgement about which refs a `git fetch -q origin <trunk>` moved, made
once in code and wrong the first time someone passes it a refspec, and D5 is the argument against
it.

