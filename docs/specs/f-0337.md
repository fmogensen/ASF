# F-0337 — 572 parked cards, and nothing that ever looks at them again

Intake parks a card by writing `priority: later` on it (`asf/kernel/ports.py:717-722`, the
`intake-decide` verdict's own write). Parking is total: the card and everything under it through
`parent` become `State.PARKED` — no launch, no PR upkeep, no answer, no mint, never Stuck, never
LIMBO, holding nobody through `after:` or `writes` (`decide.py:_parked`, `:1182-1193`; the module
docstring's own Parked rule).

Nothing in the kernel ever reads a parked card again. There is no un-park, no expiry, no revisit:
the one writer that sets `priority:` is the intake decision that parked it, and the Definition of
Ready — Stage 3, the only groomer the kernel has — cannot reach a parked card at all, because
`hold_unready` skips `iid in parked` and only considers cards it judged `start` (`decide.py:669-671`).

So the pile only grows. Measured on the record the kernel itself reads, this morning (P1):

| | |
|---|---|
| parked cards that are not Done | **572** (254 Story, 220 Task, 94 Feature, 4 Bug) |
| cards that carry the park themselves (`priority: later` on their own card) | **94** — every one a Feature |
| cards parked by an ancestor, never by their own field | **478**, of which 224 are Tasks or Bugs |
| distinct parked ancestors those 478 hang under | **97** |
| New Tasks/Bugs, not parked, that no lane has launched | 16 |
| the oldest card on the record | 19 days (the record is 19 days old) |

Two of those rows decide the shape of this card, and they are the two a reading of the trigger
would have got wrong:

* **Nobody is parked by their own card except Features.** All 224 parked Tasks and Bugs are parked
  because a Feature above them carries `later`. And `_parked` has no child override: it walks
  `parent` upward and parks the item the moment *any* ancestor says `later`, whatever the child's
  own field says (`:1186-1192`). **A Task cannot be un-parked.** Clearing `priority:` on a Task
  that never carried it writes nothing and changes nothing; the only card whose field can revive
  anything is the one the park is on. Verified by running both halves (P5).
* **Nothing is 30 days old.** The record began 19 days ago, so an age floor in months sweeps
  exactly nothing, and "142 Parked Tasks from September" means *parked twelve to nineteen days
  ago*, not parked last quarter. The floor has to be in days.

Read through those two facts, Stage 4 is this: a continuous sweep, on seats no other work wants,
over the New cards nothing launched and over **the 94 cards that carry a park** — oldest first,
`kernel.groom.sweep_per_tick` of them a tick — each getting one `groom-sweep` verdict that code
applies: `retire`, `keep` or `revive`. 63 of those 94 parks are already over a week old; at three
a tick on a two-minute clock the backlog drains in under an hour, and then the sweep is quiet
until cards age into it. And `asf kernel waits` grows a parked-age histogram, so the pile is a
number an operator reads rather than one a spec had to measure by hand.

### In

* **One new module**, `asf/kernel/sweep.py`: the candidate set and its order, the seat rule, the
  `GROOM-SWEEP` verdict grammar and its parser, the brief's context lines and the parked-age
  histogram. Pure — it reads nothing but its arguments, as `dor.py` and `intake.py` are.
* **One new session kind**, `groom-sweep`: read-only, judging, never landing, on the light model,
  on `conv.prefix('groom-sweep')` + the id.
* **Two candidate halves, one order**: every New Task/Bug nothing launched, then every card that
  carries `priority: later` itself — each older than `kernel.groom.sweep_min_age_d`, never swept,
  oldest first, New before Parked.
* **Three verdicts**, a closed vocabulary: `retire` (with an optional `superseded_by`), `keep`,
  `revive`. Code applies each one; the session only judges.
* **Two new record writes**: `unpark` (the card's `priority:` deleted) and `retire` (a `removed:`
  retirement with no successor). A `retire` that names a successor reuses the shipped `supersede`
  untouched.
* **One new machine key**, `kernel_swept`: the stamp of the one sweep a card ever gets, written when
  its session launches, carrying no kernel version.
* **One new config section**, `kernel.groom`: `sweep`, `sweep_per_tick` (3), `sweep_max_concurrent`
  (2), `sweep_min_age_d` (7).
* **The parked-age histogram** on `Plan`, in the saved plan, and in the `asf kernel waits` view —
  built whether the sweep is on or off.
* **The registrations P7 and P8 name**: `READ_ONLY`, `NO_LANDING_KINDS`, `MODEL_KINDS`, the
  `report_result`/`run_text` pair that reads a verdict block off the whole log, and the applier's
  pairwise live-session guard.

### Out

* **Changing what parking means.** `_parked` keeps walking `parent` with no child override, and a
  parked card keeps getting no launch, no upkeep, no Stuck and no LIMBO. This card reads that rule
  and never edits it.
* **Sweeping a card that is parked by an ancestor** (478 of the 572, P1). Its fate is its park's,
  decided once on the card that carries the park, not 478 times (D2). A Task that happens to carry
  its own `later` *is* swept — the rule is the field, not the type.
* **Deriving, re-ranking or re-deciding anything.** The sweep writes `removed:`, deletes
  `priority:`, or writes nothing but its mark. No `rank`, no `parent`, no `decided:`, no machine
  `state`, no acceptance, no `writes:`. A revived card is judged by the next tick exactly as any
  New card is — which is the whole of "`+ DoR`": Stage 3 then holds it and fills it, with no new
  path through the Definition of Ready.
* **Cascading a retirement onto the parked cards under it.** P5 shows they stay parked and
  invisible, because `items()` forces a retired card to `later`. Retiring 478 descendants is a
  record-wide write of a different size and risk, and nothing reads them meanwhile.
* **Re-sweeping.** One card, one verdict, ever (D6). A card the operator wants looked at again is
  looked at again by clearing `kernel_swept` — a field, not a second config key and a second
  counter for a case this record measures at zero.
* **Any new verdict for the New half.** A New card is not parked, so `revive` has nothing to
  un-park and is refused on it (D5); `retire` and `keep` are the two that mean something.
* **A tick log line, a doctor row, a metrics event or a status column.** The sweep's output is the
  cards it writes and the histogram `asf kernel waits` prints. The applier logs each verdict the way
  `groom_fill` does, and that is the whole of its telemetry.
* **Turning the sweep on in any product file.** The `kernel.groom.sweep` default is true in
  `settings.SPEC` and false on the bare `Config`, which is exactly `intake`'s shape: on for a real
  product the next time it reads its block, off for every unit test that does not ask for it.
* **The `asf kernel waits` ledger, its classes, its targets and its existing tables.** The histogram
  is a section appended to the view; not one existing number moves.

### Preconditions the work depends on

1. `_parked(items)` parks a card whose own or whose ancestor's `priority` is `later`, with no child
   override (`decide.py:1182-1193`), and `items()` forces a retired card to Done and `later`
   (`ports.py:337-341`). P5 ran both.
2. `set_typed(rec, updates, writer=…, product=…, history=[…])` writes typed fields through the
   parser round-trip and the record stage, deleting a key whose value is None, and appends its
   History in the same write (`setfield.py:350-402`). `removal(why, [])` is a retirement with no
   landing ref. P5 ran both; neither writer needs registering (`stage.guarded` stages an unknown
   writer under its own name).
3. `RealRecord.supersede(item_id, by, why)` already writes a retirement with `superseded_by:` and
   the matching `supersedes:` on the successor, refusing a successor that is not on the record
   (`ports.py:576-605`). The sweep's `retire` with a successor is that call, unchanged.
4. `Item.created` is the card's creation date — `created:`, else its History's `created` line, else
   `stage_since` (`ports.py:224-232`) — and is present on 100 % of the population (P1).
   `intake.py:267` is the precedent for ordering by it with `'~'` sorting the undated last.
5. `FINISH_FIRST` + `queued` give last place and a who-is-waiting read (P6); `_free(facts, config)`
   is seats less live sessions, and a Launch of a read-only kind takes one.
6. `Launch` carries a `findings` list that both judging lanes use for context — the groom-fill's
   `dor` reason, the intake's goals (`decide.py:1664`, `:1692`) — so the sweep's brief needs no new
   action field.
7. The applier's `self.set(iid, **fields)` batches machine-key writes per card into one

8. `Facts.now` is an ISO-8601 UTC string, '' when unknown (`model.py:365`); `decide._utc`
   (`:911-916`) parses that shape and a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` too.
9. The record holds no Story for F-0337 and this session's block is `S:122855-122904` (P11).

### The choices

| | chosen | against | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **The sweep is a second lane beside the Definition of Ready**, with its own kind, its own block and its own budget | Widen Stage 3: add the three words to `dor.VERDICTS` and let `groom-fill` answer both questions | They are different questions about different cards. | D2 | **The Parked half sweeps the card that carries the park**, whatever its type — not its parked descendants | One judgement per parked card (572 of them), the reading the trigger's "551 parked items" suggests | `revive` is not expressible on a descendant: `_parked` has no child override, so clearing `priority:` on a Task that never carried it changes nothing (P5's first and third blocks are the proof). | D4 | **A card Stage 3 is about to groom-fill is not a sweep candidate**, read off the DoR's own hold map for this tick | Use the per-card fill cap (`dor_fills >= max_fills`) to tell the lanes apart | P3 measured `dor_fills` as 0 on every card on the record, because it is gated on the running kernel version — so a cap-based split would hand every DoR card to the sweep as well. The tick's own `unready` map is the fact that cannot be stale: if Stage 3 holds the card this tick, Stage 4 leaves it alone this tick |
| D5 | **Three verdicts, and `revive` is refused on a card that is not parked** | A fourth verdict for the New half; or let `revive` mean "re-rank it" on a New card | `revive` has exactly one meaning — delete the `priority:` that parks a subtree — and a New card has none to delete. Refusing it is a one-line guard whose message names what the card actually is, where accepting it would be a write that silently does nothing (the failure mode D2 exists to avoid) |
| D6 | **One sweep per card, ever: `kernel_swept` is stamped when the session launches**, with no version gate | Stamp it when the session ends; or count tries and allow `sweep_max_tries` | Stamping at launch is how `dor_fills` is counted (`apply.py:432-434`) and it is the only version that cannot loop: a session that crashes, is killed over age, or returns an unparseable block has still spent the card's judgement, and a candidate filter with no steady state re-fires every tick for ever. No version gate because P3 shows what the gate does to a counter that is meant to be permanent. The cost of being wrong is one card left as it was, carrying a note saying why — against the cost of a model being paid to re-judge 572 cards on every release |
| D7 | **`retire` takes `superseded_by` when there is one and works without it**; a value that is neither an id nor a sha is a rejection, never an absence | Require it, as `dor`'s `superseded` does | Most of the 94 parks are not superseded by anything: they are obsolete, which is a reason and not a pointer — and `asf retire --why` is the shipped way to say exactly that (`removal(why, [])`, P5). Requiring a successor would push the model into naming a plausible one, which is worse than no pointer. The silent-default risk is closed the other way round: `reason` is required always, and an unreadable `superseded_by` rejects the block whole instead of being read as "none" |
| D8 | **The sweep takes a seat only when no other class wants one**: last in `FINISH_FIRST`, and only while `queued` holds no `'seat'`, `'wip'` or `'overlap'` entry — plus `sweep_max_concurrent` live sessions at most | Order alone — being last in `FINISH_FIRST` already means every Ready item was served first | Order alone is true within a tick and false across ticks: three sweeps a tick on a two-minute clock against sessions that run for minutes would hold up to fifteen of eighteen seats by the fifth idle tick, and the tick that then has Ready work finds no seat. The `queued` read is what makes "idle" mean *idle* rather than merely *last*: a build the WIP cap holds, or one waiting on an overlapping footprint, is work that wants this seat |
| D9 | **The histogram is built from the cards' `created:` dates**, not from the wait ledger's parked spells | Fold it off `report()`'s current `parked` waits, which are already in hand | P4: the ledger is a day old, so it would report all 572 as parked for hours, and it follows Tasks and Bugs only — so 348 of the 572 are invisible to it and every parked Feature is. `created:` is on 100 % of them (P1). It measures the card's age rather than its park's, which is the honest thing to call it and the number the sweep itself orders by |
| D10 | **The histogram is computed every tick, carried on `Plan`, saved with the plan**, and rendered by `asf kernel waits` — on or off | Compute it inside `report()` by reading the record there; or only when the sweep is on | `report()` is handed a ledger and a plan and reads neither the record nor the network (P9); giving it a record read would put a card-tree walk in the status line's path. `decide` already holds the items and the parked set, and the plan file is two minutes old at worst. Computing it with the sweep off matters because the histogram is the evidence an operator turns the sweep *on* with |
| D11 | **`kernel.groom.sweep_min_age_d` defaults to 7** | 30, the shape the trigger's "September" suggests; or no floor at all | Nothing on this record is 30 days old (P1), so 30 sweeps nothing and the card ships inert. No floor at all would spend a session on a card intake parked yesterday — 297 of the 572 are under a week old. 7 days leaves 63 parks and 5 New cards eligible today, which drains in under an hour and leaves a steady state of a few a day |
| D12 | Stories S-122855–S-122860 from this session's block `S:122855-122904` | — | The record holds no Story for F-0337 (P11) |

## The design

One new module and nine files it touches — the footprint the plan's `writes:` must carry, because a
Task that writes a path absent from `writes:` is held:

```
asf/kernel/sweep.py            §1   the lane: candidates, seats, verdict, brief lines, histogram (new file)
asf/kernel/decide.py           §2   the candidates, the seat rule, READ_ONLY, the histogram on the plan
asf/kernel/apply.py            §3   the launch mark, and the verdict applied
asf/kernel/ports.py            §4   kernel_swept, unpark, retire, the verdict block off the log
asf/kernel/model.py            §5   Item.swept, the five Config fields
asf/kernel/settings.py         §5   the kernel.groom section and the model row
asf/kernel/briefs.py           §6   the sweep brief
asf/kernel/waits.py            §7   the parked-age table in the view
asf/kernel/loop.py             §7   the histogram saved with the plan
asf/workers/lifecycle.py       §8   the kind that never lands
tests/kernel/sweep fixtures    §9   fakes.py (the two writes, the mark) and test_sweep.py (new file)
```

Nothing is deleted and no shipped behaviour moves: `dor.py` is not edited at all, `supersede`,
`fill_card`, `widen_writes` and `mint_story` keep their bodies byte for byte, and every existing
number in the `asf kernel waits` view is unchanged.

### 1. `asf/kernel/sweep.py` — the lane

Pure, importing only `datetime`, `re`, `json` and `asf.kernel.model` (so `decide` may import it).

#### 1.1 What it is

```python
#: the session kind that judges an old New or Parked card
KIND = 'groom-sweep'

#: the prefix of the plan note a swept card carries this tick
PREFIX = 'sweep: '

#: the verdicts a groom-sweep session may return
VERDICTS = ('retire', 'keep', 'revive')

#: the line its verdict block opens with
HEAD = 'GROOM-SWEEP'

#: the keys that block carries
KEYS = ('verdict', 'superseded_by', 'reason')

#: the block a groom-sweep session ends with (its brief quotes it)
VERDICT_SCHEMA = """GROOM-SWEEP
verdict: retire | keep | revive
superseded_by: <id or sha>   # retire only, and only when one card or commit covers it
reason: <one line>"""

#: the queue entries that mean other work wants this seat (``_launches``' own words)
HOLDS = ('seat', 'wip', 'overlap')

#: the age buckets of the parked histogram, in days: ``(from, to or None)``
AGE_BUCKETS = ((0, 7), (7, 14), (14, 30), (30, 90), (90, None))
```

`class Verdict` holds `verdict`, `superseded_by` and `reason`, as `dor.Verdict` does.

#### 1.2 The verdict, parsed

`parse_verdict(text) -> (Verdict, '')` or `(None, why)`. It reuses `dor.block` for the key/value
read — that function's only tie to the DoR is two module constants, so it grows two defaulted
parameters and keeps its body:

```python
def block(text, head=HEAD, keys=KEYS):   # asf/kernel/dor.py, the only edit to that module
```

`dor.parse_verdict` calls `block(text)` and is unchanged. The sweep's own rules:

* no `GROOM-SWEEP` block ⇒ `(None, 'no GROOM-SWEEP block')`;
* `verdict` not in `VERDICTS` ⇒ rejected, naming the three;
* `reason` empty ⇒ `(None, 'reason: missing')`;
* `superseded_by` present and neither an id (`[A-Za-z]+-\d+`) nor a 7–40 hex sha ⇒ rejected
  (D7) — absent, or one of `none`/`n/a`/`-`/`—`, is an absence and `retire` stands;
* `superseded_by` on a `keep` or a `revive` ⇒ rejected: the fields must agree with the verdict.

Nothing of a rejected block is applied.

#### 1.3 The candidates, and their order

```python
def park_owner(items, iid):
    """The card the park on ``iid`` is written on — itself, or the nearest ancestor through
    ``parent`` carrying ``priority: later`` — or None when it is not parked."""

def age_days(created, now):
    """Whole days from ``created`` (a date) to ``now`` (ISO-8601 UTC), or None when either is
    unreadable."""

def order_key(items, iid, half):
    """``(half, created or '~', id)`` — the New half first, oldest first, undated last
    (``asf.kernel.intake``'s own idiom)."""

def candidates(items, states, parked, config, now, unready=()):
    """The ids this tick may sweep, in order. Empty when ``config.groom_sweep`` is off or ``now``
    is unreadable. A candidate is a card that
      - has no ``swept`` stamp, and
      - is at least ``config.groom_sweep_min_age_d`` days old by its ``created``, and
      - is either (the New half) a Task or Bug whose judged state is ``NEW``, not parked, and not
        in ``unready`` — the cards Stage 3 holds and will fill this tick (D4) — or
        (the Parked half) a card whose judged state is ``PARKED`` and whose ``park_owner`` is
        itself (D2).
    A Done or retired card is in neither half; a card with no ``created`` is never swept."""
```

A card with a live sweep session is absent by construction: its stamp was written when that session
launched (§3).

#### 1.4 The seat rule

```python
def idle_seat(free, queued):
    """Whether a sweep may take a seat: one is free and nothing else is waiting for one — no
    ``'seat'``, ``'wip'`` or ``'overlap'`` entry in the tick's ``queued`` (:data:`HOLDS`). A
    ``'risk'`` entry is not a wait for a seat."""
    return free > 0 and not any(v in HOLDS for v in (queued or {}).values())
```

#### 1.5 The brief's context

```python
def under(items, iid, parked):
    """The parked cards below ``iid`` through ``parent`` whose park is ``iid``'s — what a retire
    leaves parked and a revive makes visible again (ids, sorted)."""

def brief_lines(items, iid, now, children=()):
    """The lines the launch carries into the brief: what state the card is in and why, its age in
    days, the park it carries and how many cards hang under it (``children``, from :func:`under`;
    the ids are capped and counted), and — for the New half — the ``after:`` edges and the parent
    that hold it."""
```

For a parked Feature holding eight Tasks this reads, in the brief's own section:

```
- parked by its own `priority: later`, 17 days old
- 8 cards are parked under it: T-0093, T-0096, T-0101, T-0104, T-0107, T-0110, T-0113, T-0119
- retiring this card leaves them parked and invisible; reviving it makes all 8 visible again
```

#### 1.6 The histogram

```python
def histogram(items, parked, now):
    """The parked-age histogram (``Plan.parked_ages``): every parked card that is not Done,
    bucketed by its own age (:data:`AGE_BUCKETS`) —
      {'at': now, 'parked': n, 'parks': n, 'swept': n, 'undated': n,
       'buckets': [{'label': '7-13d', 'from': 7, 'to': 14, 'cards': n, 'parks': n, 'swept': n,
                    'oldest': 'F-0014', 'oldest_days': 19}, …]}
    ``parks`` counts the cards that carry the park themselves — the ones the sweep can act on;
    ``swept`` those already judged. Built whether or not the sweep is on (D10)."""
```


`EndSession` routes the ended session to `self.groom_sweep(s)` beside the `groom_fill` arm, and:

```python
def groom_sweep(self, s):
    """Apply an ended groom-sweep session's verdict (:func:`asf.kernel.sweep.parse_verdict`):
    ``retire`` retires the card — through ``supersede`` when it named a successor, else through
    ``retire`` — ``revive`` deletes its ``priority:`` so it and everything under it are visible
    again, and ``keep`` writes nothing. A rejected block, a verdict on a card that has since
    started, and a write the record refused are each one note on the card. The stamp was written
    when the session launched, so nothing here re-arms it."""
```

In order: parse; on `(None, why)` note `'%s: groom-sweep verdict rejected: %s'`; else check the
card is still a card the sweep may act on — the plan's own judgement this tick is `NEW` or
`PARKED` — and drop the verdict with a note naming the state when it is not (a card that started
while the session judged); else apply, each inside `try/except` with the refusal as the note, the
card unchanged:

| verdict | write |
|---|---|
| `retire` with `superseded_by` | `ports.record.supersede(iid, by, reason)` — the shipped call |
| `retire` without | `ports.record.retire(iid, reason)` |
| `revive` | `ports.record.unpark(iid, reason)`; refused with a note when the card carries no `priority: later` of its own (D5) |
| `keep` | nothing — the stamp alone |

and one log line per verdict, in `groom_fill`'s shape.

# Groom-sweep: {item_id} — {title}

{opening}

You only read and judge — never edit, commit or push anything, and run no `asf` command.

{context}

{body}

End with exactly this block, and nothing after it:

```
{schema}
```

|---|---|---|

## Records

No card is minted, no Story is derived, no event is emitted, no metrics row is appended, no branch
is pushed and no PR is opened. The sweep writes three kinds of character, two of them onto cards
and one into the operator's state directory.

| What | Where | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| the sweep stamp | the card's machine block, `kernel_swept`, through `write_fields` | **New key.** One ISO-8601 UTC stamp, written when the card's one sweep launches, never rewritten, carrying no kernel version (D6). `KERNEL_KEYS` holds it, so `asf kernel status` and `card_fields` read it like any other |
| a retirement | the card's typed block, `removed: "retired: <reason>"`, plus one `## History` line, through `set_typed` | **No new shape**: the words `asf retire --why` writes (`retire.removal`). With a successor it is the shipped `supersede` instead — `removed:` + `superseded_by:` on the card and `supersedes:` on the successor, both halves, unchanged |
| an un-park | the card's typed block: `priority:` **deleted**, plus one `## History` line | **New write, old mechanism** (`write_typed` deletes a key whose value is None, P5). Nothing else on the card moves — not `rank`, not `parent`, not the machine block — so the next tick judges it as it judges any New card |
| the parked cards under a retired or revived one | — | **Untouched.** A retired card is forced `priority: later` by `items()`, so its descendants stay parked and invisible; a revived one takes its whole subtree out of the parked set in the same walk (P5) |
| a refusal, a rejected block, a verdict on a card that started | the card's `kernel_notes`, through the applier's existing `_note` | **No new shape.** One capped line each, shown by `asf kernel status`, holding nothing |
| the plan note | `Plan.notes`, in the saved plan | `sweep: <verdict asked>` for each card swept this tick. **Never written to a card** |
| the parked-age histogram | `Plan.parked_ages`, saved in `kernel-plan.json`, printed by `asf kernel waits` | **New field, new section of an existing view.** Derived every tick from the items the tick already read; no file of its own, no ledger, no retention |

### S-122855: the sweep's candidates are the old New cards and the cards that carry a park, oldest first

Design §1.3 (D2, D3, D4, D11; P1, P2, P3). The two halves, the floor, the order, and the four
kinds of card that are never swept.

- [ ] a card that carries priority later itself is a candidate, and the cards parked under it through parent are not
- [ ] a New Task or Bug that no lane launched is a candidate, and the New half sorts ahead of every parked candidate
- [ ] inside each half the oldest card by its created date comes first, and a card with no created date is never swept
- [ ] a card younger than the configured minimum age in days is not a candidate, and the default minimum is seven days
- [ ] a Done card, a retired card, and a card that already carries a sweep stamp are each no candidate
- [ ] a New card the Definition of Ready holds and will fill this tick is no candidate, where the same card is one on a tick that fills it not
- [ ] a card whose park is on an ancestor is no candidate even when it is the oldest card on the record
- [ ] with the sweep turned off there is no candidate at all, and with the tick's clock unreadable there is none either
- [ ] the candidate order is read off the judged state, so a card judged parked and a card judged New are never interleaved

### S-122856: a sweep takes only a seat no other class is waiting for, within a tick and across ticks

Design §1.4 and §2 (D8; P6). The property the card names in its own words: the sweep never
pre-empts Ready work.

- [ ] a tick with a Ready item that did not launch for want of a seat launches no sweep
- [ ] a tick whose only waiting work is a build the WIP cap holds back launches no sweep, even with seats free
- [ ] a tick whose only waiting work is a Ready item held for an overlapping footprint launches no sweep
- [ ] a tick with an item held back for risk alone still launches a sweep, because a risk hold is not a wait for a seat
- [ ] with nothing waiting, the number of sweeps launched is the configured budget a tick and no more
- [ ] live sweep sessions from earlier ticks count, so the sessions held at once never exceed the configured concurrency
- [ ] every fix round, review, build, plan, spec and groom-fill of the tick is launched before the first sweep
- [ ] a sweep never consumes the seat of a launch of any other class in the same tick, whatever the launch order within that class
- [ ] a live sweep session leaves its card's judged state exactly as it was, and never makes it Building or Review

### S-122857: the groom-sweep verdict block is validated whole, and a field that disagrees with the verdict rejects it

Design §1.2 (D5, D7). The grammar, read off the whole session log, with nothing of a rejected
block applied.

- [ ] a retire naming a card id parses, and so does one naming a seven to forty character commit sha
- [ ] a retire naming no successor at all parses, and the reason alone carries it
- [ ] a retire whose successor is neither an id nor a sha is rejected whole, and the rejection says which field
- [ ] a keep or a revive that also names a successor is rejected, because the field disagrees with the verdict
- [ ] a verdict word outside the three is rejected, and the message names the three
- [ ] a block with no reason is rejected
- [ ] a report with no verdict block at all is rejected, and the message says so
- [ ] the block is found when it is not the last thing the session printed, as the groom-fill's is
- [ ] the Definition of Ready's own verdict parser is unchanged by the shared block reader growing its two parameters

### S-122858: code applies retire, keep and revive to the record, and a refused write leaves the card as it was

Design §3 and §4 (D2, D5, D7; P5). The writes, over a scratch record, and every refusal path.

- [ ] a retire with a successor writes the removal and the successor's own matching entry, through the shipped supersede call
- [ ] a retire with no successor writes a removal whose words are the ones the hand retire command writes
- [ ] a retired parked card leaves its descendants parked and invisible, and no descendant card is written
- [ ] a revive deletes the card's priority field and nothing else on the card, and the card still parses
- [ ] after a revive the card and every card parked under it are out of the parked set in the next tick's walk
- [ ] a revive of a card that carries no priority later of its own is refused, and the note names the card the park is on
- [ ] a keep writes nothing at all beyond the sweep stamp, and the card is byte-identical
- [ ] a write the record refuses leaves the card unchanged and puts one note on it saying why
- [ ] a verdict on a card the tick no longer judges New or parked is dropped with a note naming the state it found
- [ ] each applied verdict is one log line naming the card, the verdict and the reason

### S-122859: a card is swept once, ever, whatever the session did and whatever version runs

Design §3 (D6; P3). The stamp is spent at launch, which is the only version with a steady state.

- [ ] the sweep stamp is written when the session launches, and the card's state is not written at all
- [ ] a card that carries a stamp is never launched again, whatever verdict the first session returned
- [ ] a session that crashed, was stopped over age, or returned an unreadable block still leaves the card stamped
- [ ] a keep verdict reaches a steady state: the second tick over the same record launches no sweep at all
- [ ] the stamp is read back from the card under a kernel version other than the one that wrote it, unlike the fill count
- [ ] a sweep and a build for the same card never cancel each other out of the launch
- [ ] clearing the stamp by hand makes the card a candidate again, and nothing else does

### S-122860: asf kernel waits reports the parked-age histogram, built from the cards and not from the ledger

Design §1.6 and §7 (D9, D10; P4, P9). The number that makes the pile visible, and the one table
that reports it.

- [ ] the histogram buckets every parked card that is not Done by its own age, and counts the cards, the parks and the swept in each bucket
- [ ] a parked Feature and a parked Story are both counted, where the wait ledger follows only Tasks and Bugs
- [ ] each bucket names its oldest card and that card's age in days, and an empty bucket is left out
- [ ] the histogram counts cards with no created date apart, and never silently drops them
- [ ] the histogram is built on a tick with the sweep turned off, exactly as on one with it on
- [ ] the saved plan carries the histogram, and asf kernel waits prints it from there with no record read
- [ ] asf kernel waits with the live flag prints the histogram decided on the facts read now
- [ ] a view with no histogram at all prints every existing table unchanged and no empty section
- [ ] not one existing number of the waits view changes: the classes, the oldest waits and the biggest wait are as they were
