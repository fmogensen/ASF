# F-0219 — 64.6 % of one workflow's runs cancelled, and the factory cancels most of them itself

Twenty-four hours of one product's `ci` workflow: **390 runs, 252 cancelled — 64.6 %**, and 209 of
the 308 `pull_request` runs. The comparison in the card is the whole argument: `dco` was cancelled
1.6 % of the time and the voice/computer workflows about 15 %. Whatever is happening is happening
to the heavy workflow, and only to it.

The queue log says what: *"cancelled in-progress pr run … main's reserved runners heavy-4b held
by a pr job … sunk 66 min"*. Sixty-six minutes of a heavy runner, thrown away, to hand a
runner back to main. Another five on a second branch. The heavy pool reads `heavy 0 free,
needs 5-7` for long stretches, the starvation guard forced 23 admissions, and runs waited from 20
minutes to 3 h 42 min. The card's suspect is PR #849, merged 09-26 15:25, which reserved two heavy
runners for main only.

The suspect is right, and the mechanism is narrower and worse than the card guesses. `ci.reserve`
is a *label* reservation: of the online runners carrying `of`, all but `keep_free` carry `label`,
and a PR run's `runs-on` asks for `label` too — so the kept-free runners can only take trunk runs.
A PR job should be unable to reach a reserved runner at all. It reaches one because
**`reserve_plan` chooses which runners to keep free without ever looking at whether a runner is
busy**, and `tick_reserve` applies that choice every tick. Three lines reproduce it (P3): a
reserved runner goes offline, the plan re-picks, and it takes the PR label off the one runner in
the class that is *mid-PR-job* — in preference to an idle one. From that instant the reservation
is broken by the factory's own write; `_broken_reserve` reports `held by a pr job`; the relief
escalates at once with no wait for `trunk_escalate_min`; and the PR run is cancelled with its
sunk minutes gone.

So the factory creates the condition, detects the condition, and pays for the repair out of PR
minutes. The card's three wants map onto that loop exactly, and this spec keeps them in the card's
own order:

1. **Measure it.** Today every relief cancel writes the same claim — `cause: 'relief'` — whether
   it was a trunk starved of runners or a reservation the factory broke a tick ago, and it records
   no minutes at all. The two are not the same event and must never again be one number.
2. **Stop admitting runs that will be cancelled.** Two gates, one rule: *a PR job and main's
   reservation must not want the same runner at the same time.* Check it wherever the factory can
   still choose — when the plan picks which runner to keep free, and when the queue admits a start
   whose run can reach a reserved runner.
3. **Say whether the reservation is worth it**, and what size it should be.

### The choices

| # | Chosen | Against | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **The primary fix is the reserve plan, not admission.** `reserve_plan` prefers to keep free a runner that is *not* holding a PR job; only when it must take a held one does it take the one whose job is expected to finish soonest. | Reading the card's want 2 literally and only gating admission. | P1–P3: the plan is where the factory *creates* the condition, it creates it every tick (P2), and it needs no stale `runs-on`, no estimate and no network to stop creating it — `reserve_plan` is a pure function and the fix is one term in its sort key. Admission cannot repair a label the tick took off a runner five minutes ago, because the job is already running. Gating admission alone would leave the incident in P3 firing untouched. The card's sentence is still honoured, twice: the plan asks it of a job already running, admission asks it of a job about to start. |
| C2 | **A PR-held runner is *deprioritised*, never counted as offline.** A new `held={runner: seconds until its PR job is expected to be done}` argument to `reserve_plan`, used as the second term of the pick key — after `prefer`, before the box spread. | Passing PR-held runners through the existing `unavailable` argument. | `unavailable` means "counts as offline — it keeps nothing free for the trunk, so another runner is kept free in its place". That is right for a phantom and wrong here: a runner finishing a PR job in four minutes is a perfectly good reserved runner five minutes from now, and treating it as offline would inflate `keep_free` above the pool and make the plan keep *more* runners free the busier the pool got — the starvation the card already measures. Deprioritising ranks the same runners; it never changes how many are kept. |
| C3 | **`held` outranks the box spread.** The key becomes `(prefer missing, seconds until free, box already used, already carries the label, −candidates in the box, name last-first)`. | Keeping `spread_by: box` above it, so the spread is never weakened. | The spread exists so that one box dying does not take every reserved runner with it — a hedge against a future loss. A held runner keeps *nothing* free from the moment the label comes off until its job ends, which is a present loss of exactly the capacity the reservation is for, and (P4) it triggers an instant relief escalation that cancels the job anyway. An idle runner on a box that already has one beats a reserved runner that is not reserved yet. Recorded because a reviewer will look for the spread first. |
| C4 | **`held` is computed by the caller from what the tick already reads, and an unreadable answer is `{}`.** `ci_pool.held_by_pr(runners, busy, jobs_of, hold_s, now)`, called from `tick_reserve` and `cmd_reserve`; `{}` on any unreadable part, which restores today's behaviour exactly. | Having `reserve_plan` itself go to the host. | `reserve_plan` is pure and idempotent and its docstring says so — "the same runners give the same plan". That property is what makes the plan safe to apply every tick, and it is worth more than the convenience. A caller that cannot read the busy map degrades to the plan we have today, which is the only degradation that cannot make things worse. |
| C5 | **Admission's gate is a third gate beside the ceiling and the runner fit, and it applies to one population: a PR start whose run can reach a reserved runner.** `reserve_fit(...)`, pure, called from `Queue.admit` only when `self.reserves` is non-empty. | Gating every PR start; or gating none and relying on C1. | P12–P13: reachability is decidable — a job whose `runs-on` carries the reservation's `of` without its `label` can land on a kept-free runner, and that is precisely `_broken_reserve`'s `held by a pr job` for a run whose definition predates the reservation. Gating every PR start would hold runs that *cannot* reach a reserved runner, which is most of them, on a measure they do not need. P16: for a product with no `ci.reserve` the whole gate is unreachable and costs nothing. |
| C6 | **The gate's two numbers are measured, not configured: the p90 *hold* of the reserved class, and the trunk's measured gap.** The hold is a new figure in the existing `expect` cache (`EXPECT_VERSION` 5 → 6); the gap is written into the queue file by the pass that already lists the trunk's `push` runs, and read at admission. | A `ci.queue.pr_max_min` or `ci.queue.trunk_gap_min` knob. | P14–P15: both come free from reads the code already makes, and a measured figure cannot go stale against a workflow that got slower. The decisive argument is cost: `Queue.admit` is called per start, per pass, by the lane, and it must not grow a `gh run list`. Writing the gap from the relief pass — which already has `pushes` sorted by `createdAt` in hand — keeps the admission path at the one runner read it makes today. |
| C7 | **One new `ci.queue` key and no more: `reserve_fit: on` (default on).** | A knob per number (the gap, the hold, the safety margin); or no switch at all. | The gate refuses starts. A gate that refuses starts and cannot be turned off in one edit is a gate an operator cannot get out from under at 02:00, and `mode: off` is too big a hammer — it would drop the ceiling, the fit, the relief and the re-runs with it. One boolean is the smallest thing that is honest. Every number under it is measured (C6), so there is nothing else to tune. |
| C8 | **A hold under the gate names its own cure and the head guard is left as its backstop.** The hold line names the reservation, the seconds, and the rebase that clears it; `head_wait_max_min` still admits the head of the line whatever is free. | A hold that can never clear; or exempting the gate from the head guard. | A run whose `runs-on` predates the reservation has a head that will never change on its own, so a gate with no escape would park such a branch for ever and the operator would read it as the queue being broken. The cure is a rebase — which the lane performs in its ordinary course, and which replaces the run's definition with the trunk's. Leaving the head guard as the backstop means the worst case is today's behaviour (the run starts, and may be cancelled) rather than a branch that never runs again. Recorded loudly: **this gate reduces the waste, it does not promise zero.** |
| C9 | **The relief claim gains two fields — `reason`, one of four closed names, and `sunk_s` — and its `cause` stays `'relief'`.** `RELIEF_REASONS = ('reserved', 'escalated', 'starved', 'waited')`. | Splitting the cause itself into `relief-reserved` / `relief-starved`. | P19: F-0230 is specced and in plan with `relief` as one of nine closed cause names and a table built on that tuple. Renaming the cause would put this card's Task and that Feature's Task on the same tuple at the same time. A second, orthogonal field is additive: F-0230's table keeps adding to 100 % with no edit, and can split its `relief` row on `reason` later if it wants to. P6 shows the four names are already the four branches of the code, so the field is a label on a distinction the code already makes — not a new judgement. |
| C10 | **`sunk_s` on a claim is every class, and the relief's own class-filtered `sunk` stays what it is.** `_sunk_s` takes `classes=None` to mean every class; the claim passes `None`, the ordering key keeps the filter. | One number for both. | The class filter exists to order candidates — least sunk of *the contended class* first — and narrowing it is what makes that ordering right. "What did cancelling this run throw away" is a different question with a different answer: an operator reading a waste total wants every runner-minute the run held, not only the heavy ones. Two readers, two numbers, and the docstring says which is which. |
| C11 | **Every claim site records `sunk_s`, not only the relief's.** The `superseded`, `duplicate-push`, `stall`, `job-timeout`, `cancelled-left` and `merged-pr` claims each get it. | Recording minutes on the relief claims alone. | The card asks for the two columns side by side — "the runs cancelled because a newer push replaced them (expected) and the runs cancelled to hand main's reserved runners back (waste), **with the sunk minutes for each**". A waste column with no expected column beside it is a number with no scale. P11 bounds the cost: one job read per newly cancelled run, once, because the claim makes the run skip afterwards. |
| C12 | **The split is printed by `asf ci queue`, from the claim ledger, with no `gh` call.** `cancel_totals(product, now=None)`, pure over `load_claims`. | A new `asf ci cancels`-shaped table; a `doctor` row; a scorecard cause. | P19: `asf ci cancels` is F-0230's, by name, and building a second table over the same ledger would be two commands answering one question. `asf ci queue` is where an operator already goes to ask why nothing is starting, and the ledger is already on disk beside the queue file — so the block is a read of a file the command's product already owns. A `doctor` row is defensible next and is out (Out (e)): a row that goes red on a number nobody has read once gets tuned by guesswork. |
| C13 | **The verdict and the suggestion are `asf ci reserve --measure`, not a new command.** | A new `asf ci reserve-measure`; a row in `asf ci queue`; a metrics stream event. | P17: the flag is one `add_argument` and one branch in `cmd_reserve`, with the title, the product argument and the exit codes already there. The question — *is this reservation worth its price, and what size should it be* — is a question about `ci.reserve`, asked by the operator who is about to edit `keep_free`, and it belongs beside the plan that applies it. |
| C14 | **The suggestion is a stated arithmetic, printed with its inputs, and it is never applied.** Five steps over measured figures (§5); `--measure` writes nothing and changes no label. | A `--suggest --apply` that edits the product file; or a heuristic whose derivation is not printed. | The number is advice about a capacity trade the operator owns, derived from a window they choose, over a claim ledger whose sunk half is only two days deep (P9). Printing the inputs beside the answer is what lets them disagree with it. And a command that rewrites `ci.reserve` from a seven-day average would be a command that can starve the trunk or the whole PR side on one quiet week. |
| C15 | **What the measure cannot know, it says.** The verdict prints main's measured wait against the PR minutes measured sunk, and then the sentence that it is **not** a counterfactual: it does not know what main would have waited with no reservation. The sunk half also prints the window it actually covers when `--days` exceeds the ledger's TTL. | Presenting the difference as "the reservation saved/wasted N minutes". | The card asks "whether #849's reservation saves or wastes time overall", and the honest answer from data taken *after* #849 is a comparison of two measured costs, not a measurement of a road not taken. ### §1 `asf/ci_pool.py` — the plan stops keeping a busy runner free

`Reserve`, `ReservePlan.candidates/labeled/reserved/add/remove` and `apply_reserve`'s
adds-before-removes order are unchanged. One argument and one term in one sort key:

```python
def reserve_plan(res, pool, runners, unavailable=(), held=None):
    """...  A runner in ``held`` is running a PR job: it keeps nothing free for the trunk until
    that job ends, and taking the label off it now breaks the reservation the moment it is
    applied (:func:`asf.ci_queue._broken_reserve` reads that as ``held by a pr job``, and the
    relief then cancels the job to repair it). ``held`` maps such a runner to the seconds until
    its job is expected to be done, so an idle runner is kept free before a held one, and of two
    held ones the one finishing soonest. Unlike ``unavailable`` a held runner is still counted
    and still eligible: the number kept free never changes.

    The runners kept free are chosen, one at a time, by: carrying ``prefer`` (when set) first;
    then the fewest seconds until the runner is free (0 when idle); then a box not yet holding a
    kept-free runner (``spread_by: box``); then one already without the label (no churn); then a
    box with more candidates; then the name, last first. ..."""
    ...
    wait = {n: s for n, s in (held or {}).items()}
    ...
        pick = min(left, key=lambda r: (bool(res.prefer) and res.prefer not in r.norm_labels(),
                                        wait.get(r.name, 0),
                                        used.get(box_of(r), 0), res.label in r.norm_labels(),
```

The plan stays pure and stays idempotent for a fixed `(runners, held)`: `held` is data in, like

### §2 `asf/ci_pool.py` — who is holding a PR job, and for how much longer

```python
def held_by_pr(runners, busy, jobs_of, hold_s, now):
    """``{runner: seconds until its PR job is expected to be done}`` for each online runner
    running a job of a ``pull_request`` run: ``hold_s(workflow)`` is the measured p90 seconds a
    run of that workflow holds a runner (:meth:`asf.ci_queue.Queue.hold_s`), the remainder is
    that less the job's elapsed, floored at 0 — a job already past the p90 is expected to end
    now. ``busy`` is :meth:`asf.ci_queue.Source.busy_runners` (``{runner: {run, job, repo}}``)
    and ``jobs_of(run_id)`` its jobs. ``{}`` when ``busy`` is None, and a runner whose job or
    figure cannot be read is left out — the plan then ranks it as it does today. Pure; never
    raises."""
```

`reserve_plans(product, runners, pool=None, unavailable=None, held=None)` passes it through.
`tick_reserve` and `cmd_reserve` build it inside one `try`, from a `ci_queue.Queue` over the same

```python
def _held(product, runners):
    """``held`` for :func:`reserve_plans`, from the CI host; ``{}`` when anything is unreadable
    (the plan then ranks as it did before this figure existed)."""
    try:
        from asf import ci_queue
        q = ci_queue.Queue(product, write=False)
        src = q.source
        return held_by_pr(runners, src.busy_runners(), src.live_jobs,
                          lambda wf: q.hold_s(wf), q.now)
    except Exception:       # noqa: BLE001 — an unreadable host never changes the plan
        return {}
```

### §6 `asf ci reserve --measure` — the verdict, and a size

```python
def reserve_measure(product, days=7, source=None, now=None):
    """What ``ci.reserve`` cost and bought over the last ``days``, per reservation. Reads the
    host's finished runs of the trunk and PR workflows in the window and their jobs' start
    times, and the claim ledger (:func:`cancel_totals`) for the minutes sunk to it. Writes
    nothing. ``None`` for a figure the host would not give."""
```

| row | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| `main waited` | trunk `push` runs of the trunk workflow finished in the window: how many, the median and p90 of `created_at` → the run's first job's `started_at`, and the total |
| `PR waited` | `pull_request` runs of the PR workflow, the same three figures |
| `PR sunk — reserved runners handed back` | `relief:reserved` from the ledger: runs and minutes — **the waste** |
| `PR sunk — a newer push replaced it` | `superseded` from the ledger: runs and minutes — **expected** |
| `PR sunk — trunk starvation` | `relief:starved` and `relief:escalated`: the minutes the queue trades deliberately |
| `trunk demand` | per reservation, the p90 over the window's trunk runs of the required jobs of the reserved class the run had queued at once (`run_peaks` over `_required_names`) |

Then the verdict and the suggestion, each printing its inputs:

```
== CI RESERVE <product> (MEASURE — 7 days, nothing changed)
main waited                              64 runs   median 3 min   p90 21 min   total 412 min
PR waited                               308 runs   median 9 min   p90 71 min   total 4 118 min
PR sunk — reserved runners handed back    9 runs                                total 814 min  WASTE
PR sunk — a newer push replaced it       18 runs                                total  64 min  expected
PR sunk — trunk starvation               27 runs                                total 239 min
trunk demand (heavy, required jobs)      p90 3

verdict: ci.reserve keeps 2 heavy runners for main. Over 7 days main waited 412 min in total
  and PR runs sunk 814 min having those runners handed back — the reservation's measured cost is
  twice the wait it is there to remove. This is not a counterfactual: it does not say what main
  would have waited with no reservation.
suggest: ci.reserve.keep_free: 1 (now 2) — the cost above exceeds main's whole wait, so one
  fewer; main's p90 wait 21 min is above ci.queue.trunk_wait_min (20), so not zero; capped at
  trunk demand p90 3 and at 11 (the heavy class holds 12).
sunk minutes cover the last 2 days only (the claim ledger's TTL); the waits cover all 7.
```

The suggestion is five stated steps over the rows above:

1. `base = min(trunk demand p90, class size − 1)` — never reserve a whole class, and never more
   than main has been measured to ask for at once.
2. when `reserved` sunk minutes exceed main's total wait: `min(base, keep_free − 1)`, floored at
   0 — the reservation costs more than the wait it removes.
3. else when main's p90 wait exceeds `ci.queue.trunk_wait_min`: `min(base, keep_free + 1)` —
   main still waits past the bar the relief itself uses.
4. else `keep_free` — it holds.
5. every step names its inputs in the printed line, and a step whose input is `None` is skipped
   and said to be skipped.

* **The tick's `ci reserve:` lines.** Unchanged in shape. A tick that would have taken the label

## Records

* **`<state>/<product>/ci-cancels.json`** — every claim gains `sunk_s` (whole seconds, every
  class); a `relief` claim gains `reason`, one of `RELIEF_REASONS`. The `cause` values, the key
  (the run id as a string), the `at` stamp and `CLAIM_TTL_S` are unchanged, so a claim written
  before this change stays readable and simply has no `sunk_s` — `cancel_totals` counts its run
  and adds no minutes for it.
* **`<state>/<product>/ci-queue.json`** — each `relief` record gains `reason` and `sunk_s`; a new
  top-level `trunk` (`{at, newest, queued, gap_s}`) holds the trunk's measured cadence, written
  by the relief pass and read by admission; `expect.<workflow>.per_run.<id>` gains `hold`; and
  `expect.<workflow>.v` becomes 6, which re-measures each workflow once on the first pass after
  the upgrade. `prune()` is unchanged.
* **The CI host** — the labels `apply_reserve` writes. The same number of writes, on different
  runners: a runner holding a PR job keeps its PR label and an idle one gives its up.
* **Nothing else.** No metrics event, no backlog card, no History line, no `doctor` row, no
  scorecard row, no new file. `asf ci queue` and `asf ci reserve --measure` are stdout.
* **Events on stdout** — the tick's `ci reserve:` line may name a held runner; the relief's lines
  are unchanged; `asf ci queue` gains the cancels block; a held PR start gains one hold line.
