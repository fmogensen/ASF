# F-0070 — a job that will never finish is not a job that is still running

The factory watches CI closely and never once asks whether a running job is getting anywhere. It
knows a runner is busy with no job on it (`update_phantoms`, `asf/ci_queue.py:1227`). It knows a
required trunk job has been queued for forty minutes (`_starved_jobs`, `:2272`). It knows how many
minutes a run's jobs have sunk into their runners, to the second, because that is what cancelling
one throws away (`_sunk_s`, `:2303`). Every one of those reads a job's *start*. Nothing in the tree
reads what the job has done since: `grep -rn 'in_progress' asf/` finds twelve call sites and not one
of them compares a step's clock to now.

So a job that hangs inside a step is, to this factory, a job that is running. It holds its runner,
it holds its reserved slot, it holds the PR's verdict and the branch behind it, and the queue keeps
counting it as capacity in use — until something outside asf ends it. That something is the job's
budget: `timeout-minutes` in the workflow, or the provider's own platform ceiling when the workflow
declares none, which is what this product's own workflow does (`.github/workflows/tests.yml` has no
`timeout-minutes` at all, so its ceiling is the provider's default, six hours). A job that wedges
in its second step is therefore paid for at the full price of the last step it will never reach.
The card's sentence — "burns the whole budget first" — is that, exactly.

The fix is not a shorter budget. A shorter budget cuts the honest long job as hard as the hung one,
and the two are indistinguishable by duration: that is why the budget is set where it is. What
separates them is *movement*, and the provider already publishes the movement — every job's steps
come back from the same jobs endpoint the queue reads twice a pass, each with its own
`started_at` and `completed_at`. This card reads them. A job whose newest started step began more
than ten minutes ago, with no step completed since, is not running: it is stuck inside one named
step, and it is stopped there instead of at its budget hours later.

This is the CI half of F-0066, which asked the same question of a worker session — "silence is one
way a session dies; standing still is the other" — and answered it by stamping a clock the log did
not carry. Here the clock is already in the record; only the reading is missing.

## 1. Decisions

### 1.1 In

- **A pure judge, `ci_queue.step_silence(job, now, limit_s)`** (§2.2). Over one job's `steps`:
  the newest step with a `started_at`, its age, and whether any step completed at or after that
  start. Returns `(class, step name, since)` — `('step-silence', 'unit tests', <the step's start>)`
  past the limit, `('moving', …)` under it, `('unknown', None, None)` when the job carries no
  readable step. No clock of its own, no host, no configuration: `now` and `limit_s` are arguments.
  The card's rule, one function, and the only place it is written down.
- **The steps travel on the read the pass already makes** (§2.1). `GitHubSource._repo_jobs`'s `jq`
  gains `steps: [.steps[] | {name, status, started_at, completed_at}]`. That call is the one the
  phantom watch makes for every non-completed run of every repo the runners serve
  (`:849-891`), and its results for the product's own repo are already memoised on the instance
  (`self._live`). Reading the steps costs no extra request in the pass that matters.
- **`Source.live_runs()`, and the status listings memoised per pass** (§2.1). One new reader on the
  seam: the product's own non-completed runs, `[{id, status, headBranch, headSha, createdAt}]`,
  or None when unreadable. `GitHubSource` implements it from the two `_runs_by_status` calls
  `busy_runners` already makes, memoised on the instance by `(repo, status)` so a pass that asks
  both questions pays for one listing. The base class returns None: a provider whose backend cannot
  list runs raises nothing.
- **A watch across passes, beside the phantom watch** (§2.3). `update_stalls(q, src)` — for every
  in-progress job of every live run of the product, judge it, and keep one record per
  `<run>:<job>` under a new `stalled` key in the queue file: the step's own `since`, the step name,
  the branch and sha, `last`, and `passes`. Confirmed on the `STALL_PASSES = 2`nd consecutive pass
  (`STALL_GAP_S = 10 * 60`, the phantom watch's rule: passes further apart than that are not
  consecutive). A job whose newest step changed, or whose run left the listing, leaves the watch.
  An unreadable read learns nothing and forgets nothing.
- **The confirming pass cancels the run** (§2.4). One `gh run cancel` — the provider has no
  per-job cancel, so the job is stopped by cancelling the run it is in, which is how every other
  cancel in this module works. `mode: on` cancels; `mode: dry-run` prints `would cancel` and writes
  nothing; `mode: off` makes no call. That mode gate *is* the fix mode the card names: it is the
  one switch under which this module already writes to the host.
- **The cancel is remembered and re-run, like every other cancel here** (§2.4, D5). The run's
  record joins the queue file's `relief` list carrying `stall: 'step-silence'` and `stall_step`,
  so the machinery that exists takes it from there: `rerun_ids` keeps the lane from reading the
  cancelled checks as a verdict on the branch (`:1186`), `_rerun_cancelled` re-runs it through the
  line at its own priority, the workflow-change guard still refuses a re-run on a stale workflow,
  and `RELIEF_TTL_S` still drops a record that could not be re-run in a day. That is the rule the
  card asks for — *a cancel is never a verdict, and every cancel is re-run* — and it is the rule a
  run cut short by its budget already gets (`RERUN_CONCLUSIONS` carries `timed_out`, `:2696`).
- **Twice on one sha, then the operator** (§2.4, D8). `started(rec)` treats a record marked
  `stall` as ready at once — nothing protects it. The record counts `stalls` per `(branch, sha)`;
  at `MAX_STALL_RERUNS = 2` the third stall is cancelled and **not** re-run: one loud line naming
  the branch, the step and the count, and the doctor row goes red. A step that wedges every time is
  a defect in the step, and re-running it for a day is how a loop is built.
- **`ci.queue.step_silence_min`, default 10** (§2.5). The product file's own key, beside the five
  wait minutes it already carries, read through the same duration grammar `stage_limits.silent_min`
  uses (an int is minutes; `10m` / `1h` work). `0` or `off` turns the watch off entirely — the
  product is back to today. Declared in `FIELDS`, checked by `config_problems`, documented in
  `docs/products.example.yaml` and set in `sample/product.yaml`.
- **The step-silence rows in the doctor and in the status clause** (§2.6). `ci_queue.stall_rows`
  joins `runner_rows` under the existing `ci runners` doctor row group, off the queue file, with no
  `gh` call — red for a confirmed stall, red for a sha at the re-run cap. `phantom_lines`'s
  companion `stall_lines` joins the Capacity row's queue clause the same way. No new row name, no
  new table, no new command.
- **The class on the `ci` stream** (§2.7). One new key on the event, `stall` (`str|null`, default
  None) — `step-silence` when the queue file records that the factory cancelled this run for it,
  `budget` when the run or any of its jobs concluded `timed_out` — and one on `JOB_SCHEMA`,
  `stall_step` (`str|null`), the step the hung job was inside. `ci_from_api` joins them off the
  product's queue file by run id and job name. The card's second acceptance.
- **The two class words, `step-silence` and `budget`** (D1). `budget` is not new behaviour: it is
  the name for what the provider already does to a job that passes its `timeout-minutes`, so the
  stream can say which of the two ended a run and an operator can count them against each other.

### 1.2 Out

- **No new budget, no new timeout.** This card adds no duration ceiling of its own and changes no
  workflow. A job's budget stays `timeout-minutes` in the workflow file, enforced by the provider,
  and the `budget` class is only the *name* this factory gives what comes back. (That this repo's
  own `tests.yml` declares no `timeout-minutes` is true and is a workflow's card, not this one.)
- **The phantom watch, the relief and the dedupe are untouched.** Their rules, their lines, their
  records and their tests keep their behaviour to the letter. This card adds a watch beside them
  and a `stall` field to a record they write; it moves nothing they decide.
- **No job-level cancel.** The provider has no endpoint for one. The run is cancelled, the whole
  run is re-run, and no attempt is made to preserve the jobs that had already passed.
- **No relief exemption is inherited** (D6). A hung job on the trunk is cancelled; a hung job on a
  PR that changes CI config is cancelled; an S1's hung job is cancelled. Those exemptions exist
  because cancelling a *healthy* run throws away work that would have finished. This run will not
  finish.
- **Nothing is read between steps.** A job that completed step 3 and has not started step 4 is
  `moving` by this rule, exactly as the card words it ("no completed step since"). A runner that
  dies between two steps is the phantom watch's case and the budget's, not this one.
- **No Bug is filed, no item is held, no event kind is added.** A stall at the re-run cap is a line
  and a red doctor row. Filing a Bug for a wedged step is `asf file-bugs`' contract over the CI
  stream, which this card gives the field to do it from later.
- **No new metrics stream and no new counter.** `ticks` gains no key; `stall` and `stall_step` land
  on the two `ci` schemas that exist.
- **Nothing reads a step's log, its name pattern or its exit status.** The rule is two timestamps.
  A spec that matched step names would be a fixture owned by another repo's workflow file.
- **No cloud lane, no second provider.** `Source`'s base returns None for both new readers, and a
  product whose backend cannot list steps holds class `unknown` for ever — which raises nothing,
  cancels nothing and is the only safe answer when the evidence is absent (D4).

## 2. The design

### 2.1 The seam — `asf/ci_queue.py`, `Source` and `GitHubSource`

Two readers on the seam, both returning None rather than raising, both unimplemented on the base:

```python
class Source:
    def live_runs(self):
        """The product's own runs not completed: ``[{id, status, headBranch, headSha,
        createdAt}]``, or None when unreadable. The population the step-silence watch judges."""
        return None

    def live_jobs(self, run_id):
        """One run's jobs as they stand now (latest attempt): ``[{name, status, labels,
        runner_name, created_at, started_at, completed_at, steps}]`` — ``steps`` being
        ``[{name, status, started_at, completed_at}]`` in the provider's own order — or None
        when unreadable. A provider that publishes no steps leaves the key out."""
        return None
```

`GitHubSource` gains one line in `_repo_jobs`'s `jq` and one memo:

```python
    def _runs_by_status(self, repo, status):
        key = (repo, status)
        if key in self._by_status:          # one listing per (repo, status) per pass: the phantom
            return self._by_status[key]     # watch and the stall watch ask the same question
        ...

    def _repo_jobs(self, repo, run_id):
        text = self._gh(['api', f'repos/{repo}/actions/runs/{run_id}/jobs?per_page=100'
                                '&filter=latest',
                         '--jq', '.jobs[] | {name, status, labels, runner_name, created_at, '
                         'started_at, completed_at, steps: [.steps[]? | {name, status, '
                         'started_at, completed_at}]}'])
```

`live_runs` returns the product's own repo, both statuses, in the queue's own vocabulary
(`QUEUED_STATUSES | {'in_progress'}`), from the memo above — so a pass in which the phantom watch
ran first pays nothing for it, and a pass in which only the stall watch runs makes the two listings
the phantom watch would have made.

### 2.2 The judge

```python
#: a job whose newest started step is this old with no step completed since is stalled
DEFAULT_STEP_SILENCE_MIN = 10
MOVING, STEP_SILENCE, BUDGET, UNKNOWN = 'moving', 'step-silence', 'budget', 'unknown'


def step_silence(job, now, limit_s):
    """``(class, step name, since)`` for one in-progress job (the card's rule):

    the newest of its steps carrying a ``started_at`` is the step it is inside; when that start
    is more than ``limit_s`` old and no step of the job completed at or after it, the job is
    :data:`STEP_SILENCE`, named by that step. Under the limit — or with a step completed since —
    it is :data:`MOVING`. A job with no step carrying a start is :data:`UNKNOWN` and nothing is
    claimed about it (a provider that publishes no steps, a job the listing caught before its
    first step, a step whose stamp will not parse).
    """
```

Read as a table, over one job at a time:

| the job's steps | class | step | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `build` started 30m ago, completed 25m ago; `test` started 11m ago, not completed | `step-silence` | `test` | the newest start is 11m old; the only completion is older than it |
| `build` started 30m, completed 25m; `test` started 11m, completed 2m ago | `moving` | `test` | a step completed after the newest start |
| `test` started 4m ago | `moving` | `test` | under the limit |
| `build` completed 12m ago, `test` not started | `moving` | `build` | the newest *start* is `build`'s, and `build` completed at or after it — the card's "no completed step since" is satisfied (§1.2) |
| `steps: []`, or absent, or every `started_at` null | `unknown` | None | no evidence (D4) |
| two steps with the same `started_at` | `step-silence` past the limit, named by the later one in the provider's order | | ties break on the provider's own order, never on the name |

And the limit, at the module's own level (D11, P16):

```python
def step_silence_seconds(product):
    """``ci.queue.step_silence_min`` as seconds: an int is minutes, ``10m``/``1h``/``30s`` parse,
    default :data:`DEFAULT_STEP_SILENCE_MIN`. ``0``, ``off``, ``none`` or ``false`` → 0, the
    watch off."""
```

### 2.3 The watch — `update_stalls(q, src)`

Called from `queue_pass`, **before** `relieve_trunk` (D6):

```python
        n = cancel_duplicate_pushes(...)
        s = update_stalls(q, src=src, items=items, out=out, dry_run=dry_run)
        c, r = relieve_trunk(...)
```

One pass, mirroring `update_phantoms` (P7) line for line:

1. `limit = step_silence_seconds(product)`; `0` returns at once, no host call.
2. `runs = src.live_runs()`; None returns at once — the watch learns nothing and forgets nothing,
   and the records already in the file stand.
3. For each run, `jobs = src.live_jobs(run['id'])`; None skips that run only. For each job with
   `status == 'in_progress'`, `cls, step, since = step_silence(job, q.now, limit)`.
4. `cls == STEP_SILENCE` → the record under `f"{run['id']}:{job['name']}"`:

```json
"stalled": {
  "111:gate": {"run": 111, "job": "gate", "step": "unit tests",
               "since": "2026-09-28T06:41:00Z", "last": "2026-09-28T06:53:00Z", "passes": 2,
               "branch": "task/T-0341", "sha": "bbbb…", "item": "T-0341", "label": "heavy",
               "workflow": "pr.yml"}
}
```
   `since` is the step's own start (D9). A record whose previous pass was more than `STALL_GAP_S`
   ago, or whose `step`/`since` changed, starts again at `passes: 1` — the job moved.
5. Every other class, and every job and run no longer in the listing, leaves the watch.
6. `passes == STALL_PASSES` → §2.4. The line, in the module's voice:

```
ci queue: stalled in-progress pr run 111 (T-0341, heavy) — its job gate has been inside step
  "unit tests" for 11 min with no step completed since
```

`STALL_PASSES = 2`, `STALL_GAP_S = 10 * 60`, `MAX_STALL_RERUNS = 2` join the module's constants.

### 2.4 The cancel, and the re-run

On the confirming pass, per confirmed record, in `since` order (the longest-stuck first):

```
ci queue: cancelled in-progress pr run 111 (T-0341, heavy) — stalled on step silence: its job
  gate has been inside step "unit tests" for 12 min with no step completed since; re-run once
  its branch reaches the head of the line
ci queue: would cancel in-progress pr run 111 (T-0341, heavy) — stalled on step silence: …
ci queue: cancel of stalled pr run 111 refused — tried again next pass
```

- `mode: on` → `gh run cancel <id> -R <slug>`. A refused cancel keeps the record (`passes` stays at
  the cap) and says so; the run may have completed under the read, and the next pass will not find
  it in the listing at all.
- `mode: dry-run` → the `would cancel` line, no call, no write.
- A successful cancel appends the `relief` record of P10 with `trunk_id`/`trunk_sha`/
  `trunk_created` absent and two keys of its own:

```python
rec = {'id': rid, 'kind': kind, 'item': item, 'prio': prio, 'label': label, 'workflow': wf,
       'at': _iso(now), 'branch': branch, 'sha': sha,
       'stall': STEP_SILENCE, 'stall_step': step, 'stalls': prior + 1}
```
  and the `stalled` record is dropped.
- `started(rec)`, the predicate `_rerun_cancelled` calls (P10), gains one clause at the top: a record
  carrying `stall` is ready at once — `(None, 'its stalled step')` — because nothing protects it.
  Its re-run line says which step it was cancelled for:

```
ci queue: re-ran pr run 111 (T-0341, heavy) — cancelled 3 min ago, stalled in step "unit tests"
```
- `prior` is the count of `stalls` over the `relief` records and the `stalled` records of the same
  `(branch, sha)`, so it survives the record leaving the file. At `prior + 1 > MAX_STALL_RERUNS`
  the run is cancelled and **no** record is written (D8):

```
ci queue: NOT re-running pr run 111 (T-0341) — step "unit tests" has stalled 3 times on
  task/T-0341 at bbbbbbbbb; the step is the defect, not the run
```

Everything downstream is unchanged: `rerun_ids` carries the id while the record lives, so the lane
waits for the re-run instead of reading the cancelled checks as a verdict (P11); the TTL and the
stale-workflow guard apply as they do to a relief cancel (P12).

### 2.5 The configuration

`FIELDS` gains `step_silence_min`; `config_problems` gains one line — the key joins the existing
"a number of minutes > 0" loop, with `0` and the off words allowed:

```yaml
ci:
  queue:
    step_silence_min: 10   # an in-progress job whose newest started step is this old with no
                           # step completed since is stalled: its run is cancelled and re-run
                           # (at most twice per sha). 0 or `off` turns the watch off.
```

That block is added to `docs/products.example.yaml` beside the other wait minutes, and
`step_silence_min: 10` is set in `sample/product.yaml`.

### 2.6 The rows and the clause

```python
def stall_text(rec, now):
    """``run 111 (T-0341) job gate stalled in step "unit tests" for 12 min``."""


def stall_lines(product, now=None):
    """One line per confirmed step-silence stall, longest first, off the queue file."""


def stall_rows(product, now=None):
    """The doctor's ``ci runners`` rows for the stall watch, ``[(required, ok, detail)]``: one red
    row per confirmed stall, one red row per sha at :data:`MAX_STALL_RERUNS`, and nothing at all
    when neither is — the group's ok row is the phantom watch's. No ``gh`` call."""
```

`doctor.check_ci_runners` returns `ci_queue.runner_rows(product) + ci_queue.stall_rows(product)`;
`status_clause` joins `stall_lines(product, now)` after `phantom_lines(product, now)` in the same
`'; '.join`. Nothing else in either file changes.

### 2.7 The class on the stream

`SCHEMAS['ci']` gains `'stall': (('str', 'null'), None)`; `JOB_SCHEMA` gains
`'stall_step': (('str', 'null'), None)`. `validate` refuses a `stall` that is neither
`step-silence` nor `budget` (the shape of the `gates.conclusion` check at `metrics.py:258`).

`ci_from_api` fills both, per run:

- `stall = 'budget'` when the run's conclusion is `timed_out`, or any job's is;
- `stall = 'step-silence'`, and `stall_step` on the named job, when the product's queue file
  records this run id as cancelled for step silence — step-silence wins when both are true;
- else None.

The join reads one file, once per backfill: `ci_queue.stalled_runs(product)` →
`{run id: {'job': …, 'step': …, 'at': …}}`, from the `relief` records carrying `stall` and from a
new `stalls` history list the cancel appends to (the `relief` record leaves the file the moment it
is re-run, and the event is written after the run completes). `STALL_HISTORY_TTL_S = 48 * 60 * 60`
prunes it — two days, so a backfill on either side of midnight still joins, and P19's first-write-
wins is never fed an event with a `stall` it could have known (P20).

A product with no queue file, or one the queue does not watch, joins nothing and every event's
`stall` is None or `budget`.

## 4. Records

### 4.1 Files the change writes

  - `stalled`: `{"<run>:<job>": {run, job, step, since, last, passes, branch, sha, item, label,
