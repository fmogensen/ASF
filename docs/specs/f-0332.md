# F-0332 — the item that was corrected thirty-seven times

The card is a scorecard cause: `kind:correct` took 0.158 of session spend over the fortnight
before it was filed, against a threshold of 0.15, and it must read under 0.15 — and fall by at
least 20 % — over the fortnight after this lands (`asf/scorecard/diagnose.py:25`, `:289-295`).

Read today through the same arithmetic the verify will use, the cause has not held still. It is
**0.186**: 869 correction sessions, $771.03 of $4,144.75, 269.7 hours (P1). In the same fortnight
the factory ran 216 `coder`, 170 `delivery-code` and 116 `code` sessions — about 500 first-time
code sessions against 869 corrections of them.

The money is not spread across those 869. It is in a tail: **71 % of the correction sessions, and
74 % of their spend, are on the 82 items that had four or more rounds** (P2). One Task had 37.
Three caps already stand between a correction row and a session, and all three let that Task
through:

* the **relaunch cap** parks a job handed the same head, card and cause twice
  (`relaunch.py:44`) — and for `correct` the cause is not even compared, only the head
  (`HEAD_CAPPED`, `:52`). Every lane rebuild moves the head, so every lane rebuild buys a round.
* the **round cap** (`ROUND_CAP = 3`, `lifecycle.py:86`) is counted on the *finding*, not the
  item, and the item's own rounds counter is frozen at 3 on purpose (B-0048,
  `lifecycle.py:3133-3136`): *"A different finding goes back to a correct session, whatever the
  rounds."* (`:3089-3092`). A stream of different findings never escalates (P4).
* the **daily cap** (`DAILY_CAP = 6`, `loops.py:33`) resets every 24 h, so it permits 84 launches
  a fortnight. The worst job used 37 of them (P7). Its sibling rule, **same report**, never fires
  on this ledger at all: the key it compares is stamped only when a park is already being
  decided, so 297 of the 339 keys on the record are empty (P8).

Three caps, each right about the thing it watches, and no one of them answers the question the
money asks: *how many correction sessions may one item have before a person looks at it?* Nothing
counts them. This spec adds the counter — one rule, in the module that already holds the two
rules past the relaunch cap, that no moved head, no new cause, no new finding and no new day can
reset.

* **P4 — the escalation ladder cannot see an item, only a finding.** `hold`
  (`lifecycle.py:3086-3147`) writes `same` from `next_finding` and escalates on it alone; the
  item's `rounds` stops climbing at the cap by design, so it can never cross one:

  ```
  ROUND_CAP (lifecycle)        : 3
  CORRECTION_ROUNDS (feeder)   : 3

  ```

  The live `same` values on the worst items read `1, 2, 3, 4, None` — they reset, round after
  round, because the next hold names something else. `None` is a hold of a no-round kind, which
  records no `same` at all.
* **P5 — the park is not a ceiling; it is lifted and laid again.** `relaunch.loop_key`'s own
  docstring says so (`relaunch.py:347-350`): *"one loop re-parked tick after tick (the lane's new
  correction lifting the park, the cap laying it again)."* A park is the item's correction only
  while nothing newer is pending (`pending_correction`, `lifecycle.py:1115-1140`;
  `correction_of`, `:1346-1368`), so the lane's next hold supersedes it and the feeder's
  `FIX → CORRECT` row returns. The registry shows the churn: **4685 `relaunch cap` parks recorded
  in the window, and 1105 correction launches made anyway.**
* **P6 — what releases a park.** `asf unpark`, the item closing, its Feature being replanned
  (`health.py:869-878`), and — for a `relaunch cap` park — any card edit (`health.py:863-867`).
  53 unparks were recorded on correction jobs in the window, 26 of them by hand. A ceiling
  therefore counts *since the item's latest unpark*, which is already what every rule in
  `loops.judge` counts over.
* **P7 — the daily cap's arithmetic.** 6 launches per job per 24 h is 84 a fortnight. Replayed
  over the real ledger it refuses 191 of 1105 correction launches, and no job in the window was
  stopped by reaching a *total*, because there is no total.
* **P8 — the same-report rule is inert on this record.** `report_key` is written by
  `_stamp_report_key` (`step_wave.py:683-691`), which `relaunch_capped` calls only once some rule
  has *already* refused a launch (`:694-712`). So the rule that needs two keyed runs sees at most
  one:

  Not one launch in the fortnight was refused by it. This is a defect of its own and is left
  alone here (Out) — naming it is not fixing it, and the ceiling below does not depend on it.

### In

* A third rule in `asf/workers/loops.py`: **a total cap** — launches of one job since the item's
  latest unpark, whatever changed between them and however long it took.
* Its two flags, `relaunch_total_cap` (the number, default 6) and `relaunch_total_kinds` (the job
  kinds it applies to, default `correct`), read by `loops.settings` and nowhere else, with the
  three registrations a flag needs.
* The kind gate and the wiring: `relaunch.loop_guard` applies the rule only to a launch whose kind
  is in the configured set, and `asf/tick/step_wave.py` hands the guard the launch's kind.
* The park's reason line, saying how many launches, what the cap is, which flag sets it and what
  releases it.
* A replay pin over an anonymised slice of the loop this card measured, beside the two that are
  there.
* The one-keyword narrowing of the three shipped assertions the new default reddens — two in
  `JudgeTests`, one in `ReplayPinTests`, each gaining `total_cap=None` (§1a). In scope because
  they are part of this change, not a surprise the coder discovers.

### Out

* **The three caps that already exist.** The relaunch cap, the head cap, the round cap, the
  per-finding ladder and the daily cap are not retuned, renumbered or rewritten. The new rule is
  appended after them and fires only past what they catch (P11).
* **The no-round exemptions** of `naming`, `copies` and `footprint` (P3). Each is a measured
  decision about whose fault a hold was, and the ceiling does not need them changed: it counts
  *launches*, so a hold that spends no round still counts toward it. That is the point.
* **The same-report rule's inertness** (P8). `report_key` is stamped only when a park is already
  being decided, so the rule has refused nothing in the measured fortnight. It is a defect of its
  own, with its own fix (stamp the key when health records a run's end, where the log is
  certainly that run's), its own blast radius and its own measurement, and it wants its own card:
  the evidence is P8, and nothing in this spec depends on it.
* **The sibling causes.** `kind:review` is over its threshold at 0.207 and `kind:adjudicate` is
  under; neither is touched. A generic ceiling would refuse 3 % of review launches and 10 % of
  adjudicate launches (P10) — the flag can be widened to them later, on their own card's
  evidence, with no code change.
* **`reshape` and `delivery-code`**, the two other loops the same rule would catch at 48 % and
  29 % (P10). Out of the default for the throughput reason in D5, reachable by one flag line.
* **Escalating to an adjudication at the ceiling.** D2.
* **Making a correction session cheaper, or a first-time session better**, so that fewer
  corrections are needed. Both are real levers and neither is this card: P2 says the spend is in
  the tail of rounds, not in the price of a round.
* **Any change to the scorecard, its thresholds, its window or its verify.** The cause is read
  exactly as it is read today; this spec's job is to move the number, not the ruler.

### Preconditions the work depends on

1. The loop guard is wired and running: `step_wave.relaunch_assessment` passes
   `guard=loops.settings(product)` into `relaunch.assess` on every screened row
   (`step_wave.py:676`), and `relaunch_capped` acts on its verdict (`:694`). The new rule needs no
   new call site.
2. The runs the guard judges are already "the job's runs since the item's latest unpark", already
   filtered of a spent quota window, a heartbeat-start refusal and an operator stop
   (`relaunch.py:239-258`, `counted` `:89`). The ceiling inherits all four exclusions by reading
   the same list, which is why a quota-exhausted window does not burn a round of it.
3. A park is written by `relaunch.park_fields` and read by the feeder as `PARKED` with
   `waits_on='operator'` (`rows.py:1037-1041`), lifted by `asf unpark`, a card edit, a replan or
   the item closing (P6). The new rule writes its park through that same function and changes none
   of it.
4. The count is read off the ledger, never off a correction field, so no lane hold, no moved head
   and no new finding can reset it (P5 is why this matters).
5. `conventions.flags` is the one map a behaviour switch lives in, and a new name needs three
   registrations: `KNOWN_FLAGS`, `tests/test_env.py:761` and a `docs/products.example.yaml` line
   (P12). `tests/test_conventions_flags.py` walks the source and fails on a `.flag('<name>')` that
   is not registered.
6. The record holds no Story for F-0332 (the session brief: *none yet*), and this session's range
   is `S:99855-99904`.

### The choices

| | chosen | against | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A **total cap on launches of one job since the item's latest unpark** — the count no moved head, new cause, new finding or new day resets | A fourth rule keyed on something cleverer: rounds-per-week, spend-per-item, a convergence heuristic over the holds | Every existing cap is keyed on *what changed* and every one of them is defeated by something changing (P3, P4, P5, P7). A count of launches is the one quantity that cannot be reset by the thing being measured, and it is the quantity the money is denominated in. It is also the rule `loops.py`'s own docstring already promises and does not deliver: *"the hard ceiling a loop no rule foresaw still stops at"* — which today resets at midnight |
| D2 | At the ceiling the row **parks for the operator**, as every other loop-guard park does | Hand the item one adjudication first, the way the per-finding cap does at `at_cap` | An adjudication is itself a repair session with its own cause key, and at $0.79 against a correction's $0.89 it would move the spend, not remove it — about 26 new adjudications in the measured fortnight. The per-finding ladder already offers an adjudication where a *ruling* is what is missing (`rows.py:1085-1098`); an item that has burned six separate correction sessions is not short of a ruling, it is short of a person. `operator_flagged` and the one `ALARM loop` line already make that visible |

## The design

One new rule, two new flags, one new fixture, across **nine paths** — the footprint the plan's
`writes:` must carry, because a Task that writes a path absent from `writes:` is held:

```
asf/workers/loops.py                      §1   the rule, its two constants, settings, replay
asf/workers/relaunch.py                   §2   the kind gate in loop_guard
asf/tick/step_wave.py                     §3   the launch's kind reaches the guard
asf/conventions.py                        §4   the two flag names in KNOWN_FLAGS
tests/test_env.py                         §4   the two names in _PLANNED_FLAG_VALUES
docs/products.example.yaml                §4   a commented line for each
tests/fixtures/replay/loop_total.jsonl    §5   the new replay fixture (new file)
tests/test_loops.py                       §1a  TotalCapTests, TotalReplayPinTests, 3 edits
tests/test_relaunch.py                    §1a  TotalCapTests
```

Nothing is deleted. **No park that fires today changes** — not its rule name, not its words, not
the pin's 14 at the default (P11). Three shipped assertions do move, and only three: §1a names
them, with the measurement. The claim is not "nothing changes"; it is "nothing changes that a
product in production can see".

### 1. `asf/workers/loops.py` — the third rule

The module's two rules become three. Beside `SAME_REPORT` and `DAILY_CAP`:

```python
#: Launches of one job since the item's latest unpark before the row parks, whatever changed
#: between them and however long it took: the ceiling no moved head, new cause, new finding and
#: no new day resets. Twice the per-finding round cap (asf.workers.lifecycle.ROUND_CAP).
TOTAL_CAP = 6
#: The job kinds the total cap applies to (flag ``relaunch_total_kinds``). A kind outside it is
#: judged by the other two rules alone — F-0332 D5: a delivery lead's seventh session may be its
#: seventh member, where a correction's seventh round is a loop.
TOTAL_KINDS = ('correct',)
SAME_REPORT_RULE, DAILY_RULE, TOTAL_RULE = 'same report', 'daily cap', 'total cap'
```

`judge` gains one parameter and one block, after the daily cap's and before the `return None`:

```python
def judge(runs, card='', now=None, same_report=SAME_REPORT, daily_cap=DAILY_CAP,
          total_cap=TOTAL_CAP):
    ...
    if total_cap and len(runs) >= total_cap:
        return TOTAL_RULE, (
            f'{len(runs)} launches since the last unpark, at the total cap of {total_cap} '
            f'(flags.relaunch_total_cap) — a moved head, a new cause and a new day have each '
            f'bought a round and the item is still not done')
    return None
```

`runs` is already the list the caller means: the job's runs since the item's latest unpark, a
spent quota window and an uncounted run excluded (`relaunch.py:244-248`). Nothing is filtered
again here, and no clock is read — the rule has no window, which is what makes it a ceiling.

`settings` reads the two new flags, and only it does:

```python
def settings(product):
    """``{'same_report', 'daily_cap', 'total_cap', 'total_kinds'}`` — this module's three rules'
    caps, and the kinds the third applies to."""
    conv = _conv(product)
    return {'same_report': _int_flag(conv, 'relaunch_same_report', SAME_REPORT),
            'daily_cap': _int_flag(conv, 'relaunch_daily_cap', DAILY_CAP),
            'total_cap': _int_flag(conv, 'relaunch_total_cap', TOTAL_CAP),
            'total_kinds': _kinds_flag(conv, 'relaunch_total_kinds', TOTAL_KINDS)}
```

`_kinds_flag` is `_int_flag`'s shape for a list: a flag that is `off` is `()`, a comma-separated
string or a YAML list is its names stripped and lowercased, anything else is the default — so a
malformed value is never a silently empty set, by the same rule `_int_flag` already follows for a
malformed number.

It is deliberately *not* a reuse of `asf/briefs/build.py:262-274` (`cheap_kinds`, for
`models.cheap_kinds`), which already splits a list-or-string flag with
`re.split(r'[\s,\[\]]+', v)` — copy that splitter, not the function. The one difference is the
decision: `cheap_kinds` returns an **empty** frozenset for a malformed value, which is right for
it (an unreadable flag means "no kind is cheap", and the built-in table stands). Here an empty set
turns the whole rule off, so a typo would silently remove the ceiling — the hazard S-99856's
checkbox names. `_kinds_flag` returns the **default** instead. Same splitter, opposite fallback,
on purpose.

`replay` takes `total_cap=TOTAL_CAP` and hands it to `judge`, so the pin can drive all three
rules and turn any of them off. It needs no kind of its own: it replays one ledger slice whose
jobs are the caller's choice.

### 1a. The three shipped assertions this reddens, and the one keyword each takes

D8 makes the new parameter default to `TOTAL_CAP = 6`, like its two siblings. Every shipped call
that turns the other two rules off **and happens to pass six runs** therefore meets a rule that
has no window to turn off, and gets a refusal it did not get before. There are exactly three, and
this is measured, not predicted: §1's block above was applied verbatim to a scratch copy of
`loops.py` at `adf00a222` and the shipped `tests/test_loops.py` run against it — `Ran 23 tests` /
`FAILED (failures=3)`, naming exactly the three calls tabled below, then `OK` with the three
edits applied and nothing else changed. `tests/test_loops.py` is byte-identical at
`origin/main` `cf319ff18` (P12), so the measurement stands on the plan's base too.

| the call | what it does | the edit |
|---|---|---|
| `tests/test_loops.py:89` `JudgeTests.test_a_launch_older_than_24h_does_not_count_toward_the_cap` | 6 runs (1 older than the window + 5 recent), `same_report=None, daily_cap=6` | add `total_cap=None` |
| `tests/test_loops.py:94` `JudgeTests.test_daily_cap_off_disables_the_rule` | 6 runs, `same_report=None, daily_cap=None` | add `total_cap=None` |
| `tests/test_loops.py:145` `ReplayPinTests.test_turning_off_both_rules_avoids_nothing` | `replay(sessions, same_report=None, daily_cap=None)` → 10 refusals, all `total cap`; asserts `[]` | add `total_cap=None` |

Each keeps the assertion its name makes. The point of all three is *"this rule, turned off,
refuses nothing"*, and `total_cap=None` is how that sentence is now written for three rules
instead of two. None of them is a behaviour the product relies on; each is a statement about one
rule in isolation, which is what the new keyword restores.

**Which assertion wins at `:89`.** The shipped test is named *"a launch older than 24h does not
count toward the cap"*, and S-99855's acceptance line reads *"a launch older than any window still
counts, because the rule has no window"*. On the same six runs these say opposite things, and the
spec must not leave a coder to guess. **The new line is the intended behaviour.** The old test is
right about the rule it was written for — the *daily* cap is windowed and must stay windowed, and
that is precisely what makes it resettable (P7) — so it keeps its assertion and gains
`total_cap=None`, narrowing it to the rule it names. The ceiling's own treatment of the same six
runs is asserted the other way, in `TotalCapTests`, by S-99855's line. Two rules, two windows, two
tests: the edit is a narrowing of scope, not a reversal, and the old test is amended rather than
deleted or left to fail.

### 2. `asf/workers/relaunch.py` — the kind gate

`loop_guard` takes the launch's kind and gates the new rule on it. Everything else in the
function is unchanged:

```python
def loop_guard(path, job, item, card='', guard=None, now=None, kind=''):

`assess` grows the same keyword and passes it through — **`verdict` does not**. `verdict` calls

arrival. Its signature stands unchanged. The park itself is written by
`relaunch_capped` through `park_fields` exactly as it is today (D2), so the correction's shape,

The printed park, on the item of P2 with 37 rounds:

```
ALARM loop correct-t-0042 T-0042 — correct-t-0042 6 launches since the last unpark, at the
total cap of 6 (flags.relaunch_total_cap) — a moved head, a new cause and a new day have each
bought a round and the item is still not done. Not relaunched (total cap): the row is parked
until the card changes or `asf unpark T-0042`
parked   correct-t-0042           T-0042     — …the same reason…

The printed count is **six, not seven**, and the distinction is the rule's whole arithmetic.
`relaunch_assessment` runs *before* the spawn, so `runs` holds the job's **prior** launches and
the launch being refused is not among them (`lifecycle.item_runs`). The rule fires at
`len(runs) >= 6` — six prior launches — and `f'{len(runs)} …'` prints six. This matches the daily
cap exactly, which prints `len(recent)` and also says six at a cap of six (`loops.py:108-112`). The
seventh *launch* is the one refused; the sixth is the last one that ran. The table below is right
to call that round 7, and S-99855's line (*"the count in the reason is the number of launches it
has had"*) is right to ask for six. Measured, with §1 applied: six prior launches in the ledger →
`('total cap', '6 launches since the last unpark, at the total cap of 6 …')`.

and the feeder's row from the next read on: `FIX → CORRECT  T-0042  PARKED relaunch cap`,
`waits_on: operator`, launching nothing.
