# F-0207 — the gap never came, and then the debt was forgotten

The upgrade to `5cab3a2` was owed from 06:21 and installed by hand at 06:41. For those twenty
minutes one product's ticks printed `tick: waiting — upgrade to 5cab3a2 pending` and did no work,
while every tick of the owning product printed

```
upgrade: deferred to the next tick — another asf tick is running (pid N)
```

Main was green the whole time. Nothing was wrong with the target, the network or the package: the
install simply never ran, because the one condition it waits for — *no other asf process on the
floor* — was never true at the one moment it was ever tested, the start of the owner's tick.

Two things make that condition unreachable, and they are still in this checkout:

* **The owner's own clocks are on the floor and are never parked.** A product runs several clocks
  (P3); `waiting()` parks every product except the marker's owner (P4), by design, so the one
  product that must find an empty floor is the one product that keeps putting processes on it.
* **The only attempt is at a tick's start** (P2) — on a cron scheduler every interval clock fires
  on the aligned minute, so that is the fullest the floor ever is. The emptiest moment in a
  factory's day, the instant a long tick or a gate exits, is tested by nobody.

And when the ten-minute park marker expires, the debt goes with it: nothing on disk says an
install is owed (P8), the parked products resume, fresh half-hour harvest gates spawn onto the
floor, and the whole cycle starts again from a worse position.

This card is not "wait longer". It is: **an owed install becomes a durable record with its own
rules, every process that was on the floor tries the install on its way out, and from one clock
interval after the debt is recorded the floor is drained on purpose — the owner's own clocks
included — so the gap is made rather than waited for.**

### The choices

| # | Chosen | Against | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **One durable record of the debt, beside the park: `state/upgrade-due.json`** (`sha`, `owner`, `at`, `passes`, and the park stretch's `parked_at`/`loud`). The pending marker keeps its job, its TTL, its cool-down, its `held()` and both its views, unchanged. | Giving `upgrade-pending.json` no TTL, or a longer one, and carrying the debt in it. | The TTL is B-0141's protection and is pinned twice (P7, P12): it exists because *parking* other products is expensive. Owing an install is not expensive. Two facts with different lifetimes cannot share one record — and the incident is precisely what happens when the cheap fact is thrown away with the expensive one. The debt is paid off by an install, cancelled by a refusal or a failure, and by nothing else — never by age. |
| C2 | **The install is attempted again as the last act of every tick of the owner**, after the steps, the state commit and the summary, on every return path including the parked one and the one that could not take the product lock. | Only at the tick's start (today, P2); a retry between steps; a dedicated installer clock or daemon. | P3: clocks fire on aligned minutes, so a tick's start is the fullest the floor ever is and its exit the emptiest — and the exiting process is the one that knows the floor just lost a member. A mid-tick install is the `ImportError` the deferral exists to prevent. A new clock would be one more process on the floor it is trying to empty. |
| C3 | **The detached background harvest makes the same attempt as its last act**, in `step_harvest.main`. | Leaving the harvest out and letting the next tick notice it ended. | P6: the gate is the longest-lived process on the floor, so more often than not it *is* the last one out; the tick that would otherwise notice is up to a clock interval away. Its `main()` already ends in a plain `return` with nothing imported after it. |
| C4 | **From `DUE_GRACE_S` after the debt is recorded, every tick of every product parks — the owner included — until the floor is empty or the stretch caps out.** | Keeping the owner exempt for ever (today, P4); making the owner's tick block on a longer drain instead. | The exemption is the reason no gap ever came (P4). A longer blocking drain holds the record lock while it sleeps and is still bounded by a number smaller than what it waits for (P6). Parking is cheap and short: a parked tick exits in a second and spawns no gate, so the floor can only shrink and the last process out installs (C2, C3). The grace keeps the cheap paths first — for one clock interval the owner goes on working while the start and exit attempts try with nobody parked at all. |
| C5 | **While a debt is recorded, no tick spawns a background harvest** — the suppression reads `due() or pending()`, keeping the marker case (an operator's `asf upgrade --wait` writes a marker and no debt) and keeping its line word for word. | Leaving the suppression on the marker alone. | The suppression is what makes the drain monotonic, and today it dies with the marker at ten minutes while the debt lives on — after which a fresh many-minute gate walks onto the floor the install is waiting to empty (P7). Same wording, same pinned test (P12). |
| C6 | **The park stretch is bounded and cyclic, and the debt outlives it**: `DUE_PARK_MAX_S` (= `PENDING_TTL_S`) parked, then one `NEEDS OPERATOR` line and `DUE_RESUME_S` running, then another stretch. | Parking until the install happens; dropping the debt when the stretch caps out, as the TTL does to the marker. | B-0141's rule is that a stuck upgrade never stops the factory, so an install that cannot succeed must not park the machine for ever. But *forgetting* it is this card's defect. A bound on the park and no bound on the debt is the only pair that honours both — and because nothing new joins the floor while a debt exists (C5), each stretch starts from a smaller floor than the last. |
| C7 | **Only the debt's owner installs.** | The card's third option: the parked product's tick does the install itself. | The reinstall replaces the venv under whatever else is running, and a parked tick cannot know that the owner's own long tick is not mid-import (P10). "Whichever process finishes last" reaches the same gap without that bet (C2, C3), and the owner is the product whose repo the head was read from in the first place. |
| C8 | **One `flock` serialises the install and holds back new ticks during it**: `state/upgrade.lock` (`contextlib` and `fcntl` join this module's imports), held for the whole of `_install`, non-blocking, dying with its process; `waiting()` is True while it is held. Two installs can never overlap. A tick is *narrowed*, not excluded: one that passed `waiting()` a moment before the lock was taken still starts, exactly as one already running does — for a running process the floor check is the protection, and this lock is what makes the far more frequent new start safe. | Relying on `other_ticks` alone (today); a blocking lock so a tick waits out an install; reusing `asf.tick.tick._flock`. | With an attempt at every tick exit and every harvest exit, two of them can pass the floor check in the same second, and a clock can fire into the half-minute a `pipx install --force` takes — both are the `ImportError` this module opens on (P10). A tick that blocks on an install is a tick that is on the floor while the install runs, which is the thing being avoided. The tick's helper is a *waiting* poll loop for a product lock; importing `asf.tick.tick` from `asf.upgrade` to borrow it would tangle two modules for four lines. |
| C9 | **`passes` is a counter on the debt and the number the card's test reads**, printed in the line a busy exit prints: `upgrade: due 5cab3a2 — pass 4, the floor is busy (pid 4242)`. | No counter; driving the park off the counter instead of off time. | "Installs within N passes" needs an N that is written down, and a starving install has to be legible in one line rather than reconstructed from a log. It stays a record and never a rule: the exit attempts would burn a pass-based grace in seconds, so the grace and the stretch are wall-clock (C4, C6). |
| C10 | **Both views name a debt when no park is showing, and nothing else gains a surface.** | An `asf upgrade --status`; a doctor section of its own; a record card per debt. | P11 — the incident was read out of a log by hand. The two places that already answer "is this factory ticking" must also answer "and does it owe an install". The views read `read_due()`, not `due()`, for the same reason `held()` skips the ancestor check: a `git merge-base` does not belong in a view. No new command, no config key, no schema change, no card. |

### In, and out

**In.** `asf/upgrade.py`: the due record (`due_path`, `read_due`, `bump_due`, `due`, `clear_due`,
`settle_due`, `due_label`), the install lock (`lock_path`, `install_lock`, `installing`, and
`contextlib` and `fcntl` in this module's imports), the
last-one-out attempt (`install_due`), the drain window (`draining`, and `waiting()`'s two new
clauses), `install()` writing the debt on a floor-deferral and settling it on every terminal
outcome, and the module docstring that documents all of it. `asf/tick/tick.py`: the exit attempt on
every path of `cmd_tick`. `asf/tick/step_harvest.py`: the suppression on the debt, and the
harvest's own exit attempt. `asf/doctor.py` and `asf/views/status.py`: the debt named where the
park already is.

**Out, deliberately.** The pending marker's rules — its TTL, its `expired.json`, its cool-down,
`held()`, `held_label()` and every test that pins them (C1). `upgrade.drain_wait_s` and the start
drain: unchanged, still the fast path, still 180 s. The batching interval (`min_interval_min`) and
the `Urgent: yes` override. `refuses()` / the CI guard. `--wait`'s manual semantics. Any new
config key or command (C10, P14). A parked product installing for the owner (C7). `--dry-run`,
which must touch nothing on this machine and so makes no attempt. `docs/guide/upgrading.md`, which
documents the operator's manual upgrade and says nothing about the tick's deferral.

## The design

Three Stories write `asf/upgrade.py` and must land in order (the record, then the attempt, then the
window); the two others write `asf/tick/*` and the views.

### 1. `asf/upgrade.py` — the debt

```python
#: the debt's grace: one clock interval in which the owner's ticks go on working and the start and
#: exit attempts try for the gap with nobody parked
DUE_GRACE_S = 5 * 60
#: one park stretch — the same bound the other products' park has carried since B-0141
DUE_PARK_MAX_S = PENDING_TTL_S
#: and how long the factory runs between two stretches
DUE_RESUME_S = PENDING_TTL_S


def due_path():
    return os.path.join(env.ASF_HOME, 'state', 'upgrade-due.json')


def read_due():
    """The install this factory owes, as written — no clearing, no git (the views read this)."""
    try:
        with open(due_path(), encoding='utf-8') as f:
            data = json.load(f)
    except (OSError, ValueError):
        return None
    return data if isinstance(data, dict) and data.get('sha') and data.get('at') else None


def clear_due():
    try:
        os.remove(due_path())
    except OSError:
        pass


def _write_due(data):
    _write_json(due_path(), data)
    return data


def bump_due(sha, owner, now=None):
    """Record that an install of ``sha`` is owed by product ``owner``, or count one more pass
    against the record already there. Keeps the first ``at`` — the debt's age is what the drain
    window reads — and starts a fresh record when the sha moved: a newer head is a new debt, and
    the passes the old one waited say nothing about this one.

    Unlike :func:`write_pending` it is never refused. A cool-down suppresses the *park*; the debt
    is not a park (F-0207: the debt was thrown away with the marker and forgotten)."""
    old = read_due()
    if old is not None and old.get('sha') == sha:
        return _write_due(dict(old, owner=owner, passes=int(old.get('passes') or 0) + 1))
    return _write_due({'sha': sha, 'owner': owner, 'at': now or time.time(), 'passes': 1})


def due(installed=None, out=print, run=subprocess.run):
    """The debt, or ``None``. Removed when it is paid — the sha is the installed build's own
    commit or an ancestor of it (:func:`_installed`: a later install carried it). Never removed by
    age: a debt is not a park, and this record exists because the last one was forgotten after ten
    minutes."""
    data = read_due()
    if data is None:
        return None
    if installed is None:
        from asf import drift
        installed = drift.installed_commit()
    if _installed(data['sha'], installed, run):
        clear_due()
        return None
    return data


def due_label(data):
    """``upgrade to <sha> due since <time> — pass <n> (owner <product>)`` — what a view says when
    no park is showing and the factory still owes an install."""
    return (f'upgrade to {data["sha"][:7]} due since '
            f'{time.strftime("%H:%M", time.localtime(data["at"]))} — '
            f'pass {data.get("passes") or 0} (owner {data.get("owner") or "operator"})')


def settle_due(rc, ref, out=print):
    """Pay off or cancel the debt after an install that actually ran. 0 pays it. ``DEFERRED`` from
    :func:`_install` cancels it — a red or unreadable head is refused, and the next green head
    becomes due on its own (B-0141). Any other code cancels it with a ``NEEDS OPERATOR`` line: a
    broken install is an operator's problem, and nothing may keep draining the floor for it."""
    data = read_due()
    if data is None or data['sha'] != ref:
        return
    clear_due()
    if rc not in (0, DEFERRED):
        out(f'NEEDS OPERATOR: the install of {ref[:7]} failed (exit {rc}) — the ticks resume; '
            f'rerun asf upgrade --to {ref[:7]} --wait once it is fixed')
```

### 2. `asf/upgrade.py` — the lock, and the last one out

```python
def lock_path():
    return os.path.join(env.ASF_HOME, 'state', 'upgrade.lock')


@contextlib.contextmanager
def install_lock():
    """``flock`` over :func:`lock_path` for the length of one install: yields the open file, or
    ``None`` when another process holds it. Never waits, and dies with its process, so a killed
    install parks nobody."""
    os.makedirs(os.path.dirname(lock_path()), exist_ok=True)
    f = open(lock_path(), 'a+', encoding='utf-8')
    try:
        try:
            fcntl.flock(f, fcntl.LOCK_EX | fcntl.LOCK_NB)
        except OSError:
            yield None
            return
        yield f
    finally:
        f.close()


def installing():
    """True when an install is running right now — the one thing a starting tick must never run
    under (the ``ImportError`` this module opens on). Read by :func:`waiting`, which narrows that
    window rather than closing it: a tick that read this a moment before the lock was taken still
    starts, and for a process already running it is the floor check, not this, that protects it."""
    with install_lock() as f:
        return f is None


def install_due(product_name, run=subprocess.run, out=print, sleep=time.sleep):
    """The last-one-out attempt: take the install this factory owes, if the floor is empty now.

    ``None`` when nothing is owed, when this product does not own it (C7) or while an install is
    already running; :data:`DEFERRED` when the floor was still busy, which counts one pass and
    says so; else :func:`install`'s own code.

    Called as the last act of a process that was itself on the floor
    (:func:`asf.tick.tick.cmd_tick`, :func:`asf.tick.step_harvest.main`). Every clock fires on the
    minute, so a tick's start is the fullest the floor ever is and its exit the emptiest — and the
    exiting process is the one that knows the floor just lost a member."""
    data = due(run=run, out=out)
    if data is None or data.get('owner') != product_name or installing():
        return None
    others = other_ticks(run)
    if others:
        data = bump_due(data['sha'], product_name)
        out(f'upgrade: due {data["sha"][:7]} — pass {data["passes"]}, the floor is busy '
            f'(pid {", ".join(str(p) for p in others)})')
        return DEFERRED
    out(f'upgrade: the floor is empty at this exit — installing {data["sha"][:7]}')
    return install(data['sha'], run=run, out=out, owner=product_name, wait_s=0, sleep=sleep)
```

`install()` gains the debt at its two ends, and the lock around the install itself. Its floor
deferral (`asf/upgrade.py:573-583`) writes the debt the park marker cannot outlive:

```python
    if others:
        where = 'the next tick' if owner else 'later'
        out(f'upgrade: deferred to {where} — another asf process is running '
            f'(pid {", ".join(str(p) for p in others)})')
        for line in describe(others, run):
            out(f'  {line}')
        if owner and ref:
            data = bump_due(ref, owner)   # the debt outlives the park and its cool-down (C1)
            out(f'upgrade: due {ref[:7]} — pass {data["passes"]}, owed by {owner}')
        if not owner:
            if marked is not None:
                clear_pending()
            out('upgrade: NOT installed — rerun with --wait [SECONDS] to wait for them to end')
        return DEFERRED
    with install_lock() as lock:
        if lock is None:   # C8: another process is installing; this one is not a second one
            out('upgrade: another install is running — nothing done here')
            return DEFERRED
        rc = _install(ref, run, out, pin=pin) if pin != ref else _install(ref, run, out)
    if rc == 0:
        record_last(ref)
    settle_due(rc, ref, out)               # paid, refused, or failed: never left owed (C6)
    if owner or marked is not None:
        clear_pending()
    return rc
```

The early return is the only path that keeps a debt, and it is the only path on which nothing was
installed because of the floor. Every other outcome settles it — which is what keeps B-0141's rule
intact: a target that cannot be installed stops draining the floor at once.

### 3. `asf/upgrade.py` — the drain window

```python
def draining(out=print, now=None, installed=None, run=subprocess.run):
    """True when no tick may start because the install this factory owes is draining the floor.

    It parks every product, the owner included: the owner's own overlapping clocks are why no gap
    ever came (F-0207). A parked tick exits in a second and spawns no gate (:func:`due` is what
    :func:`asf.tick.step_harvest.run` reads), so the floor can only shrink and the last process out
    installs (:func:`install_due`).

    Bounded, and cyclic. A debt younger than :data:`DUE_GRACE_S` parks nobody — the start drain and
    the exit attempts get a full clock interval first. Then one stretch of :data:`DUE_PARK_MAX_S`
    parks everything; past it the park lifts with one ``NEEDS OPERATOR`` line and the factory runs
    for :data:`DUE_RESUME_S` before another stretch may start. The debt is never dropped here."""
    data = due(installed, out=out, run=run)
    if data is None or (now or time.time()) - data['at'] < DUE_GRACE_S:
        return False
    now = now or time.time()
    since = data.get('parked_at')
    if not isinstance(since, (int, float)) or now - since > DUE_PARK_MAX_S + DUE_RESUME_S:
        data, since = _write_due(dict(data, parked_at=now, loud=False)), now   # a new stretch
    if now - since <= DUE_PARK_MAX_S:
        out(f'tick: waiting — {due_label(data)}; the floor is draining')
        return True
    if not data.get('loud'):
        _write_due(dict(data, loud=True))
        out(f'NEEDS OPERATOR: upgrade to {data["sha"][:7]} has not found an empty floor in '
            f'{int((now - data["at"]) // 60)} min — the ticks resume and the install is still '
            f'due (asf upgrade --to {data["sha"][:7]} --wait)')
    return False


def waiting(product_name, out=print, now=None, installed=None, run=subprocess.run):
    """True when this tick must not start: an install is running right now (C8), an upgrade owned
    by another product is parked (unchanged), or the install this factory owes is draining the
    floor — which parks the owner too, from :data:`DUE_GRACE_S` on."""
    if installing():
        out('tick: waiting — an install is running')
        return True
    data = pending(now, installed, out=out, run=run)
    if data is not None and data.get('owner') != product_name:
        out(f'tick: waiting — upgrade to {data["sha"][:7]} pending')
        return True
    return draining(out=out, now=now, installed=installed, run=run)
```

`pending()` is still called first and unconditionally, so its expiry, its `NEEDS OPERATOR` line and
its cool-down fire exactly as they do today; the owner still reads `False` from that branch. What
is new is the third clause, and the fact that the owner is in it.

### 4. `asf/tick/tick.py` — the tick's last act

`cmd_tick`'s body becomes `_tick(args, product)` verbatim, and the wrapper owns the exit:

```python
def cmd_tick(args, root=None):
    product = env.load_product(getattr(args, 'product', None))
    if getattr(args, 'dry_run', False):
        from asf.tick import dry_run
        return dry_run.run(product, fresh=getattr(args, 'fresh', False))  # installs nothing, ever
    try:
        return _tick(args, product)
    finally:
        # F-0207: the tick's last act. Every clock fires on the minute, so this exit is the
        # emptiest the floor gets — emptier than the start, where the install is tried today. It
        # runs after every step, the state commit and the summary, and nothing in the tick imports
        # after it: the package may be a new one by the time it returns.
        from asf import upgrade
        try:
            upgrade.install_due(product.name)
        except Exception as e:  # noqa: BLE001 — an install at the exit never fails a tick
            print(f'upgrade: the exit install did not run ({type(e).__name__}: {e})')
```

It is in a `finally`, so it covers all of them: the parked return (`upgrade.waiting`), the
`another tick of <p> is running — skipped` return, the `StepError` return, the `--shadow` return,
the ordinary one, and a raise. Because the whole of today's body moves into `_tick`, the product
tick lock is already closed when the attempt runs — an install must not hold a lock the next tick
waits 45 minutes for. A parked tick is the cheapest installer the factory has: it does no work at
all, and it exits into a floor one process smaller than when it started.

### 5. `asf/tick/step_harvest.py` — the gate suppresses and hands over

## Acceptance tests

All new classes are in `tests/test_install.py`, which owns this code (P12), beside
`PendingUpgradeTest` and `DrainTest`.

**The debt's own rules.** `DueRecordTest` (a `HomeCase`): a tick's floor-deferral writes
`upgrade-due.json` with the sha, the owner, an `at` within a minute of now and `passes: 1`, beside
the park marker; a second deferral increments `passes` and keeps the first `at`; a deferral for a
*moved* head starts a fresh record (`passes: 1`, a new `at`, no `parked_at`); the debt is written
even while `cooling()` refuses a new park marker, and the run says both (`no pending mark until` and
`upgrade: due`); `due()` returns the record unchanged after `10 * PENDING_TTL_S` (nothing expires

prefix; `settle_due` clears it for `0`, clears it silently for `DEFERRED` and clears it with the
`NEEDS OPERATOR: the install of … failed (exit 1)` line for any other code, and leaves a record
whose sha is a different one alone; a full `cmd_upgrade` run whose pipx succeeds leaves neither

**The drain window.** `DrainWindowTest`: inside `DUE_GRACE_S` the owner's tick runs
(`waiting('asf')` False) while another product's is parked by the marker exactly as today; at
`DUE_GRACE_S` every product parks, the owner included, with the `…; the floor is draining` line,
and `parked_at` is stamped once and not re-stamped on the next call; past `DUE_PARK_MAX_S` the park
lifts, prints the `NEEDS OPERATOR: … has not found an empty floor in 15 min` line once, sets
`loud`, and does not print it again on the following call; past `DUE_PARK_MAX_S + DUE_RESUME_S` a
new stretch parks again with `loud` cleared; the debt is still on disk after all of it; and a debt

**The card, end to end.** `StarvedUpgradeTest` — the test the card names, and the one that would
have caught it. It drives `tick.cmd_tick` for the owner over N clock passes against a scripted
`SequencedRun` and a fake clock, with `drift.check` reporting the head as `5cab3a2`-shaped drift:

* *the incident.* Every tick **start** sees another asf pid, for every pass — the floor is never
  empty on the aligned minute. Asserted: the install runs, by pass 3 at the latest; it runs from an
  exit and not from a start (the pipx call is preceded by `the floor is empty at this exit`, or by
  the draining line for the passes before it); `upgrade-due.json` and `upgrade-pending.json` are
  both gone afterwards; and no line after the install says `deferred`. On the code as it stands
  this same script installs nothing at all, at any N — that is the defect this class pins.
* *the floor that will not empty by itself.* Every start and every exit sees another pid until the
  park engages, after which the script empties (the parked ticks spawn nothing and the running one
  ends). Asserted: from `DUE_GRACE_S` on every tick prints the draining line and runs no step, the
  install happens within `DUE_PARK_MAX_S` of the grace, and `passes` in the record at the moment of
  the install is ≤ the pass bound the class states.
* *the cool-down hole.* With `upgrade-expired.json` freshly written (so `write_pending` is refused)
  the debt is still recorded, still drains and still installs — the sequence that let the incident
  restart from a worse position.

## Records

| What | Where | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`state/upgrade-due.json`** (new) | `~/.ASF/state/`, one per ASF home | The install this factory owes: `sha`, `owner` (the product whose tick read the head), `at` (when it was first owed — what the grace and the stretch measure), `passes` (attempts that found the floor busy), and, once a stretch is running, `parked_at` and `loud`. Written by a tick's floor-deferral and by every busy exit attempt; removed only when the sha is installed or contained by the install, when the target is refused, or when an install failed. | **`state/upgrade-last.json`** | unchanged | The last install, for the batching interval. |
| **The tick's log** | stdout, per tick | New lines: `upgrade: due <sha> — pass N, owed by <product>` (a start deferral); `upgrade: due <sha> — pass N, the floor is busy (pid …)` (a busy exit); `upgrade: the floor is empty at this exit — installing <sha>`; `tick: waiting — upgrade to <sha> due since HH:MM — pass N (owner <p>); the floor is draining`; `tick: waiting — an install is running`; `upgrade: another install is running — nothing done here`. Every line that exists today keeps its wording, the harvest's suppression line included. |
| **`NEEDS OPERATOR`** | stdout, at most once per park stretch and once per failed install | `upgrade to <sha> has not found an empty floor in N min — the ticks resume and the install is still due (asf upgrade --to <sha> --wait)`, and `the install of <sha> failed (exit N) — the ticks resume; rerun asf upgrade --to <sha> --wait once it is fixed`. |

## Stories

- S: An owed install is a durable record, not a park that expires — `state/upgrade-due.json` with `sha`, `owner`, `at` and `passes`, written by a tick's floor-deferral even while a cool-down refuses a new park marker, keeping its first `at` across passes and starting fresh when the head moves, removed only when the sha is installed or contained by the install, when the target is refused, or when an install failed (with the `NEEDS OPERATOR` line for that last one), and never by age — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_install.DueRecordTest`
- S: Whichever process finishes last takes the install, and no tick runs during one — `install_due(product)` installing when the floor is empty at an exit and counting a named pass when it is not, only for the debt's owner, serialised by a non-blocking `flock` on `state/upgrade.lock` that `waiting()` also reads, called as the last act of every `cmd_tick` path (never under `--dry-run`) and of the background harvest's `main` — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_install.ExitInstallTest`
- S: The gap is made, not waited for — a bounded, cyclic drain window that parks every product including the debt's owner from `DUE_GRACE_S` on, lifts after `DUE_PARK_MAX_S` with one `NEEDS OPERATOR` line and re-arms after `DUE_RESUME_S`, always leaving the debt in place, with the background harvest's suppression moved onto the debt so nothing new joins the floor while it drains — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_install.DrainWindowTest tests.test_tick_steps.HarvestStepTests`
- S: Overlapping owner ticks install the pending upgrade within a stated number of passes, and never defer for ever — the card's own scenario driven over N clock passes: a floor that is busy at every tick start, a floor that empties only once the park engages, and the cool-down window that let the incident restart from a worse position, each ending in one `pipx install --force` at the named sha with both records gone — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_install.StarvedUpgradeTest`
- S: A factory that owes an install says so where it already says whether it is ticking — the doctor's `SCHEDULER` section and the status `Cron` row reading `read_due()` behind the park they report, with `upgrade to <sha> due since HH:MM — pass N (owner <p>)`, no git in either view, and every existing park row unchanged — acceptance: `python3 -m unittest -v tests.test_doctor.TestSchedulerSection tests.test_views.StatusViewTests`
