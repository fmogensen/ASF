# F-0255 — two idle boxes, a twenty-minute queue, and nothing connecting them

The incident in the card is not a bug in any one reader. Every reader did its job:

* `drift` said `undeclared: <name> is registered but not in ci.pool` about both idle runners — and
  was ignored, because that row says nothing about cost.
* `runner_classes.judge` said nothing, correctly: runners *did* carry
  `self-hosted,<provider>-heavy,class-pr-heavy`. They were simply all busy.
* The CI queue's relief machinery did what it can do — cancel runs *ahead* of the starved job. It
  cannot add a label, so it could not fix this.

Nobody owned the sentence that mattered: **these two boxes are idle, they are the same class as
the boxes this job runs on, and they are one label short of being able to take it.** That sentence
needs three facts at once — the live queue, the live runners' idle flag, and the declared pool —
and no reader in the tree holds all three.

It also needs a brake. The two runners were off the heavy label *on purpose*: they OOM-killed. A
reader that suggests re-adding a label an operator deliberately removed is worse than silence,
because it will be obeyed. So the declaration comes first: a pool entry can say which labels it
must never carry, **and why**, and the reader renders that as a held row instead of a suggestion.

And the finding is invisible until the runners are in `ci.pool` at all — which, in the incident,
they were not. That is the second half of the card and it is not decoration: it is what makes the
first half able to see the boxes it is about.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **The whole change is in `asf/ci_pool.py`.** The declaration, the reader, the reconcile half. | A new `asf/ci_shortfall.py`; or the reader in `asf/runner_classes.py`, which already owns required-jobs × runner-classes. | `runner_classes` is pinned all-required (P4) and its one useful export, `required_names`, is a one-line local import. | C2 | **The candidate's class is `ci_queue.class_key` — the declared `class:`, else the `role`.** | `runner_classes.runner_class` (`cls or provider/role`). | P6: the provider label is the thing the finding is *about*. | C3 | **A queued job's labels come from the host's own job record (P7), not from `runs_on`.** | Resolve the job to its workflow's `runs-on` via `runner_classes.job_for` + `resolved_runs_on`. | The host has already resolved `${{ vars.X }}`, `matrix` and `fromJSON(format(…))` — it had to, to queue the job. | C4 | **Exactly one missing label.** A candidate is flagged only when it carries every label the job asks for *but one*. | Any gap, with the missing set named; or a gap of ≤ 2. | "Lacked only the provider-heavy label" is the card's own sentence, and one missing label is the only size that is *actionable*: add this label and this job starts. Two or more is a different box, not a mislabelled one. |
| C5 | **The candidate must be in a class that already serves the job.** The classes that serve a queued job are those of the runners carrying all its labels; a candidate's class must be one of them. | Any idle runner one label short. | Without it the finding fires in every healthy pool: a `light` runner is one label (`heavy`) short of every heavy job, by design. | C7 | **A live runner that `ci.pool` does not declare is still a candidate**, when the class its `class-<name>` label names is a class the pool declares. A second knob for the same judgement is a second thing to tune wrong. |
| C9 | **`exclude_labels` is a map, label → reason, and the reason is required.** A string, a list, an empty reason, a non-scalar reason: all refused by `pool_problems`, loudly, on every product load. | `exclude_labels: [heavy]` with a sibling `exclude_reason: <text>`. | The acceptance is `held: <reason>`, which has to name the reason for *that* label; a single sibling field cannot when two labels are excluded for two causes. | C13 | **A held exclusion is still a row** — advisory and `ok=True`. | Say nothing at all when the candidate is excluded. | The caveat in the card is the point: an operator looking at a 20-minute queue and two idle boxes needs to be told *why nothing is being suggested*, or they will add the label by hand. Silence is how the OOM comes back. |
| C14 | **`asf ci reconcile --declare` writes the product file, and refuses while `ASF_JOB` is set**, naming the rule. | C15 | **The write is additive-only, pre-validated, and refuses as a whole.** Rendered entries are appended to the existing `ci.pool:` list as new lines; the merged list is put through `_pool_problems` first and nothing is written unless it is clean. | Write per runner, best effort. | P11: there is no round-trip, so the only safe edit is one that adds lines and touches none. `provider` and `role` are `POOL_REQUIRED`, so an entry whose values cannot be derived would make the product file *invalid on every subsequent load* — a command that can brick the operator's product file is not a convenience. |

| C17 | **`--declare` refuses under `ci.pool: discover`.** | Let it mint declared entries from the census. | Under `discover` the pool *is* the census and the tiers are `ci_census`'s to place; a declared list written underneath it would be a second source of truth for the same runners. |

### In, and out

**In.**

1. `exclude_labels` as a pool-entry field: on `PoolEntry`, in `POOL_FIELDS`, validated by
   `_pool_problems`, documented in `docs/products.example.yaml`.
2. `Backend.queued_jobs()` (default `()`) and its `GitHubBackend` implementation.
3. `label_gap()` — the reader — spliced into `ci_pool.doctor_rows` and printed by `tick_reserve`.
4. `plan`: an excluded label is removed, never blocked (C11).
5. `asf ci reconcile`: the undeclared-runner listing, and `--declare`.

**Out.**

* (a) **The reserve step is the only tick pass that carries this finding.** `ci_pool.tick` calls
  `tick_reserve` only when the product declares `ci.reserve`; a pooled product without one sees
  the finding in `asf doctor` alone. The card names the reserve step, and the alternative — a
  queued-jobs read on every tick of every pooled product — is a standing cost for a finding that
  is silent almost always.
* (b) **No row for an excluded label a runner currently carries.** `plan` already removes it
  (C11 closes the one hole), so the machinery enforces the declaration without a second voice.
* (c) **`drift`'s signature and rows are unchanged** (P3), and so is `runner_classes` except for
  one function being called by a new caller.
* (d) **No new config key, no new state file, no new `asf` command, no new module** (C1, C8).
* (e) **`--declare` cannot bootstrap an empty pool.** `cmd_reconcile` already returns early on
  `not pool`; `asf init` is the bootstrap and stays it.
* (f) **Nothing is suggested to the CI host.** Not one label write is added anywhere: the finding
  is a sentence, and `apply` is the only thing that writes labels, unchanged.

## The design

### §1 The declaration — `exclude_labels` on a pool entry

```yaml
ci:
  pool:
    - {runner: ci-1,  box: box-1, provider: alpha, size: 8c-16g, role: heavy, slots: 1}
    - {runner: ci-1b, box: box-1, provider: alpha, size: 8c-16g, role: light}
    - {runner: ci-2,  box: box-2, provider: beta,  size: 4c-8g,  role: light,
       exclude_labels: {heavy: out-of-memory kills on a 4c-8g box}}
```

`POOL_FIELDS` gains `'exclude_labels'` (it is not in `POOL_REQUIRED`). `PoolEntry` gains

```python
    #: ``exclude_labels`` in the product file: ``{label: reason}``, the labels this runner must
    #: never carry and why. Never a label the entry itself derives (C10), so :func:`labels` and
    #: the reconcile's target set are untouched by it.
    exclude: dict = dataclasses.field(default_factory=dict)

    def excluded(self, label):
        """The reason ``label`` is excluded on this runner, or '' when it is not."""
        return self.exclude.get(_norm(label), '')
```

`labels()` is unchanged — an exclusion names no label the entry derives, so the derivation cannot
contradict it. `load_pool` reads the field with both keys normalised through `_norm` and the
reason kept verbatim; a non-dict value yields `{}` there (the loader never raises — the refusal
is `_pool_problems`', on every product load).

`_pool_problems` gains, per entry, with `key = f'ci.pool[{i}]'`:

| condition | message at |
| --- | --- |
| not a mapping | `{key}.exclude_labels` — `must be a map of label to reason, not {v!r}` |
| a label that fails `LABEL_RE` | `{key}.exclude_labels.{label}` — `must be one label (letters, digits, . _ -)` |
| a reason that is empty, or not a scalar | `{key}.exclude_labels.{label}` — `needs a reason — an exclusion nobody can read is how the label comes back` |
| the label is the entry's own `role` | `{key}.exclude_labels.{label}` — `is this runner's declared role — a role is removed by changing role:, not by excluding it` |
| the label is the entry's own `class-<cls>` | `{key}.exclude_labels.{label}` — `is this runner's declared class label` |
| the label is a default label (`DEFAULT_LABELS`) | `{key}.exclude_labels.{label}` — `is a label the host sets itself and never removes` |

The example in `docs/products.example.yaml` gains the `ci-2` entry above and a comment naming
what the field is for — the comment block over `pool:` already explains the derived labels, and
this is the one exception to them.

### §2 The host read — `Backend.queued_jobs()`

On `Backend`, beside `variables()` and for the same reason (P2):

```python
    def queued_jobs(self):
        """Every job the host reports queued, oldest first: ``{'name', 'labels', 'created_at',
        'run_id', 'url'}``. ``()`` for a host that cannot say — which is also the safe answer:
        nothing queued is no finding. A host that *has* a queue and could not be asked raises
        :class:`BackendError`."""
        return ()
```

`GitHubBackend.queued_jobs()` follows `jobs_on`'s two-step shape — queued runs, then each run's
queued jobs:

```python
    def queued_jobs(self):
        if not self.slug:
            return []
        runs = self._lines([f'repos/{self.slug}/actions/runs?status=queued&per_page=100',
                            '--jq', '.workflow_runs[].id'])
        out = []
        for run_id in runs:
            out += [{**j, 'run_id': run_id} for j in self._lines(
                [f'repos/{self.slug}/actions/runs/{run_id}/jobs?per_page=100', '--jq',
                 '.jobs[] | select(.status == "queued")'
                 ' | {name, labels, created_at, url: .html_url}'])]
        return sorted(out, key=lambda j: j.get('created_at') or '')
```

The first call is the cheap discriminator: on a quiet host it returns no runs and there is no
second call. `_api`'s read memoisation and `gh_limit.guard` apply unchanged.

### §3 The reader — `label_gap`

```python
def label_gap(pool, runners, queued, required=(), wait_min=20, now=None, out_of_pool=True):
    """``[(ok, detail)]`` — one row per idle runner that is one label short of a queued required
    job of its own class, and one per such runner the pool holds back by a declared exclusion
    (``ok=True``: a held row, never a suggestion). ``[]`` when nothing is queued long enough,
    which is almost always.

    ``queued``: :meth:`Backend.queued_jobs`. ``required``: the required job names
    (:func:`asf.runner_classes.required_names`); empty means every queued job counts, as it does
    for the queue's own starvation rule. ``wait_min``: a job queued less than this is not yet a
    finding (C8).
    """
```

The judgement, in order:

1. **The jobs.** Each entry of `queued` whose `deploy.job_key(name)` is in `required` (every one
   when `required` is empty), whose `created_at` is more than `wait_min` minutes before `now`,
   and whose labels (`ci_queue._job_labels`) contain `self-hosted`. Oldest first. Nothing left:
   return `[]` — no further work, no further call.
2. **The classes that serve it.** `declared = {e.runner: e for e in pool}`. A live runner
   *serves* the job when its `norm_labels()` is a superset of the job's labels. The serving class
   set is `{class_key(e) for each serving runner the pool declares}` (`ci_queue.class_key`, C2).
   Empty: skip this job (C6).
3. **The candidates.** For each live runner `r` with `r.online and not r.busy`:
   * its class is `class_key(declared[r.name])` when the pool declares it; else, when
     `out_of_pool`, the class named by its single `class-<name>` label if the pool declares that
     class; else it is no candidate (C7);
   * its class must be in the serving set (C5);
   * `missing = job.labels - r.norm_labels()` must be exactly one label (C4), and that label must
     not be `is_default(label, r)` — a label the host owns is never suggested.
4. **The row.** With `e = declared.get(r.name)` and `why = e.excluded(label) if e else ''`:

```
label gap: ci-2 (class light, idle) lacks 'alpha-heavy' for the queued required job
  gate-heavy — held: out-of-memory kills on a 4c-8g box
```

```
label gap: ci-7 (class pr-heavy, idle) lacks 'alpha-heavy', which the queued required job
  gate-heavy has waited 23m for — asf ci reconcile
```

```
label gap: ci-7 (class pr-heavy, idle, not in ci.pool) lacks 'alpha-heavy', which the queued
  required job gate-heavy has waited 23m for — asf ci reconcile --declare first
```

(Each is one line in reality; wrapped here.) The held row is `(True, …)`, the two suggestions
`(False, …)`. A (runner, label) pair is reported once, against the oldest job that wants it.
Every minute figure is `m`-suffixed and no row carries a bare seconds token.

### §4 Where the reader is called

**`ci_pool.doctor_rows`** — after the `drift` rows, before `reserve_rows`, as advisory rows
(C12), and only in declared mode:

```python
    rows = [(True, ok, detail) for ok, detail in drift(...)]
    rows += [(False, ok, detail) for ok, detail in _gap_rows(product, backend, runners)]
    return rows + reserve_rows(product, runners, pool) + classes
```

`_gap_rows(product, backend, runners)` is the one adapter: `[]` unless
`pool_mode(product) == 'declared'`; `backend.queued_jobs()` in its **own** `try`, a
`BackendError` becoming the single row
`(None, f'label gap: cannot read the host\'s queued jobs — {e}')` so the drift rows above it
survive (P5's `check_ci_pool` wraps everything in one `except` — a raise here would erase them);
then `required_names(product)` and `trunk_wait_min(product)`, both behind a non-empty queue, both
local imports.

**`ci_pool.tick_reserve`** — after the reserve lines, from the runners it has already read:

```python
    for _ok, detail in _gap_rows(product, backend, runners):
        out(f'ci reserve: {detail}')
```

Nothing is written, nothing is raised, and on a quiet host this is one `gh` call that returns no
runs.

### §5 `asf ci reconcile` declares the live runners

`Step` gains nothing. The undeclared branch of `plan` keeps its `Step` but its note becomes the
verdict of the derivation, from a new pure function:

```python
def declare_entry(r, pool):
    """``(rendered line, why)`` for a live runner ``r`` the pool does not declare: the
    ``ci.pool`` entry it would be, or ``('', why)`` naming what cannot be derived.

    ``provider`` comes from the one ``provider-<name>`` label it carries, else from the pool's
    single declared provider when there is only one; ``role`` from the one label of its own that
    is a declared role (:func:`roles`); ``class`` from a ``class-<name>`` label when it carries
    one; ``box`` and ``size`` are inventory the host does not hold and are left out.
    """
```

Rendered, at the indentation of the list's existing items:

```
    - {runner: ci-7, provider: alpha, role: heavy}  # box/size: inventory, not on the host
```

`cmd_reconcile` prints, after the plan table and its existing lines:

```
2 live runner(s) missing from ci.pool; --declare writes the 1 that is complete into
/…/products/p.yaml:
    - {runner: ci-7, provider: alpha, role: heavy}  # box/size: inventory, not on the host
NEEDS OPERATOR: ci-9 carries no label that is a declared role (heavy, light) — add it to ci.pool by hand
```

and with `--declare`, in this order, each refusal one line and `rc=2`:

| guard | line |
| --- | --- |
| `ASF_JOB` set (P12, C14) | `ci reconcile: --declare refused — a session never edits the file that holds its own authority; run it at the console` |

The insertion is pure and testable on its own:

```python
def pool_insert(text, lines):
    """``(new text, why)`` — ``lines`` added as new items at the end of the ``ci.pool:`` list in
    a product file's ``text``, every other byte untouched (P11). ``why`` is non-empty and the
    text unchanged when there is no ``pool:`` under a top-level ``ci:``, or no ``- `` item under
    it to take the indentation from (``pool: discover``, an empty list).

    The scan tracks the current top-level key (a line matching ``^(\\w[\\w-]*):``) and takes the
    first ``pool:`` under ``ci:``; from there it walks the item lines, their continuations, and
    any blank or comment line between them, and inserts before the first line whose indentation
    is at or left of ``pool:``'s. ``env.key_line`` is not reused: it answers from a path, and
    this function edits the text it is given.
    """
```

`--declare` is registered beside `--apply`:

```python
    r.add_argument('--declare', action='store_true',
                   help='write the live runners missing from ci.pool into the product file as '
                        'declared entries; default is the listing alone')
```

`--apply` and `--declare` are independent and may be given together: `--declare` acts on the
product file, `--apply` on the host's labels, and the file write happens first so the labels the
plan writes are the ones the file now declares.

### What it looks like when it is there

The incident, replayed. Before the declare, `asf doctor`:

```
ci pool   RED   undeclared: ci-7 is registered but not in ci.pool
ci pool   RED   undeclared: ci-8 is registered but not in ci.pool
ci pool   WARN  label gap: ci-7 (class pr-heavy, idle, not in ci.pool) lacks 'alpha-heavy', which the queued required job gate-heavy has waited 23m for — asf ci reconcile --declare first
ci pool   WARN  label gap: ci-8 (class pr-heavy, idle, not in ci.pool) lacks 'alpha-heavy', which the queued required job gate-heavy has waited 23m for — asf ci reconcile --declare first
```

After `asf ci reconcile --declare` and an operator who decides the boxes really are too small,
`ci.pool` carries `exclude_labels: {alpha-heavy: out-of-memory kills on a 4c-8g box}` and the same
doctor says:

```
ci pool   OK    label gap: ci-7 (class pr-heavy, idle) lacks 'alpha-heavy' for the queued required job gate-heavy — held: out-of-memory kills on a 4c-8g box
```

Which is the answer the incident needed: not "add the label", but **"these two boxes cannot take
this job, here is why, buy bigger ones"** — on the doctor, every time the queue starves, without
anybody having to remember the reason.

## Records

* **`~/.ASF/products/<p>.yaml`** — the one file this change writes, and only under
  `asf ci reconcile --declare` at a console (C14). The write is additive: new `- {runner: …}`
  lines at the end of the existing `ci.pool:` list, every other byte untouched (C15, P11). It is
  the operator's file, not the record; nothing in the factory reads a `ci.pool` entry it did not
  already read. `exclude_labels` is written there **by hand, never by this command** — the
  reason is a human sentence and `--declare` has nothing to put in it.
* **`asf doctor`, the `ci pool` rows** — new advisory rows, red for a suggestion and green for a
  held exclusion (C12, C13), spliced between the `drift` rows and the `reserve` rows. The label
  (`'ci pool'`) and the required flag of every existing row are unchanged, and `asf/doctor.py`
  gains no line (P5).
* **The tick's log, the `health` step** — one `ci reserve: label gap: …` line per finding, from
  `tick_reserve`, only for a product that declares `ci.reserve` (Out (a)). Minutes are
  `m`-suffixed; no line carries a bare seconds token.
* **`asf ci reconcile` stdout** — the plan table keeps its four columns and every declared
  runner's row is byte-for-byte what it is today. An undeclared runner's `action` cell changes
  from `not in ci.pool — untouched` to the verdict of its derivation, and a new block after the
  table lists the entries `--declare` would write plus a `NEEDS OPERATOR` line per runner that
  cannot be derived (C16).
* **No new state file, no new config key, no new metrics stream, no new record item.** The
  finding is computed from the host and the product file every time it is asked for, and is never
  stored — it is live state, and a stored copy of live state is a lie waiting to be read
  (C12's reason, in the other direction).
* **`docs/products.example.yaml`** — one new pool entry carrying `exclude_labels`, and the
  comment that says what it is for. It stays valid under `env.validate_product_text` (P15).
* **`docs/guide/ci-runner-pool.md`** — the guide the example's comment points at gains the
  exclusion and the two new reconcile behaviours; no other doc changes.
