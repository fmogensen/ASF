# F-0026 — the boundary holds, and footprint is not grant

The prototype's writes boundary snapshotted the tree and reverted every write outside the
declared footprint, **keeping the reverted content out of the branch**. Three runs died on a
footprint that was merely incomplete: the work was done, the boundary threw it away, and the next
run started from nothing. The Constitution already names this as the failure — *"its writes
boundary detected and reverted rather than preventing"*, and *"a capability list is not a
boundary"* (`docs/CONSTITUTION.md:78-85`). The prototype's worst single boundary defect (b122)
was a second thing: `writes:` meant both *what sizes a wave* and *what a session may write*, and
when the two disagreed the frontier said two items were parallel while the boundary granted each
of them the other's files (`docs/research/prior-art-prototype.md:117-119`).

Here is what this factory has today.

| Today | What it costs |
| --- | --- |
| **Nothing checks that a branch's diff stays inside its Task's `writes:`.** The only consumers of a footprint are the feeder's wave gate (`asf/feeder/rows.py:393`), the groom's shape proposals (`asf/groom/shape.py`), `asf check`'s Active-overlap finding (`asf/record/check.py:195`), and red-test *attribution* (`asf/harvest/harvest.py:973`). A branch may land a file no card ever declared. | the card's first acceptance line: nothing is held, so nothing is detected |
| **`writes:` is documented as the grant.** The plan template says `writes: <the path globs this Task's diff may touch>` (`asf/briefs/templates/plan.md:14`); the coder's brief says `THE BOUNDARY IS writes:` (`asf/briefs/templates/coder.md:7`); the generated preamble says `Writes (the footprint this job may touch)` (`asf/briefs/preamble.py:396`) — one line naming both meanings at once. | b122 verbatim: widening the footprint to fit the work serialises the wave, so nobody widens it, and the work lands outside it anyway |
| **A spec, plan or review lane has no footprint at all and cannot be given one.** Its item is a Feature, and `asf check` *rejects* `writes:` on a Feature (`asf/record/check.py:170`). Its brief prints `(none declared)`; its boundary is a sentence in a template ("Create only that file"). | a doc lane can edit any file in the repo and land it behind the docs-only gate, which runs no test command (`asf/harvest/harvest.py:845`) |
| **One path already discards content.** A branch whose deliverable is on the trunk is marked landed and *deleted*, its other files named in one line and dropped with it (`asf/harvest/harvest.py:878-890`: `not its deliverable, dropped with the branch`). | the card's second acceptance line, today, in the one place the boundary already has an opinion |

This spec adds the check the card asks for, in the form the card asks for — **hold, list, never
revert** — and splits the one field into two with a stated containment law, so that the wave's
disjointness test remains a sound proof that no two live sessions write the same file.

## 1. Decisions

### 1.1 In

- **`asf/boundary.py`** — a new leaf module, pure functions over strings, no filesystem and no
  git, beside `asf/feeder/footprint.py` in spirit and importing only `re` and
  `asf.conventions`. It owns: `covers(glob, path)`, `outside(paths, grant)`,
  `within(inner, outer)` (the containment law), `deliverable_of(conv, branch, item)` (moved here
  from harvest, its one caller updated), `grant_for(conv, branch, item, card)` and
  `refusal(conv, branch, item, card, touched)`.
- **`grant:`** — a new optional typed field on a Task and on a Feature: the path globs a session
  on that item's branch may write. Absent on a Task → the grant *is* the footprint (today's
  behaviour, unchanged, no card edits needed). Absent on a Feature → the grant is the lane's
  deliverable and the item's review files (§2.3).
- **The containment law** — a grant is never wider than the footprint that reserves it. Enforced
  statically by `asf check`: a Task whose `grant:` leaves its `writes:`, and two Active Tasks
  whose grants overlap, are findings with a line number.
- **Harvest's boundary screen** — one call in `run_product_harvest`'s eligible loop, after the
  lane refusal and **before** the approvals class, the rebase and the gate: a branch whose diff
  names a file outside its grant is held with a `boundary` correction whose text lists the extra
  paths, and never enters the gated set.
- **Nothing is discarded** — the held branch keeps every commit and stays on origin, as every
  hold already does; and `land_already`'s `extras` stop being deleted with the branch. That
  branch is archived to `archive/<branch>` by the machinery that already exists for a superseded
  branch (`archive_commit`, `archive_superseded`), so the content survives the landing.
- **The groom's list** — a `Branches held at the writes boundary` section listing item, branch
  and the out-of-grant paths, answerable with one new word, `grant <glob>`, which appends a glob
  to the card's `grant:` with a history line.
- **The held branch lands without a new session** — when the card's grant has grown to cover the
  diff, harvest clears the `boundary` correction itself and gates the branch in the same tick.
  A hold that can be answered by widening a field must not also cost a session.
- **The brief says both** — the preamble prints two lines, `Footprint (what sizes the wave)` and
  `Grant (what this session may write)`; the coder and fixer templates name the grant as the
  boundary; the plan template documents the optional fourth Task line.
- **`conventions.boundary`** — `hold` (the default), `warn` (print the extra paths, land anyway)
  or `off`. A factory whose cards under-declare their footprints can adopt the check without
  holding half its lane on the first tick — which is the same failure the prototype had, arrived
  at from the other side.
- Tests: the matcher (§3.1), the resolver and the law (§3.2), the hold and the re-admission
  (§3.3), the archive (§3.4), the groom (§3.5), the briefs (§3.6).

### 1.2 Out

- **Preventing the write.** Nothing here stops a session mid-turn. The Constitution's *"a
  capability list is not a boundary"* is about not *trusting* a declaration; the runtime-level
  refusal is the approvals hook's job (`asf/approvals.py`, F-0031/F-0062) and is out of this
  card. This is a **landing** boundary: not one file is prevented, and not one file lands
  undeclared.
- **Reverting, resetting, `checkout --`, `git revert`, stripping a commit, rewriting a branch,
  dropping a file from a diff.** The word the card uses is *hold*. No code in this spec removes
  content from a branch, and §3.4 is the test that says so.
- **Deleting a held branch, ever.** A hold leaves origin exactly as the session left it.
- **A boundary on the record repo's own branches** (`run_harvest`, the `--repo` path,
  `asf/harvest/harvest.py:563`). Those are the record's own commits — cards, groom files, the
  index — and no Task footprint describes them. Stated here so the omission is a decision and
  not an oversight.
- **Changing what sizes a wave.** The feeder keeps gating on `writes:`
  (`asf/feeder/rows.py:393`, `running_footprints` at `:296`). The containment law is what makes
  that still correct; nothing in the feeder changes.
- **A new metrics stream or event.** A boundary hold is a correction like any other, visible in
  `sessions.jsonl` and in the session's `result`/`reason` where a hold already is. Counting
  boundary holds per week is a rollup question, and this card is not the rollup.
- **Making `grant:` required.** A required field means every existing Task card is invalid on the
  day this lands. Absent means "the footprint", which is what every card today already says.
- **Widening `writes:` to mean the grant.** That is the defect, not the fix: two Tasks whose
  footprints overlap never run together, so a footprint widened to cover a session's real reach
  costs the factory its parallelism, and b122 is what happens when nobody pays that cost.
- **Renaming `writes:`.** The field is in every card, every plan, `asf new --writes`, the plan
  parser and four modules. The *documentation* of what it means changes; the key does not.
- **`job_grants`** (`asf/briefs/build.py:170`) — the operator's list of directories a session may
  read outside its worktree. Different thing, different scope (operator config, not a card),
  untouched. §2.1 names the distinction so the two are never conflated in prose again.

## 2. The design

### 2.1 Two words, and which is which

Both fields are lists of path globs, relative to the product repo's root.

| | `writes:` — the **footprint** | `grant:` — the **grant** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answers | *what does this item reserve?* | *what may this session write?* |
| Read by | the feeder's wave gate, the groom's merge/batch/split proposals, `asf check`'s Active-overlap finding | harvest's boundary screen, the brief, `asf check`'s containment findings |
| On a Task | required (unchanged) | optional; absent = the footprint |
| On a Feature | forbidden (unchanged) | optional; absent = the lane's deliverable and the item's review files |
| Widening it costs | parallelism — two overlapping footprints never run together | nothing, as long as it stays inside the footprint |
| The law | — | `grant ⊆ writes` wherever both exist |

Neither is `job_grants` (`asf/briefs/build.py:170`), which is the operator's list of directories
a session may **read** outside its worktree. That is operator config about the filesystem; these
two are card fields about the repo.

The law is the point. The feeder proves two Tasks may run together by showing their footprints do
not overlap; that is only a proof about *writes* if what each session may write is inside its own
footprint. With the law, footprint-disjoint implies grant-disjoint implies no two live sessions
write one file (R-0088). Without it — the prototype — the two statements are unrelated, and
b122 is the shape that disagreement takes.

### 2.2 `asf/boundary.py`

A leaf module: `import re`, and `asf.conventions` for the deliverable. No git, no filesystem, no
product. Every function is total and returns plain data.

```python
def covers(glob, path):
    """True when ``path`` (repo-relative) is inside ``glob``.

    ``*`` matches within one segment, ``**`` matches across ``/``, ``?`` one character, and a
    wildcard-free glob that is a prefix of ``path`` at a ``/`` boundary is a directory
    (``asf/harvest`` and ``asf/harvest/`` both cover ``asf/harvest/harvest.py``). Errs toward
    False: what this returns True for is what may land unexamined.
    """

def outside(paths, grant):
    """The members of ``paths`` no glob of ``grant`` covers, in ``paths`` order, deduplicated."""

def within(inner, outer):
    """The first glob of ``inner`` that no glob of ``outer`` contains, or None — the containment
    law. A glob contains another when it covers its literal head and is no narrower in its
    wildcards; the conservative test is that ``covers`` holds for the inner glob's own text.
    """
```

`covers` is a translation to one regex per glob, cached: `**` → `.*`, `*` → `[^/]*`, `?` →
`[^/]`, `[...]` kept as a class, every other character escaped, anchored at both ends; plus the
directory-prefix rule above. `fnmatch` is not used — P6 is why.

`deliverable_of(conv, branch, item)` moves here unchanged from `asf/harvest/harvest.py:850`, and
harvest's one call site (`already_on_trunk`, `:870`) imports it from here. It is a pure
conventions-and-strings function and it now has two callers.

### 2.3 The grant of a branch

```python
def grant_for(conv, branch, item, card):
    """The globs a session on ``branch`` may write, or None when none is declared.

    1. ``card['grant']``, when the card carries one — a Task's or a Feature's, as typed.
    2. else, for a lane whose deliverable is one document (``spec``, ``plan``):
       ``[deliverable_of(...), conv.review_path(slug, '*')]`` — the document it exists to write,
       and the review files of its own item, which later rounds write on the same branch.
    3. else, ``card['writes']`` — the footprint, which is what every Task card today means.
    4. else None: nothing in the record says what this branch may write, so nothing is asserted
       about it (§1.4, "a lane with neither a grant nor a card").
    """
```

`None` and `[]` are different answers and must stay so: `None` is *undeclared* and holds nothing;
`[]` would be *may write nothing*, which no card can currently express and which this spec does
not introduce.

```python
BOUNDARY_KIND = 'boundary'

def refusal(conv, branch, item, card, touched):
    """``(BOUNDARY_KIND, text)`` when ``touched`` leaves the branch's grant, else None."""
```

The text is one line, because a correction is one line (`first_failing_line`'s shape, and the
brief prints it):

```
writes outside its grant: asf/conventions.py, tests/test_env.py (+3 more) — the grant is
asf/harvest/**, tests/test_harvest.py. Nothing is reverted and nothing is lost: move those files
inside the grant, or leave them and ask the groom to widen it (`grant: <glob>` on T-0080)
```

At most five paths are named, then `(+N more)`; the full list goes on the correction as
`outside`, and that is what the groom prints (§2.6).

### 2.4 Harvest's screen

In `run_product_harvest`'s second loop (`asf/harvest/harvest.py:1288`), between the lane refusal
and the approvals class:

```python
        refusal = lane_refusal(repo, trunk, branch, item)
        if refusal:
            results[branch] = hold_with_correction(state_dir, branch, record, *refusal, out)
            continue
        card = (items or {}).get(item) or {}
        edge = boundary.refusal(conv, branch, item, card,
                                touched_files(repo, trunk, branch, renames=False))
        if edge and boundary.mode(conv) == boundary.HOLD:
            results[branch] = hold_with_correction(state_dir, branch, record, *edge, out,
                                                   outside=boundary.outside_of(edge))
            continue
        if edge:                                  # warn: named, then gated as usual
            out(f'boundary {branch}: {edge[1]}')
```

`touched_files` grows one keyword argument, `renames=True` by default, and passes
`--no-renames` when it is False (P5); every existing caller is unchanged.

`hold_with_correction` grows one keyword argument, `outside=()`, passed through to
`lifecycle.hold` as an `extra` dict merged into the correction — so the paths are on the record,
not only in the text. `lifecycle.hold(path, run, kind, text, now, empty_cap=…, extra=None)`
gains that one parameter and nothing else; `kind` is already free-form.

`mode(conv)` reads `conventions.boundary`, one of `hold` (default), `warn`, `off`; `off` skips
the read entirely, so a product that wants none pays nothing.

Order matters and is deliberate: the adjudicate-commit check and the lane refusal are about the
commits, the boundary is about the diff, and only then does the branch cost an approvals lookup,
a worktree, a rebase and a gate. An out-of-grant branch therefore never enters the combined head,
and can never land inside another branch's push.

### 2.5 Nothing is discarded

Two statements, both testable.

**A held branch is untouched.** A boundary hold writes one registry line and prints one line. It
does not push, delete, archive, reset or rewrite anything; `origin/<branch>` is the same sha
after the tick as before it. This is already true of every hold — the boundary adds no exception,
and §3.4 asserts the sha.

**`land_already` stops dropping extras.** Today, a branch whose deliverable is already on the
trunk is marked landed and the branch deleted, with its other files named in a parenthetical
(`asf/harvest/harvest.py:878-890`). With extras, it is archived instead:

```python
    if extras:
        sha = archive_commit(repo, branch, f'archive({item}): {branch} — its deliverable is on '
                                           f'{trunk}; kept for {", ".join(extras)} [skip ci]')
        # push sha:refs/heads/archive/<branch>, then delete the lane branch
        out(f'landed {branch}: already on {trunk} at {tip[:7]}; not its deliverable — '
            f'{", ".join(extras)} kept on archive/{branch}')
```

The push is the same fast-forward-only push `archive_superseded` makes, and an archive push that
is refused leaves the lane branch in place and prints one line — the branch is never deleted
before its archive exists. With no extras, the path is byte-for-byte what it is today.

### 2.6 The groom's list

One new section, last but one in `GROOM_SECTIONS` (before `Auto-filed Bugs not yet decided`):

```markdown
## Branches held at the writes boundary

- [ ] T-0080 outside its grant on worker/T-0080: asf/conventions.py, tests/test_env.py — grant: asf/harvest/**, tests/test_harvest.py → answer: ____
```

`build_groom_sections` gains one optional argument, `held_boundary=()` — a list of
`(item, branch, outside, grant)` — so the builder stays a pure function of its inputs.
`cmd_groom` fills it from the ledger it already reads (P10):
`lifecycle.corrections(pool.sessions_path(product))`, kept to the entries whose `kind` is
`boundary`, joined to the card's grant. No product, no ledger → an empty section, as for every
other section with nothing to say.

`_parse_answer` gains one word:

```python
ANSWER_GRANT = re.compile(r'^grant\s+(\S+)$', re.IGNORECASE)   # -> ('grant', '<glob>')
```

The applier appends the glob to the card's `grant:` (creating the field), writes the history line
`grant: + <glob>` with the usual attribution, and — because the law is checked, not assumed —
refuses a glob that leaves the Task's footprint, leaving the line unanswered with
`(refused: outside its writes:)` appended. `no` on such a line is the existing `removed:`
answer and is untouched; every other word is unrecognised, as it is today.

### 2.7 A widened grant lands the branch, with no new session

In `run_product_harvest`'s first loop, immediately before the `is_eligible` screen
(`asf/harvest/harvest.py:1183`):

```python
        pending = lifecycle.pending_correction(record, sessions_path(state_dir)) or {}
        if pending.get('kind') == boundary.BOUNDARY_KIND and not boundary.refusal(
                conv, branch, item, (items or {}).get(item) or {},
                touched_files(repo, trunk, branch, renames=False)):
            if not dry_run:
                mark_session(state_dir, record.get('job') or branch, correction=None)
            record = dict(record, correction=None)
            out(f're-admitted {branch}: its grant now covers the diff')
```

`correction=None` is the existing idiom for clearing one (P3), the in-memory record is updated so
the `is_eligible` call below sees the cleared state, and `rounds` is left as it stands. A dry run
prints the line and writes nothing. The branch is then gated in that same tick like any other.

This is the only place a correction is cleared by anything other than a session's next run, and
it is sound precisely because the question the hold asked was about the *record*: the record
answered it.

### 2.8 The brief

`preamble.collect` gains `grant` beside `writes`; `state_lines` replaces the one line
(`asf/briefs/preamble.py:396`) with two:

```
Footprint (what sizes the wave — two Tasks whose footprints overlap never run together): asf/harvest/**
Grant (what this session may write; the harvest holds a branch that leaves it): asf/harvest/**, tests/test_harvest.py
```

Each prints `(none declared)` when empty, as today. `facts['grant']` is `boundary.grant_for`'s
answer for the row's branch, so a spec session's brief names its one document instead of
`(none declared)` — the first time a doc lane is told its boundary by the generated preamble
rather than by a sentence in a template.

`asf/briefs/templates/coder.md:7` becomes `THE BOUNDARY IS YOUR GRANT — {grant}`, and
`fixer.md:8` `Stay inside your grant — {grant}`; `{writes}` stays available and both templates
keep naming the footprint where they explain the wave. The thirteen golden briefs are
regenerated in the same Task with `ASF_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` (P11), which is also the diff a reviewer
reads to see exactly what every session will now be told.

`asf/briefs/templates/plan.md` documents the optional fourth Task line:

```
    grant: <the globs this Task's session may write — default: the same as writes:>
```

with one sentence: `writes:` sizes the wave and may be generous; `grant:` is what the harvest
enforces and may never leave `writes:`.

### 2.9 The record's side

- `asf new task --grant GLOB` (repeatable, optional) beside `--writes`
  (`asf/record/new.py:45`), written into the card's typed block.
- `asf/record/plan_tasks.py:75` reads a `grant:` line where it reads `writes:`, through the same
  `writes_lines` helper generalised to a named key.
- `asf check` (`asf/record/check.py`) gains two findings, beside the Active-overlap one it
  already makes:
  - `T-0080: grant: 'asf/other.py' is outside its writes: footprint` — the containment law, per
    card, at the `grant` line;
  - `T-0080: grant: 'asf/x.py' intersects Active task T-0081's grant 'asf/x.py'` — two Active
    Tasks whose grants overlap. The footprint version of this finding stays exactly as it is;
    with the law held, this second one can only fire on cards that broke the law, which is why
    both are checked.
  The overlap test is `asf.boundary`'s, never a second copy — the same rule `asf/groom/shape.py:7`
  already states for the footprint.
- The record's generated README (`asf/init.py:84-88`) names both fields in its typed-fields
  sentence.

### 2.10 What does not change

## 4. Records

**A card, new field** — a Task, or a Feature:

```yaml
id: T-0080
type: task
title: …
writes: [asf/harvest/**, tests/test_harvest.py]   # the footprint: what sizes the wave
grant: [asf/harvest/boundary.py, tests/test_boundary.py]   # optional; what the session may write
```

**Config, new** — `products/<p>.yaml`, under `conventions:`:

```yaml
conventions:
  # how harvest treats a branch whose diff leaves its grant
  #   hold (default) — held, the extra diff listed for the groom, nothing reverted
  #   warn           — the extra diff printed, the branch gated and landed as usual
  #   off            — not checked
  boundary: hold
```

**The session record** (`~/.ASF/state/<product>/sessions.jsonl`) — one appended line per hold,
in the shape every hold already has, with one new key inside the correction:

```json
{"job": "code-t-0080", "rounds": 1,
 "correction": {"kind": "boundary", "at": "2026-09-24T06:40:00Z",
                "text": "writes outside its grant: asf/conventions.py … (+3 more) — …",
                "outside": ["asf/conventions.py", "tests/test_env.py", "asf/env.py"]}}
```

and one appended line per re-admission, `{"job": "code-t-0080", "correction": null}`.

**The groom file** (`groom/<date>.md`, in the record clone) — one new section,
`## Branches held at the writes boundary`, one line per held branch, answered with
`grant <glob>`.

**The card's history** — one line per granted glob, through the applier that writes every groom
answer: `- 2026-09-24 grant: + asf/conventions.py (controller: <name> grant asf/conventions.py)`.

**A git ref** — `archive/<branch>`, pushed only for a `land_already` that had extras, tipped with
an empty `[skip ci]` commit, exactly as a superseded branch is archived today (P7).

**The tick's output** — `held <branch>: writes outside its grant: …`,
`boundary <branch>: …` (warn), `re-admitted <branch>: its grant now covers the diff`, and
`landed <branch>: … ; not its deliverable — <files> kept on archive/<branch>`.

**Not written.** No new stream, no new state file, no new metrics event, no Bug, no field on any
card but the two named above, and nothing at all on a branch: the boundary reads the diff and
writes only the record.
