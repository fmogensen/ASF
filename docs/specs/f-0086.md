# F-0086 — The groom proposes merges, splits and batches — shape decides delivery speed

Epic: E-0001 — ASF 0.1 — a working factory. Branch: `spec/F-0086`.

Today the groom types and asks. It files the inbox, lists undecided cards, Features without
Stories, near-duplicate titles (`asf/groom/groom.py:239-248`), and it understands five answers:
`yes`, `no`/`close`, `rank N`, `parent X`, `S1-3` (`asf/groom/groom.py:34-52`). It never looks at
shape. A Task's shape is fixed when its plan is written: one `### Task N:` with a `stories:` and a
`writes:` line (`asf/briefs/templates/plan.md:9-17`). From then on that shape decides the lane's
speed:

- the feeder launches a Task only if its `writes:` overlaps no running Task
  (`asf/feeder/rows.py:317-335`). Two Tasks of one Feature that overlap run one after the other
  anyway, and each one still costs a session and a harvest gate;
- harvest gates once per branch, and one branch is one Task (`asf/harvest/harvest.py:1-35`), so a
  Task too small to be worth a session still costs a whole gate;
- a Task whose footprint spans two areas waits on whatever holds either area. A running Task in
  one area blocks the whole thing, including the part nobody else is touching.

After this change the daily groom reads the ready Tasks' footprints and proposes three reshapes as
answerable cards. A **merge** joins Tasks the lane would serialize anyway. A **batch** joins small
Tasks of a Feature that has more ready Tasks than the lane has slots. A **split** cuts a Task along
an area boundary when one area is held and another is free. A `yes` to a merge or a batch is
applied the next day, the same way every other groom answer is. A `yes` to a split holds the Task
and hands it to a reshape session, and the parts it mints wait for the operator's `yes` before
they launch.

## Decisions

### In

- Three new groom sections, `Merges proposed`, `Batches proposed` and `Splits proposed`, each with
  one answerable line per proposal. A fourth section, `Split parts to confirm`, lists Task cards a
  reshape session minted that nobody has confirmed yet.
- A proposal line grammar: `- [ ] <id> <verb> <ids> — <why> → answer: ____`, with `<verb>` one of
  `merge`, `batch` or `split`. `--apply` reads the verb, so `yes`/`no` on a proposal line mean
  *reshape* / *decline*, not `decided` / `removed`.
- Mechanical apply for `merge` and `batch`. The first Task listed survives. It takes the union of
  every listed Task's `writes:` and `stories:`, plus a `merged:` list naming the others. Each other
  Task is marked `removed: merged into <survivor> (groom <date>)`.
- Declining: `no` on a proposal line appends that proposal's key to `reshape_declined:` on every
  Task it names. The same proposal is not made again while the key stands.
- Split apply: `yes` writes `reshape: split <area> | <area> (groom <date>)` on the Task. The feeder
  then holds the Task (a `WAITS ON reshape` row that launches nothing) and emits one launching
  `RESHAPE → PLAN` row with the new brief kind `reshape`.
- The `reshape` brief. It mints one Task per area from `BACKLOG_ID_RANGE`, each with
  `split_from: <old id>`, amends the old Task's section of the plan, and marks the old Task
  `removed: split into <ids>`.
- Parts wait for confirmation: a Task with `split_from:` and no `decided: true` gets a
  `WAITS ON confirm` row that launches nothing. Its `yes` on the groom's `Split parts to confirm`
  line is the existing `decided` answer.
- The coder brief of a merged Task names what it absorbed:
  `Also delivers: T-b, T-c — their sections of <plan>, acceptance byte-identical`.
- Two conventions, both with defaults: `area_depth` (2) and `batch_max_globs` (2).
- Two fixes that merging depends on. Ingest's Feature ladder does not count `removed` Tasks. The
  groom's `Stories without Tasks` section and `asf check`'s Story coverage do not count a Story as
  covered because a `removed` Task lists it.

### Out

- **Merging across Features.** Two Features have two plans, two specs and two acceptance sets.
  Merging across them would give one branch two owners. Merges and batches stay inside one Feature.
  Splits compare against every Feature, because a hold can come from anywhere.
- **Reshaping a Task that has started.** Only a `New` Task that no session holds, that is not
  `blocked`, and that is not already marked `reshape` is proposed or reshaped. An `Active` Task
  already has a branch.
- **Reshaping Stories as record items.** A Story is an acceptance unit and a Task is the delivery
  unit: one session, one branch, one gate. The card's "Stories" are reshaped through the Tasks that
  deliver them. A Story's own card is never merged, split or removed here. Its `stories:` entries
  move with the Tasks.
- **Applying anything without an answer.** The groom proposes. A blank answer changes nothing,
  every day.
- **Reshaping at spec or plan time.** The plan brief already says "keep them disjoint or accept
  the queue". Making the plan reviewer score shape is a later card.
- **Measuring gates saved.** No new metric. The History lines and the groom file are the record
  (see Records). Counting "gates saved" can come later, from those lines.
- **A size measure other than glob count.** The index has no line count or diff size for a Task
  that has not started. `len(writes)` is the only size fact available before the work exists.

### Preconditions

Each was checked against `origin/main` at `b0a50d2`.

- Task cards are record files. Nothing re-derives them from the plan's `### Task` headings:
  `DEFAULT_TASK_HEADING` (`asf/conventions.py:45`) is read by no ingest code. A typed field the
  groom writes on a Task therefore persists. The plan session mints Task cards from its id range
  (`asf/briefs/build.py:49`, `ID_RANGE_KINDS`).
- `removed` already takes an item out of `is_open` (`asf/record/core.py:114-118`) and out of the
  index reader (`asf/views/index_reader.py:23`), and so out of `feeder.rows.items_of`.
- **Not yet true:** ingest's Feature ladder collects `child_ids` over every Task whose parent is the
  Feature, `removed` or not (`asf/record/ingest.py:347-350`). A merged-away Task would then stay
  `New` forever and the Feature would never reach `landed`. This spec fixes that (S-7906).
- **Not yet true:** `asf check`'s Story coverage (`asf/record/check.py:121-126`) and the groom's
  `groom_stories_without_tasks` (`asf/groom/groom.py:170-174`) both read `stories:` off removed
  Tasks. The fix is the same one (S-7906).
- `feeder.footprint.overlaps` / `globs_overlap` (`asf/feeder/footprint.py`) are pure functions
  over glob lists. The groom reuses them, so a proposal and the feeder never disagree about what
  "overlap" means.
- `asf.tick.step_wave.capacity()` (`asf/tick/step_wave.py:25`) reads `config.yaml`
  `feeder.capacity` (default 4). The groom reads the lane's size through it.
- `Conventions.from_mapping` keeps unknown keys in `extra` (`asf/conventions.py:96-110`), so adding
  two named fields breaks no existing product file.
- The daily step already runs `groom --apply` (`asf/tick/step_daily.py:27-33`). The new sections
  need no new step and no new clock.

### Choices

| # | Choice | Chosen | Against | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | What gets reshaped | Tasks. Stories move with them in `stories:` | Story cards (the card's wording) | The feeder, harvest and the footprint gate all work on Tasks (`rows.py:317`, one branch per Task). A Story has no `writes:`. Merging two Story cards would change the acceptance record and deliver nothing faster. |
| D2 | Merge trigger | two or more ready Tasks of one Feature whose `writes:` overlap. One proposal per connected component of the overlap graph | a size threshold; same Story | Overlap is proof that no parallelism is lost: the feeder would run them one after the other anyway (`WAITS ON`). The merge only saves sessions and gates, so it is a safe default to offer every time. |
| D3 | Batch trigger | a Feature has more ready units (a pending merge counts as one) than `feeder.capacity`, and at least two small Tasks (`len(writes) <= batch_max_globs`) exist. The batch is the `max(2, units - capacity + 1)` smallest, ordered by `(len(writes), rank, id)` | batch any two small Tasks; batch across Features | Beyond capacity, some Tasks wait no matter what. Batching the smallest into one brings the Feature back to capacity: it keeps as much parallelism as the lane can use and saves gates. Below capacity, a batch would trade real parallelism for a gate, and that is not the groom's call to push. |
| D4 | Split trigger | a ready Task whose globs fall into two or more areas, where at least one area overlaps a *holder* and at least one area overlaps none. A holder is an `Active` Task, or a ready Task of another Feature | any Task spanning two areas | Spanning areas costs nothing until something holds one of them. Requiring one held area and one free area means the split lets work start now. Without that requirement, nearly every Task with a `tests/` file would be proposed. |
| D5 | What an area is | the literal head of a glob (`footprint._literal_head`), without its last path component unless the glob ends in `/`, cut to the first `area_depth` segments (default 2). A root file is `.` | a list of area globs per product; top-level directory only | It works with no configuration, and `asf/feeder/rows.py` and `asf/feeder/**` land in one area (`asf/feeder`). Depth 1 would put every `asf/*` path in one area, and a split would never fire. A product with a different layout sets `area_depth`. |
| D6 | Answer grammar | the verb after the id marks the line (`T-0012 merge T-0012+T-0014 — …`). `yes` applies the verb; `no`/`close` declines | new answer words (`merge`, `split`) on ordinary card lines | Ordinary `no` means `removed` (`groom.py:41-42`). Answering `no` to "merge these?" must not delete a Task. Making the line say what `yes` does keeps the operator's one-word answers. |
| D7 | Merge survivor | the first listed, ordered by `(rank, id)`, the feeder's own order (`rows.py:319`) | the largest footprint | The survivor keeps its place in the queue. Whatever the feeder would have launched first is still launched first. |
| D8 | Merge apply when the world moved | per line, atomic. If any named Task is no longer ready (`Active`, removed, held, busy), the line is skipped with `groom: merge <ids> skipped — <id> is <why>` and nothing is written | apply to the ones still ready | A partial merge is a new shape nobody proposed. Answers are applied a day after they were asked. |
| D9 | Split apply | a reshape session (`RESHAPE → PLAN`, brief kind `reshape`), with its parts held for a groom `yes` | a mechanical split by area | Cutting `writes:` by area is mechanical. Cutting Stories, Steps and fenced acceptance tests is not: a part whose acceptance needs the other part would fail its own gate. That call needs someone to read the plan. The operator's confirm stands in for a plan-review round, because the amended plan keeps the approved spec and changes only how the work is cut. |
| D10 | Row identity for a reshape | `item_id` is the Task (`T-x`), `feature_id` is its Feature, branch is `branch_for(product, 'plan', T-x)` | `item_id` = Feature | `feature_rows` skips a busy Feature entirely (`rows.py:280`). Holding the Task, not the Feature, lets the Feature's other Tasks keep launching while one is being reshaped. |
| D11 | Declined proposals | `reshape_declined:` (a list of keys) on each Task named. The key is `<verb> <sorted ids>`, or `split <id> <areas>`, and suppresses exactly that proposal | never re-propose that Task; re-propose daily | Re-proposing every day spends the operator's attention. Never proposing again misses the day a footprint changes. A key built from ids and areas changes when the shape does. |
| D12 | Where the logic lives | a new `asf/groom/shape.py` (pure functions over `canonical` + `derived`) called from `build_groom_sections`, with apply in `groom.apply_groom_answers` | inside the feeder | The feeder runs every tick and never writes. Proposals are a daily question for a human, which is the groom's job. `shape.py` imports `asf.feeder.footprint` so both use one overlap test. |

## The design

### What counts as ready

`shape.ready_tasks(canonical, derived)` returns every Task that meets all of these:

- `type: task`, `is_open`, machine `state: New`, not `blocked`;
- its Feature (the parent chain, or `feature:`, as in `feeder.rows._task_feature`) is decided,
  open, and at `plan-approved` or `building …`;
- no `reshape:` field;
- if it has a `split_from:`, it also has `decided: true`.

The groom has no session registry, so "no session holds it" is approximated by `state: New`. The
feeder repeats the busy check at launch, and apply re-checks at answer time (D8).

### `asf/groom/shape.py` (new)

```python
def area_of(glob, depth): ...            # D5
def areas(writes, depth): ...            # {area: [globs]} in first-seen order
def ready_tasks(canonical, derived): ... # the list above, sorted (feature rank, task rank, id)
def merge_proposals(ready, declined): ...
def batch_proposals(ready, merges, capacity, max_globs, declined): ...
def split_proposals(ready, active, depth, declined): ...
def proposal_key(verb, ids, areas=()): ...
```

Each proposal is a `Proposal(verb, ids, areas, why)`. `ids[0]` is the survivor, or the Task being
split. The rendered lines look like this:

```
## Merges proposed

- [ ] T-0012 merge T-0012+T-0014 — F-0040: writes overlap (asf/groom/** ↔ asf/groom/groom.py); they run one after the other anyway, one session and one gate saved → answer: ____

## Batches proposed

- [ ] T-0031 batch T-0031+T-0032+T-0033 — F-0041 has 6 ready Tasks for 4 slots; these 3 write ≤2 globs each: one session and one gate instead of 3 → answer: ____

## Splits proposed

- [ ] T-0050 split asf/feeder | asf/harvest — asf/feeder is held by T-0020 (Active); asf/harvest is free: the free part can start now → answer: ____

## Split parts to confirm

- [ ] T-0060 Harvest half of T-0050 — split from T-0050, launches after yes → answer: ____
```

The merge `why` names one overlapping pair (the first `footprint.overlaps` hit). The split `why`
names each held area and its holder, then the free areas.

### `asf/groom/groom.py`

- `GROOM_SECTIONS` gains, after `Stories without Tasks after plan-approved`:
  `('Merges proposed', 'merge')`, `('Batches proposed', 'batch')`, `('Splits proposed', 'split')`,
  `('Split parts to confirm', 'split_parts')`.
- `build_groom_sections` computes them. Capacity comes from `step_wave.capacity()`. `area_depth`
  and `batch_max_globs` come from the product's conventions, or the defaults when `cmd_groom` has
  no product.
- `groom_stories_without_tasks` skips removed Tasks when it collects `task_story_ids`.
- `PROPOSAL_RE = ^- \[[ xX]\]\s+(?P<id>[A-Z]-\d{4})\s+(?P<verb>merge|batch|split)\s+(?P<rest>.*?)\s+—`.
  `apply_groom_answers` tries it first. A line that matches goes to `apply_proposal` and never
  reaches `_parse_answer`. The `controller:` prefix keeps its meaning (attribution only).
- `apply_proposal(root, canonical, verb, ids, areas, answer, date, who)`:
  - `yes` + `merge`/`batch` → `merge_tasks`. It first checks every id is still ready (D8), then
    writes on the survivor `writes` (ordered union), `stories` (ordered union) and `merged` (the
    others, appended), and on each other Task `removed: merged into <survivor> (groom <date>)`.
    Each card gets one History line.
  - `yes` + `split` → checks the Task is still ready, then writes
    `reshape: split <a> | <b> (groom <date>)` and a History line.
  - `no`/`close` → appends `proposal_key(...)` to `reshape_declined` on each named Task and writes
    a History line `groom: <verb> declined`.
  - Blank or `____` → nothing. A field already at its target value (survivor already has `merged`
    listing the others; `reshape` already set) → nothing. That keeps `--apply` idempotent, like
    today (`groom.py:61-64`).
- The summary line printed by `cmd_groom` counts the new sections the way it counts the others.

### `asf/feeder/rows.py`

- New kind `RESHAPE = 'RESHAPE → PLAN'`.
- `task_rows` looks at each New Task, not busy, not blocked:
  - `reshape:` set → a `PLAN → CODE` row with action `WAITS ON reshape` and waits_on `reshape`
    (it launches nothing and takes no slot, `tiers.select`), then a launching
    `Row(tier=2, kind=RESHAPE, item_id=T, feature_id=F, brief_kind='reshape',
    branch=branch_for(product, 'plan', T), reason='groom: split <areas>')`;
  - `split_from:` set without `decided: true` → `WAITS ON confirm`, launches nothing;
  - otherwise, as today.
- `KIND_ORDER` puts `RESHAPE` after `STALE` and before new work within a Feature.
- The module docstring's row table gains the `RESHAPE → PLAN` line.

### `asf/briefs`

- `build.KINDS` and `ID_RANGE_KINDS` gain `reshape`. `DEFAULT_MODELS['reshape'] = HEAVY`, like
  `plan`.
- `templates/reshape.md` (new):

  ```
  ## Your job: reshape {item_id}

  Binding: the groom's approved split — {reason} — of this Task of `{plan_path}`, under the
  approved spec `{spec_path}`. Add no scope. Change only how {item_id} is cut.

  DELIVERABLE, on branch `{branch}` cut from `origin/{main}`:
  - one Task per area, ids from `BACKLOG_ID_RANGE`, each carrying `split_from: {item_id}`, its
    `writes:` (the part of {item_id}'s `{writes}` in that area) and `stories:`. Every Story of
    {item_id} sits on at least one part;
  - {item_id}'s section of the plan replaced by one section per part, each with Files, Steps, Gate
    and Acceptance (the spec's fenced tests, byte-identical);
  - {item_id} marked `removed: split into <ids>`.
  If a part cannot pass its own acceptance without another part, say so and leave {item_id} whole:
  `NEEDS OPERATOR: {item_id} does not split along <area> — answer no on the split line`.

  Final message: the pushed sha, the part ids with their writes, the coverage line.
  ```
- `preamble.state_lines`, for a Task with `merged:`, adds
  `Also delivers: <ids> — their sections of <plan_path>, acceptance byte-identical`.
  `templates/coder.md`'s Binding sentence reads "this Task (and any it absorbed, listed above)".

### `asf/record`

- `ingest.cmd_ingest`: `child_ids` in the Feature ladder skips Tasks with a typed `removed`.
- `check.py`: `task_story_ids` for Story coverage skips removed Tasks.

### `asf/conventions.py`

- `area_depth: int = 2`, `batch_max_globs: int = 2`, each with a `DEFAULT_*` constant and a one-line
  doc comment like its neighbours. `docs/products.example.yaml` lists both under `conventions:`,
  commented out, at their defaults.

## Records

- **The groom file** `groom/<date>.md` gains four sections, rendered as `(none)` when empty, like
  the others. The proposal lines are the question. The answered lines in yesterday's file are the
  audit trail of what was decided and by whom (`(operator)` or `(controller, starvation policy)`).
- **Typed fields on Task cards** (the record's item files):
  - `merged: [T-…]` on a survivor;
  - `removed: merged into T-… (groom <date>)` and `removed: split into T-…, T-…` on the Tasks that
    are gone;
  - `reshape: split <areas> (groom <date>)` on a Task waiting for its reshape session;
  - `split_from: T-…` on a part, plus `decided: true` once it is confirmed;
  - `reshape_declined: [<key>, …]` on each Task of a declined proposal;
  - `writes:` and `stories:`, rewritten on a survivor (ordered unions).
- **History lines** (`append_history_lines`), one per card per applied answer:
  `- <date> groom: merged T-b into T-a (operator)`,
  `- <date> groom: removed → merged into T-a (operator)`,
  `- <date> groom: reshape → split <areas> (operator)`,
  `- <date> groom: merge declined (operator)`.
