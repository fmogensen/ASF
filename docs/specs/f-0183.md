# F-0183 — `--apply` would have taken the trunk's runners away, and the doctor had nothing to say about it

A product's required jobs run on a routing label chosen by a repository variable:

```yaml
    runs-on: [self-hosted, "${{ vars.CI_REQUIRED_LABEL || 'heavy' }}"]
```

The variable is set, to `vendor-heavy`. The runners carry `vendor-heavy`. `asf ci reconcile` reads
that `runs-on`, takes the literal `heavy` out of the `|| 'heavy'` and never asks the host what
`CI_REQUIRED_LABEL` is — so as far as the reconcile is concerned `vendor-heavy` is a label no job
names, and `--apply` removes it. Measured in this checkout, on the incident's own shape:

```
runner  current labels                                                        target labels                                   action
h1      linux, self-hosted, x64, heavy, provider-alpha, vendor, vendor-heavy  linux, self-hosted, x64, heavy, provider-alpha  remove vendor, vendor-heavy
ci reconcile: h1 - vendor
ci reconcile: h1 - vendor-heavy
```

`drift` over the same three inputs returns `[]` — no finding, no row, nothing for the doctor to
show. The command that exists to keep the host honest would have left the trunk's required jobs
with no runner, quietly, and the diagnosis surface would have agreed with it.

The defect is one reader taking a default for an answer. This card is: **a `runs-on` is judged
against the value the host actually gives its variables, and a `runs-on` this reader cannot
resolve holds every removal it could possibly be about.**

### The choices

| # | Chosen | Against | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **The resolution happens in the backend's `runs_on()`: it reads the host's variables and hands `plan` and `drift` a `RunsOn` that already knows what the job runs on.** | Threading a variables map from each caller down into `plan` and `drift`. | There are two callers (P8) and both would have to grow an argument, a failure path and a decision about what to do when the read fails. The backend is the one thing in the module that talks to the host, and `parse_runs_on` stays what it is — a pure function of `(text, variables)` that a test drives with a literal dict. |
| C2 | **A `vars.NAME` the host did not name is *unresolved*, never its `\|\| 'x'` default.** | Treating an absent name as the empty string — which is what the expression engine itself does — and taking the default. | Absent means one of two indistinguishable things: genuinely unset, or set somewhere this reader does not look (an organisation variable, an environment variable, a variable on the job's `environment:`). One of those two is the incident. The price of guessing "unset" is the trunk's required jobs losing their runner (P4); the price of guessing "unknown" is one stray label living another cycle. This single rule is also what makes the design's correctness independent of how many variable sources it reads (C3). |
| C3 | **One endpoint: the repository's own variables, `repos/<slug>/actions/variables`.** | Also reading `orgs/<owner>/actions/variables`, and the repo's environment variables, so that fewer names come back unresolved. | Under C2 those reads buy no correctness — only a smaller held set. They also cost: the org endpoint needs a scope the pool's token may not carry, a user-owned repo answers it 404, and telling "no such org" from "you may not read it" means parsing `gh`'s stderr. When a name is unresolved the plan says which one, and the operator's answer is one line: set it at repository level, or take the expression out of `runs-on`. |
| C4 | **Only `vars.` changes. `matrix.`, `inputs.`, `env.` and every other expression read exactly as they do today.** | Treating every `${{ … \|\| 'x' }}` as unresolved. | The card is about a value this reader could have looked up and did not. A `workflow_dispatch` input is unset on the push and schedule runs the trunk's required jobs are, so its `\|\| 'x'` default genuinely *is* what runs; `matrix.` is already None. Widening to all of them would hold every removal on every product that writes an expression, with nothing to look up that could ever lift the hold. |
| C5 | **A variable the host names with an empty value falls to the expression's own default**; with no default it is unresolved. | Calling an empty value unresolved too. | `\|\|` takes the right side for a falsy left side, so with the value known to be empty the resolved label is known too. This is the one case where "the host named it" is unambiguous. |
| C6 | **While any `runs-on` is unresolved, `plan` holds every removal except a stale `class-` label's and a reserve's.** | (a) Refusing the whole `--apply`; (b) holding nothing and printing a warning. | (a) throws away the adds, which are the half that never loses capacity and the half a runner with no role is waiting for; the plan's whole discipline is adds first, removes second, and only the second half is unsafe here. The two exemptions are the two removals that provably cannot strand a job: no `runs-on` may route on a `class-` label (P12), and a reserve's label is the reserve's to place and the tick rewrites it every cycle (P11). |
| C7 | **The hold is pool-wide, not per-label.** | Holding only the labels an unresolved job could plausibly be asking for. | The labels of an unresolved job are, by definition, unknown — there is no subset to compute. | C8 | **A held label is its own clause in the plan's action — `keep <label> — held: a runs-on names a variable this reader could not resolve` — not folded into `blocked`.** | Reusing `blocked until workflows migrate`. | They are statements about different things and they have different answers. `blocked` says *the workflows still ask for this label*, and the operator migrates the `runs-on`. `held` says *this reader cannot tell what the workflows ask for*, and the operator makes the variable readable. | C9 | **The doctor says it too, and withholds `stranded` while any `runs-on` is unresolved.** | Leaving `drift` alone: it is read-only, so it cannot strip anything. | P6: an unresolved job is invisible to `drift`, so every runner only that job reaches is reported `stranded` — and the guide's own remedy for `stranded` is `asf ci reconcile --apply` (`docs/guide/ci-runner-pool.md:72`). **In.** `asf/ci_pool.py`: `_VARS_REF` and the `vars.` rule in `_label_token` (which returns a pair);

`unresolved_rows`; `Backend.variables` and `GitHubBackend.variables`; `GitHubBackend.runs_on`

`tests/test_ci_pool.py`: one amended test and two new classes.

**Out.** (a) Organisation- and environment-scoped variables as a *source* (C3) — they are the
reason an absent name is unresolved, not something this change reads. (b) Every expression but
`vars.` (C4). (c) The `runs-on` shapes the line reader does not read today: a `{group: …}` map, a
reusable workflow's `runs-on` decided by its caller, `runs-on` behind an anchor — all still

(d) Keeping the resolved half of a partly unresolved `runs-on`: `[self-hosted, ${{ vars.X }}]` is
unresolved whole, not "self-hosted plus an unknown". (e) The reserve, the trials, `apply`'s
add-before-remove order and `tick_reserve`: untouched. (f) Caching the variables read across
calls — it is one `gh api` call inside one `runs_on()`. (g) A flag to override a hold (C11).
(h) The labels on any live product's runners: this change decides what the reconcile *would*
write, and an operator runs it.

## The design

### §1 The host's variables — one read, behind the backend

`asf/ci_pool.py`, in the `Backend` contract beside `runs_on`:

```python
    def variables(self):
        """The host's own variables, ``{NAME: value}`` — what a workflow's ``${{ vars.NAME }}``
        reads. ``{}`` for a host that has no such thing, which is also the safe answer: a name
        that is not in this map is never resolved to an expression's default
        (:func:`_label_token`). A host that *has* variables and could not be asked raises
        :class:`BackendError`; :meth:`GitHubBackend.runs_on` turns that into the same ``{}`` and
        keeps the reason."""
        return {}
```

and on `GitHubBackend`, in the shape of `runners()` (P7):

```python
    def variables(self):
        if not self.slug:
            raise BackendError('the product has no repo_slug')
        rows = self._lines(['--paginate', f'repos/{self.slug}/actions/variables?per_page=100',
                            '--jq', '.variables[]'])
        return {str(v['name']): str(v.get('value') or '') for v in rows if v.get('name')}
```

The repository's own variables and no others, deliberately (C3): the rule in §2 is what makes that
enough.

### §2 The token reader asks the host, and admits when it cannot

```python
_VARS_REF = re.compile(r"\bvars\.([A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_-]*)")


def _label_token(tok, variables=None):
    """``(label, unresolved variable)`` for one ``runs-on`` token — the label lower-cased, or
    None when this reader cannot know it, and the ``vars.`` name that made it unknowable.

    Quotes are stripped. ``${{ vars.NAME }}`` and ``${{ vars.NAME || 'x' }}`` resolve from
    ``variables`` (:meth:`Backend.variables`), matched the way the host matches a variable name,
    without regard to case: the value when the host gives one, the expression's own ``'x'`` when
    that value is empty — which is what ``||`` does with a falsy left side — and **None when the
    host did not name the variable at all**. Absent is not unset: the value may come from an
    organisation or an environment this reader does not read, and taking ``'x'`` for it is how a
    live routing label came to be judged unused and removed (F-0183). Every other expression is
    its ``|| 'x'`` default, else None, exactly as before."""
    t = tok.strip().strip('"').strip("'").strip()
    if '${{' not in t:
        return (_norm(t) if t else None), None
    m = _EXPR_DEFAULT.match(t)
    default = _norm(m.group(1)) if m else None
    ref = _VARS_REF.search(t)
    if ref is None:
        return default, None
    name = ref.group(1)
    known = {k.upper(): v for k, v in (variables or {}).items()}
    if name.upper() not in known:
        return None, name
    value = _norm(known[name.upper()])
    return (value or default), None
```

`parse_runs_on` gains the argument, keeps the pairs, and records both halves:

```python
def parse_runs_on(workflow, text, variables=None):
    """Every job's :class:`RunsOn` in one workflow file, in file order … ``variables``: the
    host's own variables (:meth:`Backend.variables`), which a ``runs-on`` may name through
    ``${{ vars.X }}``; without them every such reference is unresolved, never its default."""
```

Each of the three token branches becomes a list of pairs, and the tail of the loop is:

```python
        toks = [lab for lab, _v in pairs]
        labels = None if any(t is None for t in toks) else frozenset(toks)
        out.append(RunsOn(workflow, job, labels,
                          frozenset(v for _l, v in pairs if v)))
```

`RunsOn` carries the fourth field (P10):

```python
    #: the ``vars.`` names this reader could not resolve — the host did not name them. A runs-on
    #: with any of these is unresolved (``labels`` is None) *and* says why, which is the
    #: difference between "not judged" and "nothing in this pool may be removed" (F-0183).
    needs_vars: frozenset = frozenset()
```

and the two readers of the fact:

```python
def unresolved_vars(runs_on):
    """``[(workflow:job, [names])]`` — every ``runs-on`` naming a host variable this reader could
    not resolve, in file order. While one stands, no label in the pool can be judged unused: any
    of them may be the label that job runs on."""
    return [(f'{ro.workflow}:{ro.job}', sorted(ro.needs_vars))
            for ro in runs_on if ro.needs_vars]


def unresolved_rows(runs_on, why=None):
    """``[(False, detail)]`` — one row per unresolved ``runs-on``, for the doctor
    (:func:`drift`) and for the reconcile's own footer. ``why``: the reason the variables could
    not be read at all (:attr:`GitHubBackend.vars_error`), when there was one."""
    return [(False, f"runs-on unresolved: {where} asks for "
                    f"{', '.join('vars.' + n for n in names)} — "
                    f"{why or 'the host did not name it'}; no label is removed while it stands "
                    f"(a stale class- label aside)")
            for where, names in unresolved_vars(runs_on)]
```

An unreadable variables list and a variable the host does not have are the same thing to §2 — both
end in "unresolved" — so the failure needs no second path, only its reason (C2, C10).

### §7 The guide

`docs/guide/ci-runner-pool.md:80-82` is the one sentence in `docs/` that states the defect (P13).
It becomes:

> Jobs on hosted runners (no `self-hosted` in their `runs-on`) and a `runs-on` that is an
> expression ASF cannot resolve (`${{ matrix.os }}`) are not judged. `${{ vars.X || 'heavy' }}` is
> read as the repository variable `X` — `asf` asks the host for it (`repos/<slug>/actions/
> variables`) — and falls back to `'heavy'` only when the host gives `X` an empty value. A name
> the host does not have is **unresolved**, not `'heavy'`: it may be set on the organisation or on
> an environment, and guessing there is how a live routing label gets removed. While any `runs-on`
> is unresolved, the reconcile removes no label but a stale `class-` one, and the doctor says
> which variable it is waiting on.

One row joins the drift table —

> | `runs-on unresolved: <workflow>:<job> asks for vars.<NAME>` | the labels of that job are unknown, so no label can be judged unused | set `<NAME>` as a repository variable, or take the expression out of the `runs-on` |

— and one bullet joins **Capacity is never lost on the way**:

> - While any `runs-on` names a variable ASF could not resolve, no label is removed at all (a
>   stale `class-` label and a reservation's label aside — no `runs-on` may ask for either). The
>   row says `keep <label> — held: …`. Adds, and the trial that follows one, happen as usual.

## Records

- **No new file, no new row, no new event.** The change is a reader and a refusal; nothing is
  written to `~/.ASF/state/<product>/` that was not written before. `ci-trials.json` and
  `ci-trials.jsonl` keep their shape and their writers: a trial still starts when a role label is
  added, and a hold never adds or removes one.
- **One more `gh api` call per `runs_on()`** — `repos/<slug>/actions/variables?per_page=100`,
  paginated, read-only, once per `asf ci reconcile` and once per `asf doctor` run that has a
  `ci.pool`. No write endpoint is touched; `add_labels` and `remove_label` are unchanged.
- **The CI host's runner labels** — what this change is about. Where `--apply` would have written
  `DELETE …/labels/vendor-heavy`, it writes nothing while the variable is unresolved, and writes
  nothing for that label at all once it resolves. The one label the incident's plan should have
  removed, and still does, is the stray provider-shaped one (C12).
- **`asf ci reconcile`'s output** — the plan table gains a `keep <labels> — held: …` action, and
  a footer line per unresolved `runs-on` naming the workflow, the job, the variable and, when
  there was one, the reason the variables could not be read.
- **`asf doctor`'s `ci pool` rows** — a new `runs-on unresolved: <workflow>:<job> asks for
  vars.<NAME> …` row, required like the rest of `drift`'s; and no `stranded:` row at all while one
  stands, where today a runner reachable only by an unreadable job is reported stranded and the
  documented remedy is the command that strips its labels (P6).

  one bullet under the plan's capacity rules (§7).
