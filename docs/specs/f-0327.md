# F-0327 — the row that waits is evidence; today nothing adds it up

The factory already writes down, every tick, exactly who holds the product up. Every held row
carries the id it waits on in its own action — `WAITS ON T-0517`, `WAITS ON delivery T-0517`,
`WAITS ON T-0056 (parked: F-0023 later)`. Two readers walk those rows
(`asf.ci_queue.dependents`, `asf.feeder.rows.waiting_roots`), they read a different grammar from
each other, each misses rows the other sees, and **nothing downstream of either decides anything
about a park, a cap or a seat**. So a Task holding thirty rows sits in the same silence as a Task
holding none: the feeder emits its row at whatever rank its Feature happens to have, the daily
relaunch cap parks it on the same count as any other, the incomplete-delivery cap parks the
delivery lead with no mention that the lead is what the whole Feature waits on, and no view in the
product prints the sentence "this item holds *n* rows".

The card names four absences — no blocker weight, no escalation, no top-blockers view, incomplete
deliveries park the lead — and this spec closes all four at one place: **one reader of the waiting
edge, whose answer the order, the park and the views all read.**

Measured below against two live records as they stand today (§1, P1–P14): of the reporting
product's **222** held rows, **36** are `WAITS ON delivery <lead>` and **neither reader counts a
single one**; `ci_queue.dependents` credits **109** of the **145** direct edges actually written;
`waiting_roots` — the one reader any surfacing rule is built on — sees **1 of the 31** rows waiting
on this product's heaviest blocker, because a suffix the feeder itself appends breaks its
exact-string guard. The heaviest blocker in the reporting product's record holds **34** rows and
its own row sits **72nd of 250**.

### In

1. **One reader of the waiting edge, with weight.** A new pure module `asf/feeder/blockers.py`:
   `target(row)` — the id or branch one row waits on, under one grammar that reads the
   `delivery ` infix (P3), tolerates every trailing clause the feeder appends (P2), resolves
   `WAITS ON landing: PR #n` through the landing row that names its own branch, and credits
   nothing that is not an id or a branch; `edges(rows)`; `weigh(rows)` →
   `{target: Weight(direct, total, via)}` where `total` credits every hop on the walk to the
   root, cycle-safe; `top(rows, n)` and `line(rows, n)`. `ci_queue.dependents` and
   `rows.waiting_roots` keep their names, signatures and return shapes and become calls into it.
2. **The weight is a term in the order — above a threshold, and nowhere else.** `_candidates`'
   `key` gains one term after the finish-phase and pushed-work terms: `-(total if total >=
   min_rows else 0)`, from `conventions.feeder.blocker_min_rows` (default **8**). Below the
   threshold the key is byte-for-byte today's, so the order only ever changes for a blocker as
   heavy as the ones the card reports (D4). The CI queue's line gains the same answer for free
   (P8): one reader, two orders.
3. **A heavy blocker is never silent.** `asf next --blockers` prints a `TOP BLOCKERS` table —
   item, direct, total, what holds *it*, its own row's action — and the `NEXT` header gains
   `· N rows wait on M blockers`. `asf status` gains a `Top blockers` cell, silent below the
   threshold, in `gate_line`'s voice: `T-0517 holds 34 rows (16 direct) · T-47334 holds 22 (6)`.
   Both read `blockers.top` over the plan the view already makes.
4. **A factory park that holds rows becomes a ruling, not a dead row.** In `correction_rows`,
   a parked correction whose item is a heavy blocker, standing at least
   `conventions.feeder.blocker_escalate_min` minutes (default **60**), and whose park is the
   *factory's* own — `relaunch cap`, `incomplete`, `empty`, `blocked`, the loop guard — becomes
   the `STALEMATE` → `adjudicate` row P9 already builds, its reason naming the weight and the
   rows behind it. One `ALARM blocker <item> — <n> rows wait on it` line on the tick, deduped
   like P13's. A park a **person** made (`asf park`, item scope) and a `priority: later` shelf
   are named by §3 and never escalated — a decision already taken is not overridden (D6).
5. **The delivery lead parked on an incomplete delivery says what it holds.** Its park is a
   factory park, so In 4 escalates it; and its row's reason names both the unbuilt members the
   hold already knows and the `n` rows behind the lead, so the ruling that follows can see at
   once that the cheapest answer is to take the unbuilt member out of the delivery.

### Out

- **Lifting a cap, unparking anything, or raising capacity.** No launch is bought by weight: the
  daily cap stays 6 (P11), the relaunch streak stays 2, `INCOMPLETE_CAP` stays 2, and nothing
  here calls `asf unpark`. A heavy blocker gets a *decision* — a ruling session or an operator
  line — never an extra round of the loop the cap exists to stop (D5).
- **`asf/workers/lifecycle.py`, `asf/workers/loops.py` and `asf/workers/relaunch.py`.** The park
  rules, their texts and their caps are read, not edited. Everything In 4 needs is already on the
  correction the feeder is handed — `kind`, `scope`, `parked`, `reason`, `at` — and
  `park_since` (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2414`) already dates it (D8).
- **`flags.roots` and `surface_stale_parks`' age rule.** That rule keeps its flag, its
  three-day threshold and its `roots_min_dependants`; it gains only the fixed reader (In 1), and
  a row In 4 already escalated is not surfaced twice. Turning `flags.roots` on for any product
  is an operator's call and no part of this card (D7).
- **A new lane state, brief kind, session kind or model.** In 4 routes to the `adjudicate` brief
  that exists; §3 adds no session.
- **Writing weight onto a card.** The weight is derived per pass, like `nearness`
  (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2660`). No `## History` line, no record field, no `index.json` key.
- **The footprint-overlap hold.** `WAITS ON widen_footprint`, `WAITS ON writes` and the other
  rows naming a *path* rather than an item (5 of 95 on this product's record) are left exactly
  as they are: there is no single holder to credit, and inventing one would be a guess (D2).
- **A row whose `WAITS ON` names neither an id nor a branch** — a person, a free slot, the
  console, a decision, `later` — is credited to nothing. Two such rows exist live on the
  reporting product's record and a grammar that credited them would name a human being in a
  machine-written view.

## The design

### 2.1 `asf/feeder/blockers.py` — the one reader (S-91755)

A new pure module, no I/O, importing only `re`, `collections` and `dataclasses` — the same shape
as `asf/feeder/footprint.py`. `asf/feeder/rows.py` imports it, never the reverse.

```
WAITS     = 'WAITS ON '
DELIVERY  = 'WAITS ON delivery '
LANDING   = 'WAITS ON landing: '
ID_RE     = re.compile(r'([A-Z]{1,3}-\d+)\b')     # ci_queue's, the tolerant one (P1)
PR_RE     = re.compile(r'PR #(\d+)')

Weight = collections.namedtuple('Weight', 'direct total via')
#: direct  int     held rows whose own target is this id or branch
#: total   int     held rows on any walk that reaches it — every hop credited, each source once
#: via     [id]    the nearest dependants, in row order, capped at VIA (5)
```

* `pr_branches(rows)` — `{pr number: branch}` off the landing rows that name their own branch;
  `ci_queue.dependents:2078-2082`'s map, moved here unchanged.
* `target(row, prs=None)` — the one grammar, in this order: a `LANDING` prefix resolves to a
  branch (through `prs` for a `PR #n`), and is dropped when it is the row's own branch or names
  the row's own item; a `DELIVERY` prefix takes the lead id after it (**new**, P3); a bare `WAITS`
  prefix takes `ID_RE.match` of the rest, so every trailing clause the feeder appends is harmless
  (P2). `''` for anything else — a free slot, the console, a decision, `later`, a path, a person
  (D10) — and `''` when the target is the row's own item.
* `edges(rows)` — `{row item: target}`, first row per item wins, exactly `dependents`' rule that
  "a row counts only when it names another item than its own".
* `weigh(rows)` — `{target: Weight}`. `direct` counts the edges; `total` walks each source to its
  root over `edges`, crediting every id on the way, with a `seen` set so a cycle terminates and
  no source is credited twice to one ancestor. This is the walk P4 and P5 measured.
* `top(rows, n=5)` — `[(target, Weight)]` by `(-total, -direct, target)`.
* `line(rows, n=3, min_rows=0)` — `T-0517 holds 34 rows (16 direct) · T-47334 holds 22 (6)`;
  `''` when nothing reaches `min_rows`.
* `park_class(correction, shelved=False)` — `'factory' | 'operator' | 'later' | 'live'`: what
  holds the blocker itself, read off the one correction the feeder is already handed and nothing
  else. `later` when the caller says the item is shelved (the caller owns that, because
  `rows.shelved` needs `items` and this module takes none); `operator` for a park at `scope:
  item` — the one `asf park <item>` makes by hand; `factory` for a parked correction whose `kind`
  is in `FACTORY_PARKS = ('relaunch cap', 'incomplete', 'empty', 'blocked', 'naming', 'copies')`
  or whose reason is the loop guard's; `live` for anything else. Only `factory` escalates (D6),
  and the order of those tests is the order above, so a shelved item under a factory park is
  `later` and is not escalated.

Then the two existing readers become calls, with their contracts intact:

* `ci_queue.dependents(rows)` → `{t: w.direct for t, w in blockers.weigh(rows).items()}`. Same
  return shape, same callers (`unblocks`, `entry_unblocks`, `stamp_unblocks`, `line_order`),
  wider reach: the queue's `unblocks n` and its tie-break now count the 36 delivery rows and the
  suffixed rows it was missing (P4, P8). `_ID`, `_WAITS`, `_LANDING` and the body of `dependents`
  go; the module keeps `unblocks`' cache and TTL.
* `rows.waiting_roots(rows)` → `{root: [dependant, …]}` built from `blockers.edges`' roots, in
  row order. Its own `on` loop, its `==` guard and its `AFTER_ID_RE` use go (P2). `AFTER_ID_RE`
  itself stays — `_capped` and others use it.

### S-91756: A blocker heavy enough to be worth reordering for leads its phase

Design §2.2 — the one new term in `_candidates`' sort key, and the convention that gates it
(D4, D7; P7, P8). Acceptance is the second block of `## Acceptance tests`. The term is gated
because the measurement says an ungated one would reorder hundreds of rows for no gain: on the
reporting product's record only 8 of 250 rows would launch, and the two heaviest of those are
already ahead of the five lightest.

- [ ] a tier-2 row whose item holds at least the minimum rows is emitted ahead of a row of the same finish phase whose item holds none
- [ ] a tier-2 row whose item holds one fewer row than the minimum leaves the emitted order identical to the order the same rows take with the term absent
- [ ] a blocker minimum of zero leaves the emitted order identical to the order the same rows take with the term absent
- [ ] a row on pushed work is still emitted ahead of a heavier blocker that is not pushed work
- [ ] an S1 row and an S2 row are still emitted ahead of every tier-2 row, whatever the weight
