# F-0327 — the row that waits is evidence; today nothing adds it up

The factory already writes down, every tick, exactly who holds the product up. Every held row
carries the id it waits on in its own action — `WAITS ON T-0517`, `WAITS ON delivery T-0517`,
`WAITS ON T-0056 (parked: F-0023 later)`. Two readers walk those rows
(`asf.ci_queue.dependents`, `asf.feeder.rows.waiting_roots`), they read a different grammar from
each other, each misses rows the other sees, and **nothing downstream of either decides anything
about a park, a cap or a seat**. So a Task holding thirty rows sits in the same silence as a Task
holding none: the feeder emits its row at whatever rank its Feature happens to have, the daily
relaunch cap parks it on the same count as any other, the incomplete-delivery cap parks the
delivery lead with no mention that the lead is what the whole Feature waits on, and no view in the
product prints the sentence "this item holds *n* rows".

The card names four absences — no blocker weight, no escalation, no top-blockers view, incomplete
deliveries park the lead — and this spec closes all four at one place: **one reader of the waiting
edge, whose answer the order, the park and the views all read.**

Measured below against two live records as they stand today (§1, P1–P24): of the reporting
product's **222** held rows, **36** are `WAITS ON delivery <lead>` and **neither reader counts a
single one**; `ci_queue.dependents` credits **109** of the **145** direct edges actually written;
`waiting_roots` — the one reader any surfacing rule is built on — sees **none of the 30** rows
waiting on this product's heaviest blocker, which has no key in its answer at all, because a
suffix the feeder itself appends breaks its exact-string guard. The heaviest blocker in the reporting product's record holds **34** rows and
its own row sits **72nd of 250**.

### In

1. **One reader of the waiting edge, with weight.** A new pure module `asf/feeder/blockers.py`:
   `target(row)` — the id or branch one row waits on, under one grammar that reads the
   `delivery ` infix (P3), tolerates every trailing clause the feeder appends (P2), resolves
   `WAITS ON landing: PR #n` through the landing row that names its own branch, and credits
   nothing that is not an id or a branch; `edges(rows)`; `weigh(rows)` →
   `{target: Weight(direct, total, via)}` where `total` credits every hop on the walk to the
   root, cycle-safe; `top(rows, n)` and `line(rows, n)`. `ci_queue.dependents` and
   `rows.waiting_roots` keep their names, signatures and return shapes and become calls into it.
2. **The weight is a term in the order — above a threshold, and nowhere else.** `_candidates`'
   `key` gains one term after the finish-phase and pushed-work terms: `-(total if total >=
   min_rows else 0)`, from `conventions.feeder.blocker_min_rows` (default **8**). Below the
   threshold the key is byte-for-byte today's, so the order only ever changes for a blocker as
   heavy as the ones the card reports (D4). The CI queue's line gains the same answer for free
   (P8): one reader, two orders.
3. **A heavy blocker is never silent.** `asf next --blockers` prints a `TOP BLOCKERS` table —
   item, direct, total, what holds *it*, its own row's action — and the `NEXT` header gains
   `· N rows wait on M blockers`. `asf status` gains a `Top blockers` cell, silent below the
   threshold, in `gate_line`'s voice: `T-0517 blocks 34 rows (16 direct) · T-47334 blocks 22 (6)`.
   Both read `blockers.top` over the plan the view already makes.
4. **A factory park that holds rows becomes a ruling, not a dead row.** A post-pass over the
   ordered rows (D15) rewrites the `PARKED` row of an item that is a heavy blocker, whose park
   has stood at least `conventions.feeder.blocker_escalate_min` minutes (default **60**), and
   whose park is the *factory's* own — `relaunch cap`, `incomplete`, `empty`, the loop guard —
   into the `STALEMATE` → `adjudicate` row P9 already builds, its reason naming the weight and
   the rows behind it. One `ALARM blocker <item> — <n> rows wait on it` line on the tick, deduped
   like P13's. Three facts already on the correction bound it to one ruling per card state: a
   hold already `settled` (D12), a park written on the item's own adjudicate job (D13), and a
   park that is a **question** awaiting an operator's answer (D14). A park a **person** made
   (`asf park`, item scope) and a `priority: later` shelf are named by §3 and never escalated —
   a decision already taken is not overridden (D6).
5. **The delivery lead parked on an incomplete delivery says what it holds.** Its park is a
   factory park, so In 4 escalates it; and its row's reason names both the unbuilt members the
   hold already knows and the `n` rows behind the lead, so the ruling that follows can see at
   once that the cheapest answer is to take the unbuilt member out of the delivery.

### Out

- **Lifting a cap, unparking anything, or raising capacity.** No launch is bought by weight: the
  daily cap stays 6 (P11), the relaunch streak stays 2, `INCOMPLETE_CAP` stays 2, and nothing
  here calls `asf unpark`. A heavy blocker gets a *decision* — a ruling session or an operator
  line — never an extra round of the loop the cap exists to stop (D5).
- **Every park rule, park text and cap under `asf/workers/`.** `asf/workers/lifecycle.py` and
  `asf/workers/loops.py` are read, not edited at all. Everything the escalation needs is already
  on the correction the feeder is handed — `kind`, `scope`, `parked`, `reason`, `at`, `settled`,
  `job` — and `park_since` (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2414`) already dates it (D8). The one exception,
  and the whole of it: **`asf/workers/relaunch.py`'s `alarmed` gains one default-valued keyword
  argument** so the blocker alarm's mark can be read back at all (C3, D16). Its park rules, its
  park texts, `park_fields`, `loop_key`, `DAILY_CAP` and every cap are untouched.
- **`flags.roots` and `surface_stale_parks`' age rule.** That rule keeps its flag, its
  three-day threshold and its `roots_min_dependants`; it gains only the fixed reader (In 1), and
  a row In 4 already escalated is not surfaced twice. Turning `flags.roots` on for any product
  is an operator's call and no part of this card (D7).
- **A new lane state, brief kind, session kind or model.** In 4 routes to the `adjudicate` brief
  that exists; §3 adds no session.
- **Writing weight onto a card.** The weight is derived per pass, like `nearness`
  (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2654`). No `## History` line, no record field, no `index.json` key.
- **`asf next --json`'s output, `docs/guide/operating.md` and the test that pins them.** Plain
  `--json` keeps the bare JSON array `rows_json` prints today, byte for byte — same 18 field names
  per row, same order; the blockers are a second flag's output (`--blockers --json`), never a
  change of shape under the old one (C4, D17). `rows_json`'s *body* does change, by one line: it
  publishes the named `rows.JSON_FIELDS` instead of `dict(r.__dict__)`, which is what keeps the
  three fields §2.4 adds to `Row` out of the array (C5, D19). The bytes, the guide and the pinned
  test are untouched; only the accident by which the shape was guaranteed is replaced by a
  declaration.
- **The footprint-overlap hold.** `WAITS ON widen_footprint`, `WAITS ON writes` and the other
  rows naming a *path* rather than an item (5 of 95 on this product's record) are left exactly
  as they are: there is no single holder to credit, and inventing one would be a guess (D2).
- **A row whose `WAITS ON` names neither an id nor a branch** — a person, a free slot, the
  console, a decision, `later` — is credited to nothing. Two such rows exist live on the
  reporting product's record and a grammar that credited them would name a human being in a
  machine-written view.

| D24 | **The view and the alarm read the rows *after* the invariant gate** | Reading the escalation's own output inside `_candidates` | I4 can still refuse an escalated row for a lane state D23 does not cover, and rewrite it to `WAITS ON landing: <branch> <state>` (P23). ## The design

### 2.1 `asf/feeder/blockers.py` — the one reader (S-91755)

A new pure module, no I/O, importing only `re` and `collections` (I7: it declares one
`namedtuple` and no dataclass) — the same shape as `asf/feeder/footprint.py`.
`asf/feeder/rows.py` imports it, never the reverse.

**The unit of weight is the held row, everywhere** (D18, I1). Not the item: `ci_queue.dependents`
increments once per row with no per-item dedupe (`asf/ci_queue.py:2103`), and D1 promises it keeps
its counts, so `direct` must count rows too. `edges` stays one entry per item because the *walk*
needs a single parent per item, and the module says which is which.

```
WAITS     = 'WAITS ON '
DELIVERY  = 'WAITS ON delivery '
LANDING   = 'WAITS ON landing: '
ID_RE     = re.compile(r'([A-Z]{1,3}-\d+)\b')     # ci_queue's, the tolerant one (P1)
PR_RE     = re.compile(r'PR #(\d+)')
VIA       = 5                                     # how many dependants `via` names
#: the park kinds the factory itself wrote — the only class that escalates (D6, D14, P18).
#: `blocked` is NOT here: it is always a question awaiting an answer (`question`, below).
#: `naming` and `copies` are NOT here either: for those two the lane deliberately keeps the
#: branch in its landing wait (`REFUSAL_KINDS`, `asf/harvest/lane.py:213`, F-0275), so I4
#: refuses `STALEMATE → ADJUDICATE` on the only branch the escalation could use — which is
#: why `correction_rows`' own stalemate excludes them by name (`:1095`) (C4, D23, P23).
#: What is left is the three kinds whose correction turns its branch BACK, the one lane state
#: that lists `STALEMATE → ADJUDICATE` (`asf/invariants.py:812-815`)
FACTORY_PARKS = ('relaunch cap', 'incomplete', 'empty')
#: a park of these kinds can be a question a run's report declared, which `rejudge` releases
#: when the answer lands (`asf/tick/rejudge.py:33`); mirrored here as literals, like the above
QUESTION_PARKS = ('blocked', 'relaunch cap')
ASKED = 'needs input — '                          # `asf/tick/rejudge.py:35`

Weight = collections.namedtuple('Weight', 'direct total via')
#: direct  int     held ROWS whose own target is this id or branch (per row, not per item)
#: total   int     held ROWS whose walk reaches it — every hop credited, each row once per id
#: via     [id]    the nearest dependant items, in row order, capped at VIA (5)
ZERO = Weight(0, 0, ())                           # I3: what a row with no weight reads as
```

* `pr_branches(rows)` — `{pr number: branch}` off the landing rows that name their own branch;
  `ci_queue.dependents:2078-2082`'s map, moved here unchanged.
* `target(row, prs=None)` — the one grammar, in this order: a `LANDING` prefix resolves to a
  branch (through `prs` for a `PR #n`), and is dropped when it is the row's own branch or names
  the row's own item; a `DELIVERY` prefix takes the lead id after it (**new**, P3); a bare `WAITS`
  prefix takes `ID_RE.match` of the rest, so every trailing clause the feeder appends is harmless
  (P2). `''` for anything else — a free slot, the console, a decision, `later`, a path, a person
  (D10) — and `''` when the target is the row's own item.
* `edges(rows)` — `{row item: target}` for the **walk**: first row per item wins, because an item
  can have only one parent in a chain. Exactly `dependents`' rule that "a row counts only when it
  names another item than its own".
* `weigh(rows)` — `{target: Weight}`. `direct` counts the **held rows** whose own `target(row)` is
  that id — once per row, so it is byte-identical to today's `dependents` (D18). `total` walks
  each held row's item to its root over `edges`, crediting that row to every id on the way, with a
  `seen` set so a cycle terminates and one row is credited at most once to any one id. Two held
  rows of the same item are two of weight in both counts; the walk they share is one. This is the
  walk P4 and P5 measured.
* `top(rows, n=5)` — `[(target, Weight)]` by `(-total, -direct, target)`.
* `line(rows, n=3, min_rows=0)` — `T-0517 blocks 34 rows (16 direct) · T-47334 blocks 22 (6)`;
  `''` when nothing reaches `min_rows`.
* `park_class(correction, shelved=False)` — `'later' | 'question' | 'operator' | 'factory' |
  'live'`: what holds the blocker itself, read off the one correction the feeder is already handed
  and nothing else. The tests run **in that order**, and only `factory` escalates (D6):
  * `later` — the caller says the item is shelved. The caller owns that fact, because
    `rows.shelved` needs `items` and this module takes none. First, so a shelved item under a
    factory park is `later` and is never escalated (P5: on this product's record every row behind
    its heaviest blocker is shelved).
  * `question` — the park is a question a run's report declared and `rejudge` will release when
    the answer lands (P18, D14): `kind` in `QUESTION_PARKS` and either `kind == 'blocked'` (always
    a question) or `ASKED` in its reason (a `relaunch cap` carrying the report's claim). Before
    `factory`, because `relaunch cap` is in *both* tuples and a question outranks a cap.
  * `operator` — a park at `scope: item`, the one `asf park <item>` makes by hand
    (`asf/workers/lifecycle.py:1353-1356` writes it as the item's correction with
    `kind='operator park'`, `scope='item'`).

    **Why `scope` is a safe test for a hand park** (I5), and it is load-bearing — a factory park
    that ever carried a scope would silently stop escalating, which is this whole Story failing
    quietly. Verified at this head: `correction_of` sets `'scope': SCOPE_ITEM` in exactly one
    branch, the operator-park one (`asf/workers/lifecycle.py:1354`), and the factory path
    (`:1357-1368`) sets no `scope` key at all; `relaunch.park_fields` writes none either
    (`asf/workers/relaunch.py:337-339`). The same branch is why D13's job test reads `c['job']`
    and not `c['park_job']`: an operator park records its park's job under `park_job` (`:1355`)
    and carries no `job`, so a hand park can never match `adjudicate-<id>` by accident. A test
    pins the pair, so a new `scope` writer under `asf/workers/` fails here rather than in the field.
  * `factory` — a parked correction whose `kind` is in `FACTORY_PARKS`, or whose reason is the
    loop guard's.
  * `live` — anything else.

Then the two existing readers become calls, with their contracts intact:

* `ci_queue.dependents(rows)` → `{t: w.direct for t, w in blockers.weigh(rows).items()}`. Same
  return shape, same callers (`unblocks`, `entry_unblocks`, `stamp_unblocks`, `line_order`),
  wider reach: the queue's `unblocks n` and its tie-break now count the 36 delivery rows and the
  suffixed rows it was missing (P4, P8). `_ID`, `_WAITS`, `_LANDING` and the body of `dependents`
  go; the module keeps `unblocks`' cache and TTL.
* `rows.waiting_roots(rows)` → `{root: [dependant, …]}` built from `blockers.edges`' roots, in
  row order. Its own `on` loop, its `==` guard and its `AFTER_ID_RE` use go (P2). `AFTER_ID_RE`
  itself stays — `_capped` and others use it.

  **The keys widen, which "same return shape" does not say on its own** (I4). Dropping the
  `AFTER_ID_RE` guard (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2437-2440`) admits as *roots* two kinds of key the
  guard excluded: a branch string behind a `LANDING` target, and a short or three-letter id
  (`AFTER_ID_RE` demands `^[A-Z]-\d{4,}$`, so `B-84837` and `T-0517` passed and `PR #1219`'s
  branch did not). The shape is the same and the one caller is unharmed — `surface_stale_parks`
  looks a root up by `r.item_id` (`:2477`), so a branch key is never hit — but the widening is
  the point of the fix, not a side effect of it, and §3 pins both: a branch root is reachable in
  the mapping, and `surface_stale_parks`' own answer over the same rows does not move.

### 2.2 The order, and the queue's line (S-91756)

`conventions.feeder.blocker_min_rows` — a whole number >= 0, default `BLOCKER_MIN_ROWS = 8`; `0`
turns the order term off and leaves §2.3's views alone. Read by one accessor
`rows.blocker_min_rows(product)` beside `max_features_in_build`'s (`asf/feeder/rows.py:443`), and
validated in `validate_mapping`'s `feeder` block (`asf/conventions.py:727-746`) with the same
words as its siblings.

In `_candidates` (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2593`), one weigh per pass beside `nearness` and `pushed`:

```python
weights = blockers.weigh(rows)                      # one pass, after every hold rule has run
min_rows = blocker_min_rows(product)

def heavy(r):
    """Minus the rows this row's item holds, once that number is worth reordering for (D4)."""
    n = (weights.get(r.item_id) or blockers.ZERO).total
    return -n if min_rows and n >= min_rows else 0
```

That weigh sits after `hold_shelved` (`:2645`) — every hold rule has run, so every `WAITS ON`
action the weigh reads is written — and **before the sort**, because it is the one weigh of the
pass: `key` reads it here and §2.4's post-pass reads the same dict (D15, and S-91756's "computed
once per plan").

**It is the weigh of the *uncut* rows, and §2.3's is not** (I1, D25). This one has to be: the
order term is an input to the cut, since `tiers.select` runs on `key`'s output, so the weigh must
precede it. The views, by contrast, are handed only what the cut returned — `plan.rows` and
`cmd_next`'s `plan_rows` (`asf/feeder/render.py:123`). `tiers.select` drops tier-2 non-launching
rows when `free == 0`, and every tier-2 row while the S1 gate holds
(`asf/feeder/tiers.py:66-77`), so **on a busy tick the two numbers differ for the same item on
the same tick**: an escalated row's reason can read "blocks 34 rows" beside a `Top blockers` cell
reading a smaller number. That is not a bug to reconcile — the two answer different questions, and
the escalation must weigh every row that waits, not only the rows that survived this tick's cut —
but on this card it must be *said*, so §2.3 labels the view's count in the table's own header and
§3 pins the gap rather than asserting the two are equal.

`key`'s tier-2 return (`:2692-2694`) gains it in third place — **and so does the
`EPIC_FEATURES` branch** (`:2686`), which is the whole of C6:

```python
# the EPIC_FEATURES branch (:2686): an Epic's own row waits on nothing, so its term is 0 —
# but it must be an int in the same position, or the two branches cannot be compared (P15)
return (r.tier, 1, 1, 0, (0, 0), ix.rank(epic), ix.BIG, r.item_id, 5, seq)

# every other tier-2 row (:2692-2694)
return (r.tier, phase, 0 if r.item_id in pushed else 1, heavy(r),
        near(r, f) if phase == 0 and f else (0, 0), *order,
        KIND_ORDER.get(r.kind, 5), seq)
```

**Why both.** `key` returns four tuple shapes and the second element is all that keeps them apart
(P15). A Feature's own row has `finish_phase` 1, which is also the `EPIC_FEATURES` branch's second
element; if it is not on pushed work its third is `1`, which is the Epic branch's third. The two
then compare element 3 — today `(0, 0)` against `(0, 0)`, both tuples. Put an `int` in one and not
the other and `sorted` raises `TypeError: '<' not supported between instances of 'tuple' and
'int'`, so `asf next`, `asf status` and every tick that plans die on any product whose record has
an Epic needing expansion and a Feature document row at once. Fence F4 proves it both ways. The
`tier < 2` and groom branches never reach element 3 against these two (element 0 and element 1
part them), so they are left alone.

The term goes after the finish-first terms, because finishing pushed work is the stronger rule and
already measured (`:2689-2690`: "22 PRs waited up to 8 days"); before `near`, because `nearness`
is keyed by Feature and cannot see a Task holding rows of *other* Features (P7). With `min_rows`
unmet the term is `0` for every row and the key is today's exactly — proven by F4's third case.
On the reporting product's record it moves `T-77108` (total 9) from 177th and `T-0517` (34) from
72nd to the front of their phase, and leaves the other 240-odd rows where they are.

One consequence, named because it is a real reorder and not a crash: a Feature row heavy enough to
fire the term now sorts **ahead of an `EPIC → FEATURES` row** it used to sort behind on rank, since
the Epic's term is `0` and the heavy row's is negative. That is the card's intent — an item holding
34 rows goes before an Epic expansion — and §3 pins it, together with the Epic row keeping its
place against a *light* Feature row.

The queue needs no edit: `stamp_unblocks` already reads `dependents` (P8), which §2.1 widened.
Its line `(Task F-0113 rank 2, unblocks 3, 3rd in line)` (`asf/ci_queue.py:4656`) starts telling
the truth about delivery holds on the same tick.

### 2.4 Escalating a factory park that holds rows (S-91758)

`conventions.feeder.blocker_escalate_min` — whole minutes >= 0, default
`BLOCKER_ESCALATE_MIN = 60`; `0` turns the escalation off and leaves §2.3 alone. Accessor and
validation beside §2.2's.

**Where it runs: a post-pass over the ordered rows, not an argument to `correction_rows`** (C2,
D15). `correction_rows` is the *first* row builder in `_candidates` (`asf/feeder/rows.py:2604`):
`rows` does not exist there, and the `WAITS ON` actions the weigh counts are written two steps
later by `hold_unlanded` (`:2640`) and `hold_shelved` (`:2645`). A weigh passed into `:2604` would
count zero rows, and a weigh at `:2645` cannot be passed back into a call at `:2604`. So
`correction_rows` takes **no new argument at all** and keeps emitting today's `PARKED` row; the
escalation rewrites that row afterwards, which is exactly what `surface_stale_parks` already does
to a stale park's row (`:2474-2502`). One new call in `_candidates`, immediately before the
`if roots:` block at `:2698-2699`:

```python
since = park_since(occ, adjudicated)            # I6: bound once, both readers take it
shelved_ids = {i for i, why in shelved(items, occ.get('parks')).items()
               if why.endswith(f' {LATER}')}    # C2, D21: the `priority: later` set ALONE
ordered = blockers.escalate_parks(ordered, corrections, weights, since, shelved_ids,
                                  min_rows=min_rows,
                                  escalate_min=blocker_escalate_min(product), now=now)
if roots:
    ordered = surface_stale_parks(ordered, items, product, since, now)
```

`corrections` (`:2597`) and `weights` (§2.2's one weigh, before the sort) are already computed in
`_candidates`. The other two are **not**, and round 2 said they were:

* `park_since(occ, adjudicated)` exists as a *call* (`:2414` defines it; P10's rule makes it at
  the `if roots:` line), but round 2's snippet made it **twice** in two adjacent lines. It is
  bound once above and both readers take the binding (I6).
* `shelved_ids` was described as "the item ids `hold_shelved` put aside", **already computed**.
  That was wrong twice over (C2, P21), and it is the correction that matters most here because
  both plausible shortcuts break a rule this spec declares elsewhere:
  * `_candidates`' `aside` is not item ids at all — it is `id(r)` **row identities** (`:2645`,
    read at `:2697`). Worse, `hold_shelved` adds to it every row whose *target* is shelved, not
    only a shelved item's own rows (`own = own or why[target]` at `:2270`, then `aside.add(id(r))`
    at `:2272-2273`), and `_candidates` then unions the `console_aside` rows in (`:2647`). Item
    ids derived from `aside` would class every item merely **waiting on** a shelved blocker as
    `later` itself — and `later` never escalates (D6), so the escalation would go silent for
    exactly the chains this card is about.
  * `shelved(items, parks)`' whole key set is wrong the other way: it keys an item-scope operator
    park as `'operator park'` (`:2235-2236`). Passing it as `shelved_ids` would make `park_class`
    answer `later` for **every item a person parked by hand**, because `later` is tested first by
    design (§2.1) — which contradicts S-91755's own acceptance line ("the hold class of an item is
    operator for an item-scope park made by hand") and would make §2.3's view say *later* where an
    operator park is the truth.

**How the mark is written: the whole correction, re-read first** (C1, D20, P20). Round 2 wrote the
mark as `correction={'blocker_alarmed': key}` through `pool_mod.update_session`. That silently
**erases the park**: `update_session` appends a line (`asf/workers/pool.py:229-232`) and
`lifecycle.fold` lands it with a one-level `dict.update` — `_run_of(runs[job],
rec.get('branch')).update(rec)` (`asf/workers/lifecycle.py:338`) — so a line carrying a *partial*

and `at`. `correction_rows:1037` would stop seeing `c.get('parked')`, the item would be unparked
by its own alarm, and S-91758's own line "no park is written, lifted or changed by the escalation"
would be violated by the escalation's one log line. `relaunch.park_fields` is safe only because it
emits the whole dict in one line (`asf/workers/relaunch.py:337-339`).

So the mark re-emits the correction complete:

```python
def mark_blocker_alarm(product, path, row):
    """One key added to the park's OWN correction, re-emitted whole: `lifecycle.fold` merges a
    line's fields shallowly, so a correction written in part is a correction replaced (D20)."""
    run = newest_parked_run(lifecycle.item_runs(path, row.item_id), row.park_job)
    corr = (run or {}).get('correction')
    if not isinstance(corr, dict) or not corr.get('parked'):
        return                                   # the park went while the wave planned: no mark
    pool_mod.update_session(product, row.park_job,
                            correction=dict(corr, blocker_alarmed=row.park_key))
```

`lifecycle.item_runs` returns each run with its folded correction, the caller's own copy
(`:912-914`), so the read is exact and local. The park's `at` is carried through unchanged, which
`alarmed` needs — it compares `corr.get('at') > since`, the item's latest unpark
(`relaunch.py:361-362`), and a park laid after that unpark passes. The guard on `corr['parked']`
means a park lifted between the plan and the write leaves the ledger alone rather than re-writing
a dead park. §3 pins that `kind`, `reason`, `at` and `parked` all survive the mark.

**Reading it back needs one argument on `alarmed`** (C3, D16). `relaunch.alarmed(path, job, item,
key)` (`:353-363`) matches `corr.get('loop_key') == key` on a **hardcoded** field name, so there is
no way to read a `blocker_alarmed` mark through it as the earlier round claimed. It gains one
default-valued keyword and nothing else:

```python
def alarmed(path, job, item, key, field='loop_key'):
    ...
    and corr.get(field) == key
```

No caller changes (the default is today's field), and the blocker alarm reads
`alarmed(path, job, item, key, field='blocker_alarmed')`. The two alarms therefore dedupe on
*separate* fields, so one park can raise both its `ALARM loop` and its `ALARM blocker` line once
each — which is the behaviour wanted, and what keying the blocker alarm on `loop_key` would have
broken. This is the one edit under `asf/workers/`: `asf/workers/relaunch.py` is in the footprint,

No park is written, lifted or changed by any of this. The one ledger write is the dedupe mark, and

`Top blockers` status cell (D9, D17; P14). Acceptance is the third block of `## Acceptance tests`.
The table is unconditional and the status cell is not, because the table is what an operator opens
to ask the question and the status row is read every tick whether or not anything is wrong.

Two things this Story must not break. Plain `asf next --json` is a bare JSON array with no `rows`
key (`render.py:96-97`), pinned by `tests/test_feeder.py:3136-3143` and promised to scripts at
`docs/guide/operating.md:161`, so the blockers ride on `--blockers --json` and the old flag is left
byte-for-byte alone (C4, D17). And the new cell says `blocks n rows` where its neighbour `S1 gate`
says `holds n rows`, because the two count different things — rows waiting on an item, against rows
the gate holds behind a seat (I5).

- [ ] the top blockers table has one row per item, naming the item, its total count, its direct count, what holds the item, and the item's own row action
- [ ] the table's header names how many held rows it counted and how many distinct items they wait on
- [ ] the table prints a dash in a cell it has no value for
- [ ] the table prints one row saying nothing is blocked when no held row names an item
- [ ] the table shows at most the number of items it was asked for, heaviest first
- [ ] the NEXT header counts the held rows and the distinct items they wait on, beside the row count and the launch count it already prints
- [ ] asf next with the blockers option prints the table and not the NEXT rows table
- [ ] asf next with the blockers option and the JSON option together prints the blockers as JSON
- [ ] asf next with the JSON option alone prints the rows array exactly as it does today, with no blockers key and no change of shape
- [ ] the status top blockers cell names the heaviest items with both counts
- [ ] the status top blockers cell names the command that prints the full table
- [ ] the status top blockers cell is absent from the table when no item reaches the minimum
- [ ] the status top blockers cell reads the plan the view already made and makes no second plan
- [ ] an unreadable plan leaves the rest of the status table intact
- [ ] the table header says the rows it counted are the rows of this plan, so its count is not read as the escalation reason's count
- [ ] the rows array printed by the JSON option carries exactly the field names it carries today, in the same order, though the row grew three fields for the factory's own use
- [ ] the three fields the escalation puts on a row for the tick are absent from the rows array
- [ ] both blocker views read the rows after the invariant gate has run, so neither names a ruling the gate refused

### S-91758: A factory park that holds rows becomes a ruling, not a dead row

Design §2.4 — the escalation as a post-pass over the ordered rows, its convention, and the deduped
alarm line (D5, D6, D7, D8, D12, D13, D14, D15, D16; P9, P10, P11, P13, P16, P17, P18). Acceptance
is the fourth block of `## Acceptance tests`. This is the card's "no escalation": the escalation is
a *decision*, never a launch the cap already refused, and never an override of a decision a person
already took.

Three bounds, each reading a fact already on the correction the feeder is handed, and together
giving at most one adjudicate row per item per card state: a hold the record shows **settled**
(D12, the same B-0128 test `correction_rows` already makes one screen above), a park written on
the item's **own adjudicate job** (D13, which is what stops the escalation re-escalating the
relaunch cap its own session earns), and a park that is a **question** a session's report asked an
operator (D14, which `rejudge` releases when the answer lands). An earlier round rested the bound
on `_capped` instead, which can never see a `STALEMATE` row — so this Story's acceptance lines pin
all three bounds by name.

Four more things this Story must get right, each one a defect review 1 found in the mechanisms
above and each one verified against the checkout (fence F6):

* **The dedupe mark must not erase the park it marks.** `lifecycle.fold` merges a line's fields
  with a one-level `dict.update`, so writing `correction={'blocker_alarmed': …}` replaces the
  correction wholesale and unparks the item — the escalation defeating itself through its own log
  line. The mark re-emits the park's correction whole (C1, D20, P20).
* **It must be written under the park's job**, not the adjudicate job the escalation launches:
  that is the run the correction lives on, and the only job `alarmed`'s scan will find it under
  (C3, D22).
* **The alarm has to live where an escalated row actually passes** — `screen`'s row loop, under
  `act` so a preview writes nothing — and not beside the `ALARM loop` line, which sits behind an
  early return that every escalated row takes (C3, D22, P22).
* **Two park kinds have to leave `FACTORY_PARKS`.** For `naming` and `copies` the lane keeps the
  branch in its landing wait on purpose, so I4 would refuse the escalated row every tick forever;
  `correction_rows`' own stalemate already excludes them by name (C4, D23, P23).

- [ ] a parked correction from a factory park whose item holds at least the minimum rows and has stood at least the escalation minutes is emitted as an adjudicate row
- [ ] that row's reason names the minutes the park has stood, the kind of park, the total count, the direct count and the first few waiting items
- [ ] a park made by hand at item scope stays a parked row whatever the weight
- [ ] a park whose item sits under a card the product gave a later priority stays a parked row whatever the weight
- [ ] a factory park whose item holds fewer than the minimum rows stays a parked row with its own text unchanged
- [ ] a factory park that has stood for less than the escalation minutes stays a parked row with its own text unchanged
- [ ] a factory park the record cannot date is never escalated
- [ ] an escalation minutes of zero leaves every parked row exactly as it is today
- [ ] a hold an adjudicate session has already finished over is parked again, and is not escalated a second time
- [ ] a park written on the item's own adjudicate job is never escalated, so the escalation cannot re-escalate the cap its own session earned
- [ ] a park for a question a session's report declared is never escalated
- [ ] a relaunch cap park whose reason says it needs input is never escalated
- [ ] a parked row that came from a branch-scope or job-scope park is never rewritten by the escalation
- [ ] the stale park surfacing rule leaves alone an item the escalation already took, so no item gets two rows
- [ ] no park is written, lifted or changed by the escalation
- [ ] no cap is raised and no extra launch is granted by the escalation
- [ ] a park for a refusal the lane handles by its own mechanics, a reword or a drop of trunk copies, is never escalated
- [ ] an item merely waiting on a shelved blocker is still escalated on its own merits, and is not treated as shelved itself
- [ ] an item an operator parked by hand is classed as an operator park and never as a later shelf
- [ ] the escalated row carries the job its park was written on, the number of rows waiting on it, and that park's own loop key
- [ ] a row the invariant gate turned back into a waiting row raises no alarm line and is named in the view by the row the gate gave it
- [ ] the tick prints one alarm line naming the item, how many rows wait on it, how long it has been parked and the command that lists the blockers
- [ ] a later tick on the same park and the same loop prints no second alarm line
- [ ] the mark that suppresses the second alarm is written on the parked correction and nowhere else
- [ ] the mark leaves the park intact: its parked flag, its kind, its reason and its recorded time all read afterwards exactly as they read before
- [ ] the mark is written under the job the park was written on, and the scan that reads it back reads it under that same job
- [ ] a pass that only previews the wave prints no alarm line and writes no mark
- [ ] a park lifted between the plan and the write leaves the ledger untouched
- [ ] the ledger scan that reads the mark back reads the field it is given, and reads the loop key field when it is given none
- [ ] one park raises its loop alarm and its blocker alarm once each, because the two dedupe on different fields
