# F-0164 — a 427 s health step, and one branch that can never publish

The card's measurement: on 2026-09-26 a tick printed `[step:health] 426.6 s` where earlier ticks of
the same day printed 26–190 s. Health's publish path pushes a session's branch with the product's
own `pre-push` hook — correctly, the push carries real code — and each of those pushes is killed at
`git.push_timeout_s` (default 120 s). One publish, on a hand-made worktree branch under no lane
prefix (`worktree-<label>-…`), timed out **twice** in that one step: `publish … refused: push timed
out … left as it was`.

Three things were asked for. **The first is already landed**: `5ec15c34e` (*perf(health): the
factory's publishes of independent runs go at once*, 2026-09-28, two days after the tick the card
measured) made the per-run pass a generator, grouped the runs that must stay ordered, and put the
publishes of independent groups through a pool of `PUBLISH_WORKERS = 3`. That commit's message also
carries the *only* split of the health step this factory has ever had — 376 s of five pushes inside a
sampled 490 s step, 16–182 s apiece — and it was sampled **by hand, off one live ledger, once**.

So what is left is the card's own first sentence and its last two remedies:

- **the split is still not measured.** The factory knows what the whole step cost (`[step:health]
  426.6 s`) and nothing about what its publishes cost. The next time this step goes long, the same
  hand-sampling has to happen again — and a pool of 3 is exactly the kind of change whose effect
  cannot be read from a number that does not exist.
- **a publish that times out is retried forever, and routed nowhere.** `republish_steps` publishes
  every ended-unpushed run's worktree again on **every** pass. Nothing counts the timeouts, and
  `push_failure` classifies the timeout text as neither `hook refused` nor `network error` (measured
  in P5) — so the branch is pushed for 120 s, refused, and pushed again next tick, forever, with no
  hold and no next action. The card's words: *that branch can never publish while its hook runs
  >120 s*.
- **health publishes whatever branch a ledger row names.** The one branch that burned 240 s of that
  step was not the factory's at all. `lifecycle.publish` refuses exactly one branch — the trunk.

### In

- **`run_steps(..., clock=None)` returns `[(job, seconds)]`** — one row per publish call it made, in
  the order the calls were made, timed around the call itself. The seconds of a pooled call are
  computed inside the worker and returned with its result, so nothing is appended from a thread
  (P3).
- **`health.publish_timing_line(timings, wall, workers)`** — one line: how many pushes, how many
  seconds of pushing, the wall clock they took together, the cap they ran under, and the slowest
  one by name. Carried as a single `('health', 'publishes', line)` entry in `found`, appended after
  the per-run entries, printed raw by the print loop's existing `published` branch (P12) — and only
  when at least one publish was made, so a pass that pushes nothing prints exactly what it prints
  today.
- **`gitpush.timeout_line(limit, refs)` and `gitpush.timed_out_after(text)`** — one owner for the
  words of a killed push and one reader of them; `push` formats its line through the first.
- **A push killed on its timeout refuses with the timeout, not with `git_error`'s pick** — in
  `lifecycle.publish`, `p.returncode == gitpush.TIMED_OUT` builds the refusal from
  `gitpush.timeout_line` before the redaction parse and before `git_error` (P6). This is what makes
  the ledger's `publish_refused` a reliable record of *why*.
- **`lifecycle.PUBLISH_TIMEOUT`, `PUBLISH_TIMEOUT_CAP = 2` and `publish_timeout_park(run, limit, n,
  now)`** — `(fields, line)`: a parked correction of its own kind with `operator_flagged: 1`, whose
  `reason` names the branch, the limit, how many passes timed out, and the two things a person can
  do (raise this product's `conventions.git.push_timeout_s`, or push the branch by hand) before
  `asf unpark <item>`.
- **`publish_timeouts` on the run row, in `RUN_FIELDS`** (P11), and **`health.branch_timeouts(registry,
  branch)`** — the count read across every run of that branch, because the branch, not the run, is
  what cannot publish.
- **`republish_steps` counts, caps, and stops** — a branch already at the cap yields no publish at
  all (the 120 s stops being spent); a timeout below the cap is counted and printed as today; the
  one that reaches the cap writes the park.
- **`conventions.launch_prefixes()`** — every prefix this factory could mint for this product: each
  kind of `DEFAULT_BRANCH_PREFIXES` (`legacy` excluded) resolved through `prefix(kind)`, plus every
  kind the product names, plus its legacy prefixes. Longest first, deduplicated — the same shape
  `all_prefixes()` returns, and `all_prefixes()` is left exactly as it is (P8, P9).
- **`lifecycle.publish(..., prefixes=())`** — a branch under none of the given prefixes is refused
  in the existing first guard, with the existing words (`is not a lane branch`), before any push and
  before any rebase. Empty `prefixes` means no such check, so `spawn`'s two callers are unchanged.
  `publish_gap` passes `product.conventions.launch_prefixes()`.
- **A not-a-lane-branch refusal does not rewrite the session's correction** — it is recorded on
  `publish_refused` (so it prints once, not every tick) and the run's pending `UNPUSHED` correction
  is left as written, because `refusal_text`'s tail — *"the factory publishes, never a push of your
  own"* — is false for a branch the factory has just declined to publish.

### Out

- **The concurrency itself.** It is landed (`5ec15c34e`); `PUBLISH_WORKERS` stays `3`, `_groups`
  stays as it is, and nothing here touches the pool.
- **Moving publishing into its own step after the wave** — the card's alternative (1). The pool
  already took the sampled 376 s of serial pushing to about a third of that, and the measurement
  this card adds is precisely what a decision to reorder the steps would have to rest on. Deciding
  it now, from a number sampled once by hand, would be deciding it blind.
- **No new metrics stream, no new tick-line key, no new event.** The split is printed where
  `[step:health] <n>s` is already read, in the tick log.
- **No new `end_reason`, no new member of `OUTCOME_CLASSES`, and no change to `push_retry`.** A
  timeout stays outside the failure-class vocabulary; it is routed on the republish path, which
  F-0235's Tasks do not touch (P14). The scorecard is not edited at all.
- **`all_prefixes()` is not widened** (P8, P9): the pruner's list stays the branches the product
  *declares*, because deleting a branch is irreversible and refusing to push one is not.
- **`prune_branches` does not learn to see a stray under no lane prefix.** This card refuses to
  *push* such a branch; it does not adopt, list or delete it.
- **`spawn`'s two `publish` calls get no `prefixes`** — they publish branches spawn itself minted.
- **The archive push inside `publish`** (`refs_only=True`, its own *"the old tip could not be
  archived"* refusal) keeps its wording, and its timeout is not counted.
- **The live judgement's own publish is not counted.** The first timeout on a branch is the one that
  ends the run; every pass after it is a republish, and that is where the count runs (D5).
- **`git.push_timeout_s` is not raised, and nothing raises it automatically.** The default stays
  120 s and the decision stays the operator's — the park's reason is where they are told.
- **`branch_kind`'s blindness for a product that names only some kinds is not fixed here.** The same
  gap makes `feeder.rows.landed_doc` unable to recognise such a product's spec and plan branches
  (`asf/feeder/rows.py:700-702`); that is a separate card, and this one adds a second, correct list
  rather than moving the first.

## The design

### 1. `run_steps` returns what each publish cost

`run_steps` keeps each queue's job beside its generator, times every publish call, and returns the
rows. The pooled branch computes the seconds inside the worker and returns them with the result, so
no shared list is written from a thread (P3):

```python
def run_steps(entries, publish, workers=None, clock=None):
    """… Returns ``[(job, seconds)]``, one row per publish made, in the order they were made."""
    clock = clock or time.monotonic

    def timed(job, ask):
        started = clock()
        try:
            return publish(*ask), (job, clock() - started)
        finally:
            pass
    ...
    timings = []
    ...
        if len(asks) == 1 or workers == 1:
            done = [timed(job, ask) for job, _qi, ask in asks]
        else:
            pool = pool or ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=workers)
            done = list(pool.map(lambda a: timed(a[0], a[2]), asks))
        for (_job, qi, _ask), (result, timing) in zip(asks, done):
            sends[qi] = result
            timings.append(timing)
    ...
    return timings
```

`asks` grows a job (it is `(job, qi, ask)`), taken from the entry the generator came from. A publish
that raises still raises, as it does today.

### 3. A killed push says so

`gitpush` owns the words and the reader:

```python
#: The refs line a push killed on its timeout carries; :func:`timed_out_after` reads it back.
def timeout_line(limit, refs):
    return f'push timed out after {limit}s: {refs or "?"} — left as it was'

TIMED_OUT_RE = re.compile(r'push timed out after (\d+)s')

def timed_out_after(text):
    """The limit a killed push was given, read off its line — or None when this is not one."""
    m = TIMED_OUT_RE.search(text or '')
    return int(m.group(1)) if m else None
```

`push` formats its line with `timeout_line`, unchanged otherwise. `lifecycle.publish` names the
timeout before anything else can bury it (P6):

```python

    if p.returncode != 0:

```

### 4. The count, the cap, the park

`lifecycle`:

```python
#: A correction kind of its own: the factory's publish of this branch was killed on its timeout,
#: twice running. No session can answer it — the repo's pre-push hook takes longer than the product
#: allows one push to take — so it is parked for a person (D7).
PUBLISH_TIMEOUT = 'publish timed out'
#: Publishes of one branch killed on the timeout before it is parked instead of pushed again.
PUBLISH_TIMEOUT_CAP = 2


def publish_timeout_park(run, limit, n, now):
    """``(fields, line)``: park ``run``'s item because the factory's publish of its branch was
    killed on its timeout ``n`` passes running. No round is spent and no session is sent back: the
    hook is slower than one push is allowed to be, and only a person can change that."""
```

whose `reason` reads:

> `{n} publishes of {branch} timed out at {limit}s: this repo's pre-push hook takes longer than
> git.push_timeout_s allows one push — raise conventions.git.push_timeout_s for this product, or
> push {branch} by hand, then asf unpark {item}`

and whose `line` is `parked {branch}: {reason}`. The `fields` are
`{'correction': {'kind': PUBLISH_TIMEOUT, 'text': …, 'at': now, 'parked': True, 'reason': …},
'operator_flagged': 1}`, and `publish_timeouts` joins `RUN_FIELDS`.

`health`:

```python
def branch_timeouts(registry, branch):
    """How many publishes of ``branch`` this factory has had killed on the timeout: the sum of
    ``publish_timeouts`` over every run of that branch (D6)."""
```

`republish_steps`, at its two ends:

```python
    if branch_timeouts(registry, branch) >= lifecycle.PUBLISH_TIMEOUT_CAP:
        return                      # parked: the push is not made again (the 120 s stops)
    ...
    reason, ev, line = yield (product, run, ev, reason, alive)
    if not line:
        return
    if ' refused: ' not in line:
        ...                         # unchanged, plus 'publish_timeouts': None
    limit = gitpush.timed_out_after(line)
    if limit:
        n = branch_timeouts(registry, branch) + 1
        fields = {'publish_timeouts': (run.get('publish_timeouts') or 0) + 1,
                  'publish_refused': line}
        if n >= lifecycle.PUBLISH_TIMEOUT_CAP:
            park, pline = lifecycle.publish_timeout_park(run, limit, n, pool_mod.now_iso())
            fields.update(park)
            pool_mod.update_session(product, job, **fields)
            run.update(fields)
            found.append((job, 'parked', pline.split(': ', 1)[1]))
            return
        pool_mod.update_session(product, job, **fields)
        run.update(fields)
        found.append((job, 'published', line))
        return
    if line == run.get('publish_refused'):
        return
    ...                             # unchanged, except: a not-a-lane-branch refusal (§5)
                                    # records publish_refused and leaves the correction alone
```

A publish that goes through clears the counter (`'publish_timeouts': None` beside the
`'publish_refused': None` already written there), so two timeouts must be consecutive to park.

### 5. Only a branch this factory could have minted

`conventions`:

```python
    def launch_prefixes(self):
        """Every prefix a branch this factory mints can carry — each kind of
        :data:`DEFAULT_BRANCH_PREFIXES` resolved through :meth:`prefix`, the kinds this product
        names, and its legacy prefixes. Wider than :meth:`all_prefixes`, which answers what the
        product *declares* its lanes to be and is what the branch pruner deletes by: a product
        naming only ``code:`` and ``fix:`` still mints ``spec/<id>`` from the default, and
        publishing its spec branch must not depend on it having said so."""
```

`lifecycle.publish` takes the list and refuses in the guard that is already there:

```python
def publish(wt, branch, remote_sha='', main='main', protected=None, push_timeout_s=None,
            prefixes=()):
    if not branch or branch == main or (prefixes and not any(branch.startswith(p) for p in prefixes)):
        return False, f'publish refused: {branch or "no branch"} is not a lane branch'
```

and `publish_gap` passes it:

```python
    ok, line = lifecycle.publish(wt, branch, ev.remote_sha, main=product.main,
                                 protected=refguard.listed(product.conventions),
                                 push_timeout_s=gitpush.push_timeout(product.conventions),
                                 prefixes=product.conventions.launch_prefixes())
```

No push is attempted, so the refusal is free: the hand-made branch that cost 240 s of the card's
step costs nothing, its line is recorded on `publish_refused` and printed once, and the run's own
correction is left saying what it said.

### What it looks like when it is there

A health pass that publishes ends with one line naming the split:

```
published spec/F-0164 at 4f2a91b
published worker/T-0719 at c81de55
publishes: 2 push(es), 168 s of pushing in 96 s (3 at once); slowest worker/T-0719 121 s
```

A branch whose hook outlives the limit is pushed twice and then never again:

```
published  republish-t-0719  publish worker/T-0719 refused: push timed out after 120s: HEAD:refs/heads/worker/T-0719 — left as it was
parked     republish-t-0719  2 publishes of worker/T-0719 timed out at 120s: this repo's pre-push hook takes longer than git.push_timeout_s allows one push — raise conventions.git.push_timeout_s for this product, or push worker/T-0719 by hand, then asf unpark T-0719
```

and the feeder's next tick shows one row: `FIX_CORRECT T-0719 — PARKED 2 publishes … waits_on:
operator`. A branch under no lane prefix is refused once, before any push:

```
published  stray-run  publish refused: worktree-<label>-hotfix-v3 is not a lane branch
```

## Records

| What | Where | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `publish_timeouts: <n>` — publishes of this run's branch killed on the timeout, cleared by one that goes through | the run's line in `sessions.jsonl`; a `RUN_FIELDS` member, so it never folds into the next launch | `republish_steps`, on the main thread |
| `publish_refused: <line>` — now guaranteed to carry the timeout's own words when that is why | same | unchanged site, with §3's refusal |
| `correction: {kind: 'publish timed out', parked: true, reason: …, text: …, at: …}` and `operator_flagged: 1` | same | `lifecycle.publish_timeout_park`, via `republish_steps` |
| `publishes: <n> push(es), <t> s of pushing in <w> s (<k> at once); slowest <job> <s> s` | the tick log, beside `[step:health] <n>s` | `health.publish_timing_line`, as a `('health', 'publishes', …)` entry in `found` |
| `parked <branch>: <reason>` | the tick log; and the feeder's `PARKED … waits_on: operator` row derived from the correction | `republish_steps` / `correction_rows` |
| `publish refused: <branch> is not a lane branch` | the tick log, once per branch (suppressed after by `publish_refused`) | `lifecycle.publish`'s first guard |

Nothing else is written: no metrics stream row, no tick-line key, no event, no `end_reason`, no
scorecard key.

## Acceptance tests

Every block is runnable from the repo root, one module per process, the way the suite runs them
(`tools/run_tests.py`).

**S-39700 — the split is in the log, per publish push.**

```bash
python3 -m unittest tests.test_health_publish_steps.PublishTimingTests -v
python3 -m unittest tests.test_health_publish_steps -v
```

`PublishTimingTests` — with a fake `clock` returning a scripted sequence, `run_steps` returns one
`(job, seconds)` row per publish it made: two independent runs published together give two rows with
each job's own seconds; two runs on one branch give two rows in ledger order; a generator with two
publishes gives two rows for that job; a generator with none gives none, and a pass with no publish
at all returns `[]`. `publish_timing_line([('a', 182.0), ('b', 16.0)], wall=190.0)` names the count
(`2`), the pushing total (`198 s`), the wall (`190 s`), the cap (`3 at once`) and the slowest job by
name (`a 182 s`), and carries no token matching `\b\d+(\.\d+)?s\b` (P13). The rest of the module —
the four existing `RunStepsTest` cases, ordering and the raising publish — stays green, which is
what proves the timing changed no behaviour.

**S-39701 — a publish killed on the timeout is named, counted twice, and then parked instead of made again.**

```bash
python3 -m unittest tests.test_gitpush.TimeoutLineTests -v
python3 -m unittest tests.test_lifecycle.PublishTimeoutRefusalTests -v
python3 -m unittest tests.test_health_publish_steps.PublishTimeoutCapTests -v
```

`TimeoutLineTests` — `timed_out_after(timeout_line(120, 'HEAD:refs/heads/x'))` is `120`;
`timed_out_after('error: failed to push some refs')` is `None`; a real push killed by a hook that
sleeps past a 1 s limit returns `TIMED_OUT` and a stderr whose last line is `timeout_line`'s own
(the existing timeout test in this module, re-read through the formatter).

`PublishTimeoutRefusalTests` — with `gitpush.push` stubbed to return
`CompletedProcess(rc=gitpush.TIMED_OUT, stderr='error: ruff failed on asf/x.py:12\n' +
timeout_line(120, ref))`, `publish` refuses with a line `timed_out_after` reads as `120` — the case
P6 measured as losing the timeout to `git_error`; with rc 1 and the same stderr the refusal is
`git_error`'s pick, exactly as today.

`PublishTimeoutCapTests` (a `Home`-based class, the harness `test_ended_run_liveness` uses) — an
ended run whose `end_reason` is `failed: not pushed …`, with `publish_gap` stubbed to return a
timeout refusal: the first pass writes `publish_timeouts: 1` and a `published` finding; the second
writes the park — `correction.kind == 'publish timed out'`, `parked` true, `operator_flagged == 1`,
the `reason` naming the branch, `120s`, `conventions.git.push_timeout_s` and `asf unpark <item>`, and
a `parked` finding; the third pass makes **no publish call at all** (the stub is never entered) and
adds no finding. A second ledger row on the same branch counts into the same cap (`branch_timeouts`
sums both runs, D6), and a publish that succeeds after one timeout clears `publish_timeouts` so the
next timeout starts again at 1.

**S-39702 — health pushes only a branch this factory could have minted, and knows which those are.**

```bash
python3 -m unittest tests.test_conventions.LaunchPrefixesTests -v
python3 -m unittest tests.test_lifecycle.PublishLanePrefixTests -v
python3 -m unittest tests.test_health_publish_steps.StrayBranchTests -v
```

`LaunchPrefixesTests` — for a product naming nothing, `launch_prefixes()` holds `worker/`, `fix/`,
`spec/`, `plan/` and `cloud/direct-`; for one whose yaml says `{code: feature/, fix: bugfix/}` it
holds `feature/`, `bugfix/`, `spec/`, `plan/`, `cloud/direct-` — the P8 case, where `all_prefixes()`
holds three and `branch_kind('spec/f-0164')` is `None`; a product with `legacy: [old/]` keeps `old/`;
the list is longest-first and deduplicated; and `all_prefixes()` returns exactly what it returns
today for all three (the pruner is untouched, D8).

`PublishLanePrefixTests` — over the same real-git fixture the existing publish tests use:
`publish(wt, 'worktree-x-hotfix-v3', prefixes=<launch_prefixes>)` is refused with `is not a lane
branch` and `ls-remote` shows no such ref (nothing was pushed, and no rebase ran); the same call with
`prefixes=()` publishes, so `spawn`'s callers are unaffected; a `spec/…` branch of a product naming
only `code:`/`fix:` **is** published (the regression D8 exists to prevent); `publish(wt, 'main', …)`
is refused as it is today.

`StrayBranchTests` — an ended unpushed run on a branch under no lane prefix: the pass records the
refusal on `publish_refused`, leaves the run's pending `UNPUSHED` correction byte-for-byte as it was
(no `refusal_text` rewrite), prints the line once, and prints nothing on the next pass; a run on a
lane branch in the same pass is published normally.
